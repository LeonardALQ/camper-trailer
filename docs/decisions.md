# Decision record: the LandCruiser 79 plan

The decisions taken on 20 Nov 2026, when Leonard replaced the Porsche Cayenne with a LandCruiser 79 that carries both bikes in an enclosed box, and the trailer was redesigned without its garage. Each entry gives the time (UTC), the decision and the reason. The documents cite these entries as "decision log" with the time, for example "decision log 12:10". Later entries override earlier ones where they say so.

Leonard's ask:

> Instead of a porsche cayenne, I'll get a land cruiser single cab 79 V8. I'll get an old one, so I'll rebuild the engine with stronger top end components, upgrade the turbo charger, put a nice high air flow exhaust, and tune the ecu to get ~300-400bhp on it.
>
> Then, I'll put an enclosed box at the back to fit the two bikes and some tools. That way, we can make the trailer smaller and lighter.

> 1. Let's plan the vehicle work. I'll get an old 79 with like 400,000 km since I'll rebuild the engine, I'll need to upgrade the transmission to handle the extra torque, upgrade the turbo charger, etc. find me the engine parts I need, the transmission parts I need, a good turbo charger and high flow exhaust, the GVM upgrade kit, and whatever other work we need to do on an old vehicle to make sure it's highly reliable please. Try to keep the cost under 30k AUD, but let me know if we need to increase the budget it's not a huge deal.
> 2. Let's design the motorbike garage on the land cruiser. We want to make sure it's enclosed to make sure the bikes are safer and not prone to theft.
> 3. Then, let's redesign our whole plan to to this new vehicle.

How the ask is read:

- The vehicle is a used VDJ79 single cab chassis with the 1VD-FTV V8 and about 400,000 km. The engine is rebuilt and made more powerful, the driveline is made to take the torque, a GVM upgrade carries the load, and everything else an old vehicle needs is refreshed for reliability.
- The AUD 30,000 budget covers the vehicle work: parts, outsourced machining and specialist work, and certification. It excludes buying the vehicle, Leonard's own labour, the bike box and the trailer.
- Registration in NSW, with Victoria as the fallback.
- The bike box is an enclosed, lockable box on the ute's tray for both bikes and some tools.
- The trailer loses its garage and is re-laid out, re-sized and re-checked for the 79, and the whole plan follows.

## 11:15

- **Engine power, level A** (report 14): up to 181 kW and about 650 Nm at the crank. The hardware is sized for level B (GTurbo VD-G333, PDI front-mount intercooler, Manta 3 inch exhaust keeping the DPF), so level B (220 to 250 kW) is a retune and a certificate later. Why: Leonard's 300 to 400 bhp is 224 to 298 kW. Level B is certifiable but not assured (about AUD 2,000, or AUD 12,000 or more if a laboratory emissions test is needed), and level C is not realistic to certify. A legal, reliable towing engine comes first, and level B stays open. The legal path recorded here was corrected at 11:50.
- **Gearbox and clutch** (report 15): a new genuine Aisin H152F (AUD 3,840) and the NPC Super HD Organic 1300 clutch with its billet flywheel. The tune limits torque in low range, first and reverse to about 450 to 500 Nm. Why: the AB60 and ZF 8HP conversions (AUD 20,000 to 34,000) are not worth it at level A.
- **Bike box** (report 17): welded aluminium, inside about 2,500 x 1,810 x 1,460 mm, rear barn doors and a kerb-side door, the 3,048 mm ramp and the WARN winch moved from the trailer. Open at this point: the rear axle load and the rollover margin, which need the GVM kit's axle rating, the lowest deck that works and a lighter box.

## 11:25

- **The vehicle to buy** (report 16): a VDJ79 single cab built 01/2017 to 08/2022, GXL first for its factory front and rear diff locks, GX second. Why: Lovells' 4,200 kg kit fits single cabs only from 01/2017, and the 3,900 kg kits' 2,360 kg rear axle is too small. This overrides report 14's preference for a pre-DPF truck, so the engine plan uses report 14's DPF variant (piezo injectors, DPF-back exhaust), about AUD 3,600 more.
- **GVM and GCM**: Lovells 4,200 kg Platinum kit TOYKIT080-GVM (front axle 1,650 kg, rear 2,550 kg), fitted by Lovells' authorised fitter, VSCCS certified, LSPV reset. Keep Toyota's 6,900 kg GCM. Why: the rear axle binds first, so the weighed ute stays at or under 3,850 kg with no more than 250 kg on the ball.
- **Brakes**: factory discs all round, slotted factory-size rotors, Bendix Ultimate 4WD pads, braided lines, DOT 4 fluid. Why: a big brake kit needs 17 inch wheels and would end the shared wheel.
- **Wheels**: ROH Trak 2 16 x 7 ET 0 5 x 150 steel wheels with LT265/75R16 123/120 tyres on the ute and the trailer. The trailer moves to Cruisemaster LC5 M14 hubs at 900 mm axle spacing. One spare under the ute's tray and one on the trailer, each fitting both. The narrow rear track stays, with no wide-track housing. Why: one wheel and tyre for both vehicles, and a rear-only offset would end the shared wheel.
- **Towing**: Hayman Reese 03186RW towbar (or a factory bar rated 3,500 and 350 kg if fitted), REDARC Tow-Pro Elite V3, 12-pin flat plug, Anderson SB50 charge feed on 6 B&S with a 50 A fuse. The ute is 12 V. The coupling height was set at 12:10.
- **Bike box limits** (report 16): at most 1,850 mm wide, rear overhang at most 1,908 mm behind the rear axle, mounted on Toyota's deck-mount stations, unladen centre of gravity under 1,000 mm, lighter than report 17's 440 kg, on the lowest deck that works. Why: 1,850 mm is the cautious reading of Toyota's rule that a mounted part may stand 60 mm past the 1,790 mm cab. Read per side, the same sentence allows 1,910 mm, which is how report 17 read it. 1,850 mm still leaves about 1,760 mm inside against the 1,680 mm the bikes need.
- **Budget**: keep the full reliable plan, about AUD 69,000 for the vehicle work plus about AUD 13,900 for the bike box, more than twice the AUD 30,000 target, and list the cuts that keep it reliable (tested and reused injectors, pump, radiator and oil pump, a rebuilt gearbox, a factory towbar if fitted). Why: Leonard asked for high reliability and said the budget can rise. The final figures are at 13:05.
- **Trailer targets for the redesign**: ATM about 3,000 kg at most, towball about 200 to 250 kg with a 6 % minimum share of the trailer's mass, GCM 6,900 kg with the ute at 3,850 kg. The plate was set at 12:10.

## 11:50

- **The engine's legal path, corrected**: TfNSW's VSI 06 Rev 4, issued 1 July 2026, adds item 6: flashing the original engine management system in a way that alters emissions output needs a VSCCS certificate. Report 14 read the 2013 revision, so its finding that level A needs no certificate is wrong. Every custom tune now needs an LA4 certificate (about AUD 2,000) with emissions evidence, for which Rev 4 accepts the Interim NSW Vehicle Emissions Test Procedure (May 2023) in place of laboratory testing. Intercoolers and exhausts that keep the emission controls still need no certificate (item 5).
- **Level A still beats level B**, now that both need a certificate. Why: level A keeps the engine's life close to stock and the driveline well inside its margin (the H152F has no published torque rating, and level B needs about 850 Nm), and its emissions evidence is the easier case. Level B stays open: the hardware is ready for it, and if the LC4 certifier accepts the driveline and the Interim NSW test at 220 to 250 kW, both can go on one certificate.

## 12:10

- **Trailer layout** (report 18): layout 1, the queen bed across the front at 750 mm with the kitchen slide-out, the air-conditioner and storage under it, a fixed roof 1,950 mm inside, and a 4,420 mm body, 1,080 mm shorter. Study figures: tare about 2,183 kg, fully loaded 2,763 kg (3,022 kg with growth), towball 185 to 246 kg in every case with no ballast. Why: it is the smallest and lightest body that keeps a made-up bed and standing room, which is what Leonard asked the change to buy. The price is that one sleeper climbs over the other. Layout 2, a walk-around bed at the back, is the fallback at 830 mm longer and 137 kg heavier.
- **ATM plate 3,100 kg**. Why: a 3,000 kg plate is 22 kg short of the heaviest case with growth. The GCM still holds: a 3,850 kg ute plus 2,850 kg on the trailer's axles (the 3,100 kg plate less the 250 kg the ute carries on its ball) is 6,700 kg against 6,900 kg. This 6,700 kg is the combination figure every summary quotes.
- **Coupling height**: the ute's ball at 440 mm laden, plus or minus 20 mm, in place of 450 mm. Why: 450 mm plus 20 mm would pass VSB1's 460 mm.
- **Running gear**: the XT Air 4,500 kg build stays, on LC5 M14 hubs at 900 mm spacing, with a 1,555 mm track. Why: no lighter air build passes VSB1's 120 % rule, and the 1,555 mm track runs in the ute's front ruts and tips at 34.5 degrees, the same as the earlier design.

## 12:45

- **The vehicle plan's decisions**: coupling 440 mm plus or minus 20 mm, the trailer at its 3,100 kg plate in every sum, the rebuild kit priced as an estimate for the DPF engine, a DPF inspection and clean as a required line, a sealed airbox as a required line, and a decision gate before the GVM kit. The bike box is built and weighed before the kit is chosen and fitted. One LC5 inspection covers the kit, the box and the winch. Why: dust past the factory airbox seal would wear the fresh engine, level A's emissions evidence needs a working DPF, the Platinum kit's margin is too small to commit before the box is weighed, and VSI 06 item 35 may treat the winch as a powered goods loading device.
- **Petrol out from under the bed**: the generator and the petrol jerry cans go in a sealed, vented drawbar box behind the stone guard, clear of the bike box's sweep, and the axle moves forward to keep the heaviest case at or under 250 kg on the ball. The space under the bed keeps the air-conditioner and storage, with no petrol. Why: petrol vapour sinks, and the air-conditioner's compressor would sit beside it under two sleeping people with only a locker seal between them.
- **One towball position**: report 16's 1,300 mm behind the ute's rear axle, so the bike box overhangs the ball by about 210 mm, not report 17's 160 mm. The redesign takes the overhang from the ute model and re-runs the jack-knife checks with it.

## 20:15

- **Engine power, level B from the start** (report 14 section 3.9). It replaces level A (11:15) and the decision that level A still beats level B (11:50). The tune targets about 230 to 250 kW (310 to 335 hp) and about 850 Nm at the crank, about 170 to 185 kW at the wheels, and never less than 224 kW (300 hp). Why: Leonard set 300 hp as the least that justifies the build. Level A stopped at 181 kW (243 hp) only because a tune within 20 % of stock needed no certificate under the 2013 rules, and VSI 06 Rev 4 needs a certificate for any tune that alters emissions, so that reason is gone. The parts are sized for this level, and report 14 rates it acceptable for towing with the exhaust temperature limit and a new radiator. The price is shorter head gasket and ring life than at 181 kW, and a certificate that report 14 rates possible but not assured.
- **What follows from it**: the tune is report 14's level B price (AUD 2,500), one LA4 certificate covers it, and the retune and second certificate that level B later needed are gone. The laboratory emissions test (AUD 10,000 or more) becomes a contingency line. Reusing the radiator is no longer a cut, because level B needs a new one. The H152F and the NPC 1300 clutch stay, with the 450 to 500 Nm cap in low range, first and reverse, and the ZF 8-speed rated 1,200 Nm is the fallback if the certifier will not accept the H152F at about 850 Nm. The budget becomes AUD 73,650 required, 77,660 with the contingency allowance, and 63,681 with every cut and the allowance.

## 21 Nov, 00:19

Decisions on the home trailer's layout study (TRAILER.md revision J), and the changes the model needed to meet the checks.

- **Water (H13)**: at most 350 L of fresh and grey water ride while towing, with both Honda EU22i kept. The tanks hold 420 L net and are filled at camp with the transfer pump, the CR45 filter and a chlorine dose. Why: the reconciled trailer with growth was 3,529 kg at 400 L, over the 3,500 kg plate. The lighter cabinetry, 6 mm HDPE tanks and five smaller items bring it to 3,438 kg at 400 L in the layout study, and the 350 L travel limit holds the model at 3,397 kg fully loaded with growth, inside H2's 3,400 kg target. Both generators stay because one EU22i takes 24.7 h to put a design hot day back. The fallback without the GCM revision was 315 L, recomputed at 01:27 (H17).
- **Towball (H14)**: the 250 kg cap is replaced by the rule it stood for. In every case the towball keeps the ute's rear axle within its rating with growth, computed from the ute model at that ball load, and stays within the 350 kg of the 79's rating, the Hayman Reese bar and the DO35. The rating was the Platinum's 2,550 kg when decided and is the Elite's 2,850 kg since H17. Why: report 16 set 250 kg as a planning proxy for the rear axle with a 3,000 kg trailer. The 7.5 % floor (MAS-04) stays.
- **Windows (H15)**: four windows, W1, W2, W4 at 500 x 450 mm and W6, and the glass in both doors, all with integrated blackout blinds. Why: W3 and W5 have no wall space, and W3 would face the back of the bike box and its stones.
- **Storage (H16)**: the bed's footprint stays empty, as Leonard asked. The trailer keeps 0.38 m3 of storage inside and 0.09 m3 in the drawbar box.
- **Lateral balance**: the split's outdoor unit is on the rear wall's road half and the inverter on the partition's road end, and the battery plinth stands 125 mm off the kerb wall. Why: the plinth, washer and dryer put the centre of gravity 89 mm to the kerb side at tare, against a 50 mm rule. It is now 33 mm at worst (MAS-11). So the rear door D2 hinges on its kerb edge, to swing away from the outdoor unit. That keeps the decision's reason and reverses its side.
- **XT rear shock crossmember**: RHS 150 x 50 x 4 in place of 150 x 50 x 3. Why: the grey tank fills the rear bay, so its plan braces went, and the rear recovery check (CHS-08A) needed the thicker wall. The 2 mm grey tank guard would buckle at about 4 kN (est.) as a shear plate, against 25.8 kN.
- **Frame checks**: each member is checked against its own grade. Why: the RHS 127 x 51 x 3.5 raisers, outriggers and floor crossmembers are C350L0 (report 22), and the model had checked every member at the rails' 450 MPa.

## 21 Nov, 03:12

Decisions on research report 26 (the Elite kit's wheels, rear track and brakes, 21 Nov 2026).

- **Wheel and plate (H18)**: keep the shared ROH Trak 2 16 x 7 ET 0 steel wheel, rated 1,400 kg, on the ute, the trailer and both spares, with the LT265/75R16 123/120 tyres. The LC5 certifier plates the Elite at a 2,800 kg rear axle (2 x 1,400 kg) and a 4,450 kg GVM (1,650 + 2,800 kg) in the same inspection (GV03), the derate path Lovells already offers on its 4,200 kg kit. Why: no 16 inch wheel that fits both vehicles has a published rating of 1,425 kg, half the Elite's 2,850 kg rear axle, and the steel wheel can be beaten back into shape in the field. The model's rear axle with growth, 2,586 kg towing, keeps 214 kg in hand. Fallback, if the derate is refused or the Elite's front caliper fouls the steel wheel at the S4 dry fit: Toyota's 16 x 7 ET 0 alloy on all ten wheels, about AUD 3,200 more (est.), a contingency line in the car build (WT04).
- **Box width (H19)**: Toyota's body builder text (BBGJ1007 section 1-2) says "the width of a mounted part should be 60 mm maximum as measured from the outermost point of the cab of the base vehicle (not including the outside mirror)". That is measured on each side, so 1,790 + 2 x 60 = 1,910 mm. The decision log's 1,850 mm (11:25) read it as 60 mm in total. The box body stays 1,850 mm, and a flare over each rear tub takes the box to 1,890 mm there. Why: the Elite's 1,555 mm rear track (research report 26, est.) puts each rear tyre 47.5 mm further out, 11 mm from the body's skin, against Toyota's 30 mm (UGW-03). The flares restore 31 mm. The factory GXL is already 1,870 mm over its own wheel flares (report 16). UTE-09 now has two limits, body 1,850 mm and flares 1,910 mm. The tubs, the floor between them and Lovells' mudflaps move out with the tyres, and the road-side tool chest is 30 mm narrower so it stays on its tub. The box is 436.5 kg, 3.5 kg under UGB-01's 440 kg, and costs AUD 51,799 shop-built or AUD 26,776 owner-built.
- **Brakes (H17 follow-on)**: the Elite brings its own front and rear brakes, booster and handbrake kit, so BR01 to BR04 go. BR05, the DOT 4 flush, stays as part of the fitter's bleed, and BR06 adds a spare set of Elite pads to carry (AUD 350, est.).
