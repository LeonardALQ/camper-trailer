# 09: Trailer wheels, tyres and stud pattern

Note added 20 Nov 2026, for the LandCruiser 79: the tow vehicle is now a Toyota LandCruiser 79 single cab V8 in place of the Porsche Cayenne, and it carries both bikes in an enclosed box on its tray (UTE-GARAGE.md). Reports 14 to 16 and VEHICLE.md cover the vehicle, and TRAILER.md revision H is the smaller trailer without a garage, plated at 3,100 kg ATM. The trailer now shares the ute's wheel and tyre, ROH Trak 2 16 x 7 ET 0 5 x 150 with LT265/75R16 123/120 (report 16 section 4), so this report's 6 x 139.7 wheel and LT245/70R16 tyre are superseded.

Research report, 19 Nov 2026. The trailer no longer shares the Cayenne's wheel (owner's decision after Porsche confirmed that none of its 18-inch wheels is rated for 984 kg or more). This report chooses the trailer's own wheel, tyre and hub stud pattern for the Cruisemaster XT Freestyle Air 4,500 kg tandem with 12-inch ABS-enabled electric drums and Bosch TSC. Report 10 covers the car.

Terms used throughout:

- Load index (LI): the number on the sidewall that sets the tyre's maximum load. LI 113 is 1,150 kg, 115 is 1,215 kg, 118 is 1,320 kg, 120 is 1,400 kg, 121 is 1,450 kg and 123 is 1,550 kg. LT tyres carry two numbers, single and dual. Only the single figure matters here.
- Load range: the strength class of a light-truck (LT) tyre. Load range E is the old "10 ply" rating, with an 80 psi (550 kPa) maximum. Load range D is "8 ply", 65 psi.
- ET (offset): the distance in mm from the wheel's centreline to its mounting face. Positive ET puts the mounting face outboard of the centreline, which pulls the tyre inboard.
- C/F (chassis to face): Cruisemaster's distance from the outer face of the chassis rail to the outer face of the hub drum.
- Effective C/F: C/F minus ET, the distance from the rail's outer face to the wheel's centreline. This is what loads the stub axle.
- Spigot: the raised centre of the hub drum that the wheel's centre bore passes over.
- Stud-centric wheel: a wheel centred by its conical nut seats rather than by its bore. Most steel 4WD and trailer wheels are stud-centric.

Why this combination:

- 6 x 139.7 is the six-stud pattern shared by most Australian utes and 4WDs, so it gives the best chance of finding a wheel in a remote town or wrecker. The 93 mm spigot is the smallest Cruisemaster offers on 12-inch drums, so any 6 x 139.7 wheel with a bore of 93 mm or more goes over it. The 1/2 inch UNF stud is the common trailer stud, sold by caravan and trailer parts shops, and Cruisemaster fits it to its 12-inch drums as standard.
- The ROH Trak 2 is a mine-site steel wheel with a published 1,250 kg rating, a lifetime structural warranty and a positive offset, which Cruisemaster requires. A steel rim bent on a rock can usually be hammered back far enough to seal and get home. An alloy rim usually cracks.
- LT245/70R16 is the tallest LT size that keeps the 830 mm axle spacing, so it has the most air and sidewall of the sizes that keep the floor near 858 mm. At the 816 kg each tyre carries fully loaded, its load-matched pressure is about 35 psi, against about 41 psi for LT225/75R16 (estimate, see the comparison). That gives more room to air down on sand and corrugations without overloading the carcass.
- The same ROH 16x7 +7 wheel also takes LT225/75R16 (approved rims 6.0 to 7.5 inch) and LT245/75R16 (6.5 to 8.0 inch), both stocked by Toyo Australia and Yokohama Australia ([Toyo Australia A/T III sheet](https://toyotires.s3-ap-southeast-2.amazonaws.com/public/brochures/Toyo%20Tires-Open%20Country%20AT_Three-Product%20Specifications.pdf), [Yokohama Australia](https://www.yokohama.com.au/our-range/tyres/geolandar-a-t-g015)). So a replacement tyre can be found even where LT245/70R16 is not stocked.

What blocks the choice: nothing in principle. ROH has to give the Trak 2 16x7 +7's weight, centre bore and nut seat, and Cruisemaster has to confirm the LC6 1/2 inch, 93 mm spigot hub as an off-the-shelf ABS-enabled 12-inch drum and the effective C/F limit below. Both are questions, listed at the end.

One finding changes an input in TRAILER.md. Cruisemaster's C/F sheet marks every effective C/F above 200 mm as unacceptable for the 4,500 kg XT Air tandem ([C/F sheet](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/wp-content/uploads/CFnOffset.Infosheet.Rev_.1.April23.pdf)). The stockist's guide explains that the chart assumes zero-offset wheels and that a positive offset brings the effective C/F back into range ([DIY Caravans FAQ](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/diy-guide-cruisemaster-suspension-kits-faqs/)). The old plan (C/F 240, ET 53, effective 187 mm) was inside the limit. The new plan must keep C/F minus ET at or under 200 mm too. C/F 195 with ET +7 gives 188 mm.

Jeep, Land Rover, Chev and Ram patterns are by special request, limited by spigot and bore clearance (same sheet).

Spigots on 12-inch drums are 93 mm ("generic size suitable for most stud centric wheels"), 106 mm (traditionally Prado) and 110 mm (traditionally Landcruiser 200, 100 and 70 series) ([spigot sheet](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/wp-content/uploads/Spigotsize.Infosheet.Rev_.1.April23.pdf)). The stockist's order form for the XT Freestyle Air tandem lets any of the four 12-inch drum patterns be combined with a 93, 106 or 110 mm spigot, and offers both standard and ABS-enabled 12-inch A/T drums ([stockist](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/shop/cruisemaster-xt-freestyle-air-suspension-kit-tandem/)). Neither sheet ties a spigot to a pattern, and neither says whether every combination is stocked with the ABS tone ring. That is a question for Cruisemaster.

Choice among the four:

- LC6 1/2 inch, 93 mm spigot (recommended). Most wheels on the road in Australia's six-stud utes and 4WDs use 6 x 139.7. The 1/2 inch UNF stud and its 60 degree nut are general trailer parts. A borrowed vehicle wheel has the right holes, but its owner's nuts may be a different thread or seat, so carry a spare set of the trailer's own nuts.
- NP6 M14. Same PCD with a stronger stud: an M14 x 1.5 stud has a tensile stress area of 124.5 mm², against 103.2 mm² for 1/2-20 UNF, 21 % more (standard thread formulas, my arithmetic). Its studs and nuts are less common than 1/2 inch UNF in trailer shops. Cruisemaster rates both on the same 12-inch drums, so the extra strength is not needed for the rating.
- LC5 M14 (5 x 150). ROH's steel 16x7 0 in 5 x 150 is rated 1,400 kg, the highest steel rating found ([ROH Trak 2](https://roh.com.au/wheels/trak-2)). Fewer vehicles use 5 x 150, and zero offset is the only 16-inch steel choice in it. The runner-up.
- LC6 M12. No nuts from Cruisemaster. Dropped.

## 2. Tyre sizes compared

All are LT all-terrain, 16 inch unless stated. Diameter is calculated from the size (rim + 2 x width x aspect). Where a maker publishes a diameter it is given too. Floor, ramp, travel height and centre of gravity follow the brief's rule: garage floor surface Z = tyre diameter + 120 mm, so each mm of diameter adds 1 mm to the floor, the travel height and (very nearly) the centre of gravity. Ramp angle = asin(floor / 3,048 mm). Today's 255/55 R18 (737.7 mm) gives floor 858, ramp 16.35 degrees, travel height 2,748 mm and a fully loaded centre of gravity at Z 1,227 (TRAILER.md).

Notes on the table:

- Load index and the 1,150 kg preference. Every size above meets 1,150 kg in load range E. In load range D, LT245/70R16 is only 113/110 (1,150 kg) in the BFGoodrich KO2 and KO3, exactly the preference and 3 % over the 1,117 kg growth check ([KO2 table](https://tiresize.com/tires/BFGoodrich/All-Terrain-TA-KO2.htm), [KO3 table](https://tiresize.com/tires/BFGoodrich/All-Terrain-TA-KO3.htm)).
- Centre of gravity. The body rises with the floor, one for one. The wheels and hubs rise only half as much, because only their radius grows. They are about 220 kg of the 3,485 kg fully loaded trailer (report 06 section 2, estimate), so the true rise is about 97 % of the diameter change. The table uses the full change, which overstates it by under 2 mm.
- Load-matched pressure is the pressure at which each tyre's rated load equals 816 kg, the TC2 axle group load of 3,265 kg shared over four tyres. It uses the usual power-law approximation of the load and inflation tables, load proportional to pressure to the power 0.585, from each size's 80 psi rating. It is an estimate for ranking only. Set real pressures from the maker's table for the weighed loads. A lower load-matched pressure means the tyre has more air volume in hand, so it can be aired down further on sand and corrugations before its sidewall works beyond its rated deflection.
- Axle spacing. 880 mm needs a second centre crossmember with the hinge and shock crossmembers at least 1,660 mm apart (report 06 section 8). That crossmember, 150 x 50 x 3 RHS across the 1,186 mm between the rails, weighs about 11 kg (estimate, 9.1 kg/m).
- LT265/75R16 sits on the 804 mm line by calculation and up to 1 mm over it by the makers' figures, before tyre growth at speed. LT235/85R16 is over it. Neither should be used without Cruisemaster's written agreement.
- The 15-inch size. Cruisemaster's 12-inch drum needs a 15-inch rim at least, because the drum is about 13 inches across ([DIY Caravans FAQ](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/diy-guide-cruisemaster-suspension-kits-faqs/)). LT235/75R15 in load range E (116/113) was found only in the Falken Wildpeak A/T4W ([Falken table](https://tiresize.com/tires/Falken/WildPeak-AT4W.htm)). The common 15-inch 4WD sizes, LT235/75R15 104 (900 kg) and 31x10.50R15 109 (1,030 kg), fail the load. 15 inch loses on availability.

How the sizes rank for this trailer:

1. LT245/70R16 keeps the 830 mm spacing, raises the floor only 12 mm, carries 1,320 kg and has the most air of the 830 mm sizes. Its weakness is availability: of the Australian lists checked, only Yokohama's carries it in load range E. With one spare aboard, and the same wheel taking LT225/75R16 and LT245/75R16, that is acceptable.
2. LT225/75R16 is the lightest (17.2 to 19.3 kg) and lowest, and three Australian ranges carry it. It has the least margin (1,215 kg against 1,117 kg, 9 %) and the narrowest footprint, and it needs about 6 psi more than LT245/70R16 at the same load, so it airs down least. It is the right choice if mass becomes the deciding factor.
3. LT245/75R16 is on every Australian list checked and carries 1,400 kg, but it moves the axles to 880 mm, adds about 11 kg of crossmember and 2 to 3 kg per tyre, and raises the floor 36 to 42 mm.
4. LT265/75R16 and LT235/85R16 are the classic outback sizes, but they sit on or over the XT's 804 mm tyre limit and raise the floor 66 to 68 mm.

Steel against alloy. ## 4. Brakes, ABS and wheel clearance

- Minimum wheel. Cruisemaster's stockist says 12-inch drums need at least a 15-inch rim, because the drum is about 13 inches (330 mm) across, and 12-inch discs need 16 inch ([DIY Caravans FAQ](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/diy-guide-cruisemaster-suspension-kits-faqs/)). A 16-inch rim has a 406 mm bead seat, so the barrel clears the drum by roughly 30 mm on the radius (my estimate, allowing for the rim's wall).
- ABS. Cruisemaster's ABS-enabled A/T brake adds a wheel speed sensor, positioner, tone ring and flylead to the backing plate ([brake sheet](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/Brakes.Infosheet.Rev.1.April24.pdf)). ABS is available on 10 and 12-inch electric drums only ([Cruisemaster ABS](https://cruisemaster.com.au/absenabled)). The sensor sits inside the drum envelope, so it sets no wheel clearance of its own (my reading of the sheet). Cruisemaster publishes no inner-face clearance figure for wheels on the 12-inch drum. Ask it to check the ROH centre disc depth.

## 5. Speed rating

No Australian rule found sets a numeric minimum speed category for trailer tyres.

- VSB1 says trailer tyres "must be of suitable specifications for the trailer's intended use", including speed rating, that wheels and tyres must comply with the ADRs, and that a trailer of 3,500 kg GTM or less carries a tyre placard showing size, load rating, speed rating and cold pressure ([VSB1 Rev 6, sections 12.1 to 12.3](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/630d18fb-e7b9-4863-b5b6-b89af2cdedee/vsb1-revision-6-.pdf)).
- ADR 95/00 (Installation of Tyres) applies to trailers (categories TA to TD). For trailers it asks only that tyres be suitable "with respect to their dimensions, speed and load performance characteristics", that new LT tyres comply with ADR 96 (Commercial Vehicle Tyres), and that no radial tyre need more than 825 kPa cold to carry the GTM ([ADR 95/00, clauses 3 and 5.3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2018L01516/asmade/2018-10-31/text/original/pdf)). The numeric speed tests in its Appendix A apply only to MA, MB and MC cars.
- I found nothing from TfNSW that adds a trailer speed category.

So the test is suitability for the towing speed, 110 km/h at most. Q (160 km/h), R (170 km/h) and S (180 km/h) all leave at least 50 km/h of margin. Recommend R or S. A Q tyre, such as the Toyo LT225/75R16, is acceptable. The LT tyres here need at most 550 kPa, well under 825 kPa. Put the size, 118/115R, and the cold pressure for the weighed load on the trailer's placard.

## 8. The Cruisemaster design session

No longer needed:

- Measuring the Porsche wheel at the session, and the C/F check against ET53 (input SUP-04 as written).

Ask instead:

1. Is the LC6 1/2 inch UNF x 41 mm pattern with a 93 mm spigot a standard part on the 12-inch ABS-enabled A/T drum (VT hub) for the XT Freestyle Air 4,500 kg tandem, with the tone ring and wheel speed sensor unchanged?
2. Does the C/F chart's 200 mm limit for the 4,500 kg air tandem apply to effective C/F (C/F minus ET), as the stockist describes? Confirm C/F 195 with ET +7 (effective 188 mm).
3. Confirm the 830 mm axle spacing with a 752 mm tyre, which leaves 78 mm between the tyres against the 75 mm minimum.
4. Which nuts does Cruisemaster supply for the 1/2 inch UNF stud (seat angle, hex size, torque), and does the 41 mm stud give full thread engagement through the ROH Trak 2's mounting disc? If the ROH Vapour alloy is chosen, is the 50 mm stud available on the drum?
5. Does the ROH Trak 2 16x7 +7 centre disc clear the 12-inch backing plate, the park brake and the ABS flylead? Cruisemaster may want one wheel or ROH's drawing.
6. With no wheel to measure, can the design session go ahead from drawings, and does that shorten the 6 to 8 week lead time?

Ask ROH: the Trak 2 16x7 +7's weight, centre bore, nut seat and stud hole diameter, and whether its 1,250 kg rating holds at the 80 psi of a load range E tyre.
