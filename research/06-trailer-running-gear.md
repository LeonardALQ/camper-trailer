# 06 Trailer running gear: suspension, hubs, brakes and sway control

Research report, 17 Nov 2026. It chooses the independent suspension, hubs, brakes and electronic sway control as one package for the tandem trailer at 3,500 kg ATM. Masses come from the design so far: about 2.5 t with the bikes out and the tanks empty, about 3.2 t loaded, and 381 kg of bikes over the axles. The towball download is taken as 140 kg in every case, because the bikes sit over the axle group. The tow vehicle report confirms the towing limits and sets the final wheel size.

Note added after reports 07 and 08. Porsche Australia rated its factory towbar at 280 kg on Australian cars, against 140 kg in Europe, and the design now aims for about 220 kg on the ball. At a 3,500 kg ATM that leaves 3,280 kg on the axles, which the 3,700 kg XT kit's 3,330 kg GTM covers. VSB1's 120% rule then asks 984 kg per wheel instead of 1,008 kg. Report 07 chose a 255/55 R18 tyre on Porsche's 8J x 18 wheel, which Porsche approves for the 2011 to 2014 GTS without ceramic brakes. The owner's car has the standard brakes and is registered as a passenger car, so the tyre is the Yokohama Geolandar A/T G015 109H XL. The figures below were worked out for 140 kg and 19 or 20 inch wheels, and change little.

## Recommendation

Fit a Cruisemaster XT Freestyle coil tandem with standard (straight) arms, 12 inch ABS-enabled electric drum brakes and Bosch Trailer Safety Control (TSC) for ABS and sway control. Order the hub drums drilled 5 x 130 with M14 x 1.5 studs and a 71.5 mm spigot, and run the Cayenne's own wheels on R14 ball-seat nuts.

The XT has one of the longest records of any independent kit a private builder can buy and fit. Cruisemaster says the XT has led independent trailer suspension for well over 10 years ([XT page](https://cruisemaster.com.au/xt/)), and the coil version was proven on a 2009 Cape York test trip ([Cruisemaster](https://cruisemaster.com.au/2009-escape-to-the-cape/)). Cruisemaster publishes its installation drawings, sells through a stockist to home builders, and has a national service network. The XT also has the smallest published bump travel of the off-road kits (60 mm), which gives the lowest flat floor over the tyres.

Coil beats air for this trailer. With the bikes in, a representative coil gives about 1.8 Hz against about 1.9 to 2.0 Hz for the airbags. With the bikes out the coil stiffens to about 2.1 Hz and the trailer rises about 25 mm, while the airbags stay near 1.9 to 2.0 Hz at constant height. The ride difference is small. The coil needs no compressor, cannot be punctured and needs almost no field repair.

Three earlier choices change:

- Electric-over-hydraulic disc brakes are dropped. No sway control system is approved with Cruisemaster's disc brakes. AL-KO ESC accepts discs only as AL-KO's 299 mm disc with an AL-KO caliper and an AL-KO iQ7 or Dexter EH1600 actuator ([AL-KO ESC FAQ](https://alko.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/ALKO_ESC_FAQ_NEW_VEHICLES_2023.pdf)), and AL-KO rates that caliper at 1,400 kg per pair on 15 and 16 inch wheels ([AL-KO hydraulic disc brakes](https://alko.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Hyd-Disc-Brakes.pdf)). This trailer puts 1,680 kg on each axle at its ATM.
- AL-KO ESC is replaced by Bosch TSC. TSC adds ABS, brakes each wheel separately, and its sway functions only act above 65 km/h, so it does not need unplugging off-road. AL-KO ESC stays a valid alternative if AL-KO electric brakes are fitted to the Cruisemaster drums.
- The 5 x 130 hubs are possible only as a special order. Neither Cruisemaster nor AL-KO stocks the pattern, and Cruisemaster's smallest standard spigot (93 mm) will not enter the Porsche's 71.6 mm centre bore. Sharing the Cayenne's own wheels also depends on their load rating. VSB1 asks 984 to 1,008 kg per wheel on this trailer, depending on the towball load, and Porsche does not publish a figure.

The AL-KO AKS coupling is outside this report.

The coil rating needs one decision. Cruisemaster rates its tandem kits by ATM and GTM and assumes 7 to 12% of ATM on the drawbar ([stockist notes](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/shop/cruisemaster-xt-freestyle-coil-suspension-kit-tandem/)). With only 140 kg on the ball, a 3,500 kg ATM puts 3,360 kg on the axles. That is 30 kg over the 3,700 kg kit's 3,330 kg GTM. VSB1 also asks each axle of a close-coupled non-load-sharing group, and its wheels and tyres, to carry 120% of its load at ATM ([VSB1 Rev 6, axles and suspension](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/630d18fb-e7b9-4863-b5b6-b89af2cdedee/vsb1-revision-6-.pdf)). At 3,500 kg that is 2,016 kg per axle and 1,008 kg per wheel. Only the 4,500 kg XT (4,050 kg GTM, 2,025 kg per axle) clears that on paper. Its 33SC-1250-1400 coils would be too stiff for a 2.5 to 3.2 t trailer. So ask Cruisemaster to confirm the spring for 3,360 kg GTM with 2.5 to 3.2 t working masses, and to say whether its tandem ratings already include the 120% rule. If Cruisemaster can only meet the plate with the stiff coils, change to XT Air (section 2). At the 220 kg towball target set after report 07, the axles carry 3,280 kg, inside the 3,700 kg kit's 3,330 kg GTM.

## 2. Coil against air

### Inputs

| Case | Trailer mass | Axle load | Sprung mass per wheel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bikes out, tanks empty | 2,500 kg | 1,180 kg | 535 kg |
| Loaded | 3,200 kg | 1,530 kg | 710 kg |
| Plated ATM | 3,500 kg | 1,680 kg | 785 kg |

Sprung mass per wheel = (trailer mass - 140 kg towball - 4 x 55 kg unsprung) / 4. The 55 kg of unsprung mass per corner is an estimate: about 27 kg for the wheel and tyre, 17 kg for the hub drum and brake, and 11 kg for half the arm and the shocks.

### Coil

Cruisemaster does not publish its coil rates, so the coil figures rest on one assumption. I assumed the 33SC-950 coil deflects 80 mm at the wheel under its rated 777 kg sprung load. The arm has 60 mm of droop, so the coil is still compressed about 20 mm at full droop and stays seated. Natural frequency is f = (1/2 pi) x square root of (g / static deflection).

| Static deflection at rated load (assumed) | Wheel rate | Loaded (3.2 t) | Bikes out (2.5 t) | Rise when the bikes come out | Droop left, bikes out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 mm | 127 N/mm | 2.13 Hz | 2.45 Hz | 19 mm | 41 mm |
| 80 mm (central estimate) | 95 N/mm | 1.84 Hz | 2.12 Hz | 25 mm | 35 mm |
| 100 mm | 76 N/mm | 1.65 Hz | 1.90 Hz | 31 mm | 29 mm |

Whatever the rate, a linear coil's bounce frequency rises 15% when the bikes come out, because the frequency scales with the square root of 710/535.

### Air at constant ride height

On the XT Air the airbag sits directly over the stub axle (drawing 33XT AIR, sheet 2), so the bag carries the wheel load one to one. Firestone publishes the natural frequency of each bellows at its design height, and it barely changes with pressure.

| Firestone bellows | 40 psi | 60 psi | 80 psi | 100 psi | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Style 224 at 6.5 in (165 mm), 3,400 kg XT kit | 2.20 Hz, 730 lb | 2.13 Hz, 1,130 lb | 2.10 Hz, 1,530 lb | 2.07 Hz, 1,940 lb | [Firestone 224](https://www.firestoneairide.com/content/dam/fsip/pdfs/product-specs/airstroke-airmount/double-convoluted/224.pdf) |
| Style 26 at 8.0 in (203 mm), 4,500 kg XT kit | 2.41 Hz, 590 lb | 2.13 Hz, 980 lb | 2.04 Hz, 1,360 lb | 2.01 Hz, 1,780 lb | [Firestone 26](https://www.firestoneairide.com/content/dam/fsip/pdfs/product-specs/airstroke-airmount/double-convoluted/26.pdf) |

Cruisemaster sets these bags lower, at 150 and 165 mm ([CIS-006](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/006%20Cruisemaster%20XTAir.pdf)). I estimated the frequency at those heights from Firestone's force and volume tables, using an adiabatic gas law with n = 1.38. These are estimates.

| Case | Style 224 at 150 mm | Style 26 at 165 mm |
|---|---|---|
| Bikes out | 56 psi, 2.04 Hz | 50 psi, 1.93 Hz |
| Loaded | 74 psi, 2.02 Hz | 66 psi, 1.90 Hz |
| Plated ATM | 82 psi, 2.01 Hz | 74 psi, 1.89 Hz |

Both bags stay within about 2% of the same frequency across the whole load range, and the ride height does not change. Cruisemaster expects 50 to 90 psi and forbids more than 100 psi.

### What this means

Ride quality. With the bikes in, the case that matters for straps, the coil (about 1.8 Hz) and the bags (1.9 to 2.0 Hz) are close. With the bikes out the coil is up to about 10% stiffer than air. The trailer is then carrying only cabinetry. Both frequencies sit far below corrugation inputs, quoted at 12 to 18 Hz in report 02, so damping and tyre pressure decide most of the shaking. The G40 twin shocks matter more than the spring type here.

Reliability. A coil has nothing to leak. Cruisemaster treats a loss of 3 to 4 psi a week as normal for airbags, and asks for compressor filter checks every 3 months or 5,000 km and valve rebuilds every 2 years ([CIS-006](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/006%20Cruisemaster%20XTAir.pdf)). Bags must be washed free of mud and stones to avoid punctures.

Field repair. A punctured bag cannot be repaired. Cruisemaster says do not attempt it ([CIS-006](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/006%20Cruisemaster%20XTAir.pdf)). The trailer can limp home on the bump stops at very slow speed, with a risk of damage to the arm brackets and no warranty. The spare is a 2.3 kg style 224 or 2.4 kg style 26 bag (5.0 and 5.3 lb assembly weights on the same Firestone sheets), plus fittings, tube and Loctite 567. A failed compressor is less serious, because the bags can be filled through the manual inflation points from a portable compressor or a service station. A broken coil is rare, and the polyurethane bump stop inside it still carries the wheel.

Air supply. Cruisemaster's air levels range from manual valves to a 12 V compressor, 11 litre tank, dual gauges, pressure switch and automatic height valves ([CIS-006](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/006%20Cruisemaster%20XTAir.pdf)). The system wants the 12 V supply above 12.0 V ([user guide](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/85-USERGUIDE-XT.pdf)). Its weight is not published. I estimate 8 to 12 kg for the compressor, tank, panel and lines.

Floor height. The floor must clear the tyre at the bump stop, and the bump stop is in the same place for coil and air. So both give the same floor height (section 7). Air can be dumped onto the bump stops at camp to lower the floor about 60 mm for loading the bikes. On the 3,048 mm ramp that takes a 865 mm floor from 16.5 to 15.3 degrees. With a coil, the floor sits about 25 mm higher when the bikes come out, which is when they are loaded.

The plate. Air carries the plated ATM with no change in ride, which suits the 120% rule. A coil chosen to meet the plate on paper may be too stiff for the real loads. This is the one case where air would win (see the rating note under the package table).

Recommendation: coil, provided Cruisemaster confirms a spring that suits 2.5 to 3.2 t and the plated GTM. Choose XT Air if it cannot.

### What a trailer hub needs for a Porsche wheel

1. A 5 x 130 mm pitch circle.
2. M14 x 1.5 press-in studs. Cruisemaster already uses M14 x 1.5 studs on its LC5 and NP6 hubs, 40 mm long on drums and 52 mm on discs ([stud sheet](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/StudPatterns.Infosheet.Rev.1.April23.pdf)). Measure the depth of the Porsche wheel's bolt holes before choosing a length. The stud must pass right through the wheel and give the nut full thread engagement.
3. Nuts with an R14 spherical seat. A 60 degree conical nut only touches the Porsche seat on a line and will work loose.
4. A spigot of 71.5 mm, so the wheel centres on the hub. Cruisemaster warns that a wheel not centred as designed vibrates, loosens and can shear studs ([user guide](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/85-USERGUIDE-XT.pdf)). A smaller spigot with a fitted hub-centric ring also works. A 93 mm or larger spigot will not fit.
5. Positive offset only. Cruisemaster does not allow negative offset and notes that positive offset reduces the effective chassis-to-face distance ([coil guide](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/Installation/33XTCOIL-INSTALLATIONGUIDE-Rev2.pdf)). Porsche's ET 47 to 59 qualifies.
6. Track = outer rail width + 2 x (C/F - ET). With ET 47 and C/F 240 mm, each wheel centre sits 193 mm outside the rail. A 275 mm tyre then clears the rail by 56 mm. Keep C/F at 225 mm or more with ET 47, and 235 mm or more with ET 57 to 59, for about 40 mm of tyre-to-rail clearance (my figures).
7. A wheel load rating of at least 1,008 kg per wheel at 3,500 kg ATM with 140 kg on the ball, or 984 kg at the 220 kg design target (the 120% rule). The 275/45 R20 110 tyre carries 1,060 kg and the 255/55 R18 109 tyre 1,030 kg. Porsche does not publish its wheels' load rating. The aftermarket 21 inch wheel in the approval report is rated 1,010 kg, which shows how close this is. The Cayenne's own permissible gross mass is 2,840 kg ([brochure](https://www.auto-brochures.com/makes/Porsche/Cayenne/Porsche_US%20Cayenne-GTS_2012.pdf)). Even with 60% of that on one axle, each car wheel carries about 850 kg, so the factory wheels may well be rated below 1,008 kg (estimate). If they are, the shared wheel has to be a rated 5 x 130 wheel. OZ Racing says its Rally Raid wheel is built for up to 1,200 kg ([OZ Rally Raid](https://www.ozracing.com/uk/alloy-wheels/oz-racing/all-terrain/rally-raid)). Retailers list the 5 x 130 version with an 84.1 mm bore, which would need a centring ring on both car and trailer, and its fit over the GTS brakes belongs to the tow vehicle report.

Is it available? Cruisemaster's standard patterns are Ford 5 stud, Landcruiser 6 stud (1/2 inch and M12), Landcruiser 5 x 150 M14 and Patrol 6 x 139.7 M14. It offers Jeep, Land Rover, Chev and Ram "by special request", with suitability limited by spigot size and centre bore clearance ([stud sheet](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/StudPatterns.Infosheet.Rev.1.April23.pdf)). The stockist's order form has an "Other" pattern option ([stockist](https://www.diycaravans.com.au/shop/cruisemaster-xt-freestyle-coil-suspension-kit-tandem/)). AL-KO stocks no 5 x 130 drum or disc hub. It can drill drums to other patterns on request ([electric drums](https://alko.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/ELEC-Drum.pdf)), and disc hubs "depending on compatibility" ([disc hubs](https://alko.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2020/05/HYD-MECH-DISC.pdf)).

It looks feasible on the VT hub. The outer bearing cup is 59.13 mm across and the dust cap 63 mm ([VT kit](https://cruisemaster.com.au/bearing-kit-vt-12-drum-disc-hub/)), so a 71.5 mm spigot keeps about 4 mm of wall round the cap. The 130 mm stud circle leaves the stud holes well clear of the bearings. Only Cruisemaster can say whether its casting and the ABS tone ring allow it. Avoid pitch-change adaptor plates, which add a joint and a set of fasteners at each wheel.

## 7. Floor height

The floor must clear the tyre at full bump. At static ride height:

floor underside = static loaded radius + bump travel + free tyre radius + clearance.

Assumptions: the static loaded radius is the free radius less 20 mm (estimate for a loaded road tyre). Bump travel is 60 mm for the XT, coil or air, and 80 mm for the ATX (section 1). The clearance is 50 mm, to cover bump stop squash on hard hits, tyre growth at speed and packed mud (my allowance, Cruisemaster leaves guard clearance to the builder). The result does not depend on the arm type or the ride height, because the tyre top at the bump stop is fixed relative to the body.

Cruisemaster's drawing checks the method. It puts an 804 mm tyre 150 mm above the top of a 100 mm rail at full bump. The formula gives 147 mm.

The Cayenne's 275/45 R20 is 755.5 mm across (508 + 2 x 0.45 x 275, calculated), which gives a floor underside of about 846 mm on 830 mm axle spacing. The 265/50 R19 is 747.6 mm (482.6 + 2 x 0.50 x 265), about 838 mm. The 255/55 R18 chosen in report 07 is 737.7 mm (457.2 + 2 x 0.55 x 255), about 828 mm. With XT Air the floor can drop about 60 mm at camp by venting the bags onto the bump stops. Do it only when parked. Cruisemaster limits running above the normal height to 10 km/h and treats running on the bump stops as an emergency measure at very slow speed ([CIS-006](https://cruisemaster.com.au/content/cis/006%20Cruisemaster%20XTAir.pdf)).

The floor problem is real. With a 750 mm tyre the floor underside is about 270 mm above the rail underside at the hinges. A 150 mm main rail leaves about 120 mm between the rail top and the floor underside. That gap has to be filled with a raised floor frame, deeper rails, or outriggers above the tyres. The chassis design should solve it.

Track choice. A track close to the Cayenne's lets the trailer run in the car's wheel tracks in sand and ruts. A wider track raises roll stability, which matters with a high garage floor and 381 kg of bikes above it. At 2,000 mm the tyres' outer edges sit at about 2,275 mm, inside the 2,400 mm body. The engineer should set this with the centre of gravity height from the mass schedule.
