### 1.2 Choice: Lippert HappiJac

The HappiJac is the only bought lift with the capacity for this bed. With the Smart Bed the moving bed would weigh about 61 kg (its 8.6 kg lift, the 20.9 kg platform, the 25.5 kg mattress, the underlay and 6 kg of bedding), above its 60 kg limit for the whole bed. The HappiJac carries 204 kg moving, so it has room for a comfortable mattress and for road shock while parked at the top. It has the longest record, its motor and controller are sold in Australia, and it can be lowered by hand with a brake lever and a socket.

Three additions bring it to H1:

- Positive travel locks. Fit four stainless spring index plungers, one per trolley, into holes drilled in the C-channels at the day and night stops. The motor brake then only holds the bed between stops. Fit a microswitch on each plunger so the motor cannot run while any plunger is engaged (0.6 kg and AUD 250, both est.).
- An obstruction stop. Fit a pressure-sensitive safety edge along the lower edges of the platform and wire it to break the down circuit (0.8 kg and AUD 300, both est.). Keep the hold-to-run switch.
- Hardpoints. Bond 40 x 20 x 3 mm aluminium RHS, laid flat, into the side wall cores behind each of the four rails over their full height, as the panels are laid up. The rails then screw into 3 mm aluminium, which meets Lippert's backing rule.

The rails sit on the kerb-side and road-side walls, 1,067 mm apart along the trailer, with the bed overhanging them by about 240 mm front and back. The connecting shaft and motor sit at the top of the rails. Order the rail frame for a 2,330 mm wall-to-wall width and confirm the rail height against the interior height chosen in section 1.4. Spare motor: HappiJac 420326, USD 489.99, AUD 760 converted ([Panther RV](https://pantherrvproducts.com/420326/)).

### 1.3 Platform and mattress

The HappiJac support rails run along the two side walls, so the platform spans 2,290 mm wall to wall. A 20 mm or 25 mm glass-skinned panel is too flexible over that span. A 40 mm aluminium honeycomb panel with a 0.8 mm top skin and a 0.5 mm bottom skin gives a stiffness of 3.3e10 N mm2 per metre and deflects 7.5 mm (L/307) under two people, the mattress and bedding spread over the bed (calc.). L/200 needs 2.2e10 N mm2 per metre. The panel weighs about 5.5 kg/m2 (calc.: skins at 2.7 kg/m2 per mm, a 40 kg/m3 core, 0.4 kg/m2 of adhesive), 19.4 kg for 2,290 x 1,550 mm, plus 1.5 kg of edge trim. CEL Compocel AL is the reference panel: 25 mm with 1 + 1 mm skins weighs 7.6 kg/m2 ([CEL datasheet](https://www.chemical-concepts.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Compocel-AL-TDS.pdf)). Ask PanelMate (WA) for the same build. Price AUD 1,200 (est.).

A ventilated underlay stops condensation under the mattress: HyperVent, 19 mm, 0.45 kg (report 04). The Froli Star Mobil (43 mm) does not fit the height budget.

## 4. Hob, sink and hot drinks

Hob: keep the Dometic CVI1525. The APAC manual confirms two 180 mm zones, 1,000 W on the left and 1,300 W on the right, with a 5 min boost to 1,700 W on the right zone only. Total power is 2,300 W at 220 to 240 V on a 10 A supply, and Dometic asks for an inverter of at least 2,300 W continuous ([manual](https://media.dometic.com/externalassets/cvi1525_9620008712_125117.pdf?ref=-588583111)). It has 9 power levels, from 100 W to 1,000 W on the left and 100 W to 1,300 W on the right, plus overheat cut-out at 190 °C, pan detection and an automatic shut-down timer. Size 525 x 350 x 55 mm, mass 7.0 kg (report 13).

The hob has no user setting that caps total power. It reaches 2,300 W only with both zones at level 9. Level 6 on the right zone (1,000 W) with level 9 on the left (1,000 W) holds it at 2,000 W. The MultiPlus-II 24/5000 delivers 4,000 W at 25 °C (report 13), so the cap is a habit rather than a need. Report 20 sets any load-shedding rule.

Sink: keep the Dometic CVS1350G, 2.5 kg, the same Cooking Vision series as the hob (report 13). Its glass lid closes over the bowl and gives bench space when the sink is not in use. Fit a mixer with a 4 L/min aerator (section 9).

Hot drinks: use an induction-compatible stainless kettle on the hob. Boiling 1 L from 20 °C puts 0.093 kWh into the water, about 0.11 kWh from the battery at 85 % hob efficiency (est.). It adds no appliance and no peak above the hob's own. A boiling-water tap is rejected because it holds water near 100 °C all day, which adds standby loss to the battery budget. A 1,000 W low-wattage kettle is the fallback if the hob is busy.
