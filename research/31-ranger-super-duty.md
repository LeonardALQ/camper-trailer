# 31 The Ford Ranger Super Duty single cab chassis: ratings, price, power and the NSW path

## 1 Findings

The Super Duty needs none of the LandCruiser build's big jobs. Ford rates it at a 4,500 kg GVM, 4,500 kg braked towing and an 8,000 kg GCM, with 1,900 kg on the front axle and 2,800 kg on the rear, and it comes with front and rear lockers, a 130 L tank, a 4.5 t tow bar, an integrated trailer brake controller, a 12-pin socket, a snorkel and light truck tyres. So there is no GVM kit, no GCM revision, no engine rebuild, no clutch and no brake upgrade. Leonard wrote 4,495 kg for the GVM. Ford's figure is 4,500 kg, and this report uses it.

What the truck still needs is small: the tow pin of the trailer's DO45-type coupling in Ford's own 4.5 t hitch (the trailer is plated at 4,000 kg from 23 Nov 2026), towing mirrors for the 2,400 mm trailer, the 48 V trailer charging, a UHF radio, a compressor and a recovery kit, and the box mounted to Ford's body rules. With the purchase inspection and three weighings they come to AUD 5,882 before the power stage, AUD 6,846 with the allowance (VEHICLE.md).

A near-new single cab chassis costs about AUD 90,000. The one listing found, a 2025 truck with 13,343 km, asks AUD 89,990 with a steel tray, and a new one is AUD 82,990 before on-road costs, so new and near-new cost about the same drive-away.

The power target splits in two. 250 hp at the wheels is about 186 kW at the wheels, roughly 220 kW and 646 Nm at the crank (est.), 43 % over the Super Duty's 154 kW. It needs the same Garrett PowerMax turbo and intercooler as the plan and a calibration inside the plan's own 650 Nm cap, so it is an option for Leonard to weigh (section 8). What counts against it is the PowerMax's estimated 6 % of flow in hand and the lack of any published record at that power. 300 hp at the wheels is about 265 kW at the crank, 72 % over, past what the PowerMax can supply, and it needs a hybrid turbo and injectors that nobody sells for this engine in Australia. No tune written for the Super Duty's heavy-duty calibration and no dyno sheet for a tuned Super Duty were found. The plan registers about 205 kW and 650 Nm at the crank, about 173 kW or 232 hp at the wheels (est.), on the PowerMax turbo and intercooler with the DPF, SCR and EGR untouched, under one LC4 certificate. Any tune of this truck in NSW needs that certificate under VSI 06 Rev 4, and Ford's warranty will not pay for a failure the tune or the turbo causes.

## 4 The near-new price in Australia

Ford lists the single cab chassis at AUD 82,990 before on-road costs and without a tray. Ford's own trays cost AUD 5,966 to 8,166 fitted ([CarExpert, 20 Sep 2025](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-news/2026-ford-ranger-super-duty-full-specs-and-accessory-list-released), [Drive, 18 Sep 2025](https://www.drive.com.au/news/2026-ford-ranger-super-duty-full-specifications-and-accessories-revealed/)).

One near-new single cab chassis was found on sale on 23 Nov 2026: a 2025 truck with 13,343 km at a dealer in Ebbw Vale, Queensland, for AUD 89,990, dropped from AUD 91,990. It has a steel tray, a dual battery system, the 4,500 kg tow kit, tint and about 3 years and 1 month of Ford's 5-year warranty left ([Drive listing 970470638](https://www.drive.com.au/cars-for-sale/car/970470638/)). Drive's price analysis on the same page puts the market between AUD 74,822 and 91,450. No 2026 single cab chassis under 20,000 km was listed on Drive that day, so this listing is the basis.

The plan's purchase figure is that listing with New South Wales duty. Service NSW charges AUD 3 per AUD 100 of value, and the higher AUD 5 rate over AUD 45,000 applies to passenger vehicles only ([Service NSW](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/check-motor-vehicle-stamp-duty)). A single cab chassis is assumed to be a non-passenger vehicle, so the duty on AUD 89,990 is AUD 2,700, and the truck costs AUD 92,690.

A new truck costs about the same drive-away: AUD 82,990, about AUD 2,500 of dealer delivery, AUD 2,565 of duty and about AUD 1,600 of registration and CTP insurance, about AUD 89,700 (est.). It comes with no tray, which the bike box replaces anyway, and Ford's conditional 7-year warranty, which applies to new and demonstrator trucks with a warranty start on or after 1 Sep 2026 that are serviced at a Ford dealer (Ford specification sheet issued 29 Oct 2026). A near-new truck's tray is a part to sell, about AUD 2,500 (est.), which the plan does not count.

## 6 What more power does to the 10-speed, the driveline and the brakes

Ford rates this engine at 600 Nm in every Ranger that has it, behind the same 10-speed, and publishes no torque rating for the gearbox itself. Power is torque times engine speed, so more power can come from holding torque higher up the rev range rather than from more peak torque. 650 Nm at 3,000 rpm is 204 kW, so about 205 kW needs only 8 % more torque than Ford's 600 Nm. 220 kW at 3,250 rpm needs 646 Nm, inside the plan's 650 Nm cap, and 265 kW at 3,500 rpm needs 723 Nm, 21 % over the engine's 600 Nm and 11 % over the cap. The plan caps torque at 650 Nm in high range and keeps Ford's 600 Nm in low range, first and reverse, where the transfer case, front differential and CV joints see the most.

The 10-speed's temperature is read on an OBD gauge. A larger cooler is bought only if it runs above 110 °C sustained on a tow (est., the threshold the LandCruiser plan used for its gearbox). The brakes need nothing. Ford rates them for an 8,000 kg combination, and more power adds no mass, but VSB 14 asks the certifier to consider brakes on any engine modification, so the plan offers Ford's ratings and a brake test if the certifier asks for one. The turbo's speed sensor port takes Garrett's sensor, which shows on the dyno that the shaft stays under Garrett's 186.2 krpm. The DPF, the SCR with its AdBlue and the EGR stay as Ford fitted them. More fuel means more soot and more regenerations (est.).

## 7 The NSW legal path

Transport for NSW's [VSI 06 Rev 4](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-03/vsi-06-light-vehicle-modification-july-2026.pdf), in force from 1 July 2026, covers light vehicles with a GVM of 4.5 t or less (page 1), so the Super Duty at 4,500 kg is one. Three of its engine items apply.

- Item 2 needs a certificate for "increasing the original power output by more than 20%", with the example of a 100 kW vehicle modified to more than 120 kW. The Super Duty's original output is 154 kW, so the line is 184.8 kW.
- Item 5 needs one for anything that adversely affects the emissions set under the ADRs, such as non-original fuel injection equipment or removing the EGR or catalysts. Intercoolers and exhausts that keep the emission controls need none.
- Item 6 needs one for flashing the engine management system so that it alters emissions output, unless the vehicle maker approved it. So every tune of this truck needs a certificate, at any power.

The note under item 5 says a modified engine must be shown to keep its emission levels, and that the Interim NSW Vehicle Emissions Test Procedure for Modified and Individually Constructed Vehicles (May 2023) may be used in place of testing to the standards. VSCCS Bulletin 2 sets the evidence TfNSW accepts for light vehicles of 4.5 t GVM or less ([TfNSW, v4](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/system/files/media/documents/2024/VSCCS-Notice-19-VSCCS-Bulletin-No.2-v4.pdf)). The national code, VSB 14 Section LA, files the work under LA4, modifying the original engine, and treats it like a performance engine conversion: the emission standard the vehicle was built to, brakes as needed, installation and noise (report 14 section 4.2). Its LA1 code is where the 20 % rule comes from, for an equivalent engine with at most 20 % more power.

Ford built the Super Duty to the heavy-duty Euro VI standard, which is tested on an engine dyno rather than on the whole vehicle. A one-off truck cannot sensibly repeat that test (est.), so the interim NSW procedure is the realistic evidence. Whether an LC4 certifier accepts it for a heavy-duty calibration is the stage's main risk. The plan assumes yes, with the DPF, SCR and EGR working and no stored faults. If the certifier asks for a laboratory test, that is AUD 10,000 (est., report 14).

An LC4 certifier (engine, fuel, intake and exhaust, [VSCCS Bulletin 01](https://nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-01/vsccs-bulletin-01-licensed-certifiers.pdf)) will ask for its written evidence list first, then the stock and tuned runs on the same dyno, the emissions result, the turbo and intercooler invoices with the tuner's description of the calibration, a scan showing no faults, a noise test, the brake and driveline evidence, and an installation to VSB 14. A modification plate goes on, Service NSW records it and the insurer is told. The certificate is about AUD 2,000 (est., report 14).

Ford's warranty is 5 years with unlimited kilometres, or 7 years for new and demonstrator trucks whose warranty starts on or after 1 Sep 2026 and that are serviced only at Ford dealers (Ford specification sheet, 29 Oct 2026). Ford says an aftermarket product does not by itself void the warranty, but if it fails or causes a Ford part to fail, the repair is not covered ([Ford Australia](https://www.ford.com.au/support/how-tos/warranty/warranties-and-coverage/do-aftermarket-accessories-affect-the-warranty)). After a tune and a turbo, a failed engine, turbo, 10-speed or transfer case is where a claim would be refused, and the owner pays.

## 8 The power the plan registers

Wheel figures use the conductor's conversion: 250 to 300 hp at the wheels is 186 to 224 kW at the wheels and roughly 220 to 265 kW at the crank, a wheel-to-crank ratio of about 0.845 (est.) through the 10-speed and full-time 4WD.

| Level | Crank (est.) | Wheels (est.) | Parts | Legal path | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock | 154 kW, 600 Nm | 130 kW, 175 hp | None | None | As bought |
| Tune on the factory turbo | About 184 kW, 600 to 650 Nm | 155 kW, 208 hp | Calibration | Items 6 and, at the line, 2 | Ford's own rating for this engine in the ordinary Ranger, so a low-risk step, but it still needs the certificate |
| The plan: PowerMax turbo, PowerMax intercooler, calibration | About 205 kW, 650 Nm | 173 kW, 232 hp | Garrett parts with a maker's record, tuner's calibration | Items 2 and 6, one LA4 certificate | Planned: about 14 % of flow in hand (est.) |
| 250 hp at the wheels, Leonard's option | About 220 kW, 646 Nm | 186 kW, 250 hp | The plan's parts, calibrated about 7 % higher | As the plan, one LA4 certificate | An option to weigh: about 6 % of flow in hand (est.) and no record |
| 300 hp at the wheels | About 265 kW, 723 Nm | 224 kW | A hybrid turbo and injectors not sold here | As above, with a laboratory test likely | Not planned |

The PowerMax's 27 % more flow than the factory turbo carries the ordinary Ranger's 184 kW to about 234 kW of air (est.), taking Ford's turbo as flat out at 184 kW. At 205 kW it keeps about 14 % in hand, and at 220 kW about 6 %. So the plan registers about 205 kW and 650 Nm at the crank, about 232 hp at the wheels (est.), 33 % over the Super Duty's 154 kW and 11 % over the ordinary Ranger's 184 kW, and shows 250 hp at the wheels beside it for Leonard to choose.

250 hp at the wheels needs no part the plan does not buy. 220 kW at 3,250 rpm is 646 Nm, inside the 650 Nm cap, so it is the same turbo, intercooler, swap parts and gauge with a calibration aimed about 7 % higher, at the stage's price (VEHICLE.md, the 250 hp option). Its risks are of degree. For the engine, about 7 % more fuel at full load (est.) means hotter exhaust, the turbo nearer Garrett's 186.2 krpm for longer on a climb with the 4,000 kg trailer, and more soot for the DPF. For the 10-speed, Ford publishes no torque rating, and the option keeps the plan's 650 Nm cap and Ford's 600 Nm in low range, first and reverse, so the gearbox sees the plan's peak torque and only more heat in its fluid on a long climb, which the gauge and TX01's 110 °C mark settle. For the warranty nothing changes: Ford pays for no failure the parts or the tune cause, and a turbo with less in hand is likelier to fail (est.). The certificate is the same LC4 certificate under items 2 and 6, and the emissions test decides the power: the interim NSW procedure is run on the calibration as registered, so if 220 kW fails it with the DPF, SCR and EGR working, the tuner steps back towards 205 kW and the test is run again. The case against it is the 6 % margin, itself an estimate, and that no tuner has published a Super Duty at that power.

300 hp at the wheels stands on its own facts. About 265 kW is past the PowerMax's estimated 234 kW of air, so it needs Falcon's hybrid, which has no Australian stockist and only its maker's claim, and injectors nobody sells for this engine in Australia. Its 723 Nm is 11 % over the plan's own 650 Nm cap, and a heavy-duty calibration that far from Ford's would likely need a laboratory emissions test (AUD 10,000, est.).

The stage keeps every emission control and needs one certificate. The factory turbo and calibration are kept, so a refused certificate takes the truck back to stock. Because the warranty risk is real, the plan fits the stage when Leonard accepts it or when Ford's warranty ends, and the truck tows the 4,000 kg trailer within Ford's ratings at stock power until then.

## 9 Open points and the assumption used

1. The maximum tow ball download is not in the Ford documents read. Assumed 450 kg (est.), 10 % of the 4,500 kg braked rating, until the owner's manual or the hitch's label is read at S0.
2. The kerb weight on each axle is not published. Weigh the truck at S0.
3. Does the DO45Plus tow pin's 32 mm shank fit Ford's 4.5 t hitch hole firmly? Assumed yes, since the BEMM forbids drilling the hitch. If not, a 4.5 t tongue with a 32 mm hole (check line).
4. Will an LC4 certifier accept the interim NSW emissions procedure for a heavy-duty Euro VI calibration? Assumed yes. If not, a laboratory test (AUD 10,000, est.) or stock.
5. Can a tuner write the Super Duty's calibration? Assumed yes, since tuners already calibrate the V6's engine and 10-speed. If none can, the stage waits.
6. Do the Clearview Next Gen towing mirrors fit the Super Duty? Assumed yes, since it shares the Ranger's cab and doors and ARB lists them for the Next Gen Ranger. Confirm by VIN.
7. Is the single cab chassis a non-passenger vehicle for NSW duty? Assumed yes, AUD 3 per AUD 100.
8. Does the Super Duty's heavy-duty Euro VI engine carry the turbo the PowerMax replaces? Garrett lists the PowerMax 930592-5001S for the 2022+ Ranger and Amarok 3.0 V6 diesel as the replacement for Ford's MB3Q-6K682-AB, and no document read on 23 Nov 2026 gives the Super Duty's own turbo. Assumed yes, since the engine is the Ranger's 3.0 L V6 with a heavy-duty calibration. Read the part number from the turbo's tag at S0. If it is another part, the stage falls back to CUT1, the calibration on Ford's turbo.
9. What does the PowerMax turbo cost here? No price is published. Assumed AUD 2,831 (est.), Falcon's hybrid for the same engine at GBP 1,300 before UK VAT, converted at 1.98 with 10 % GST.
