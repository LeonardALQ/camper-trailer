# 07: Tow vehicle, 2013 Porsche Cayenne GTS (958)

## Recommendation

Leave the suspension alone. The owner's car has the optional air suspension with PASM. It already levels itself under towball load and offers two raised levels for rough ground, so a lift adds nothing for towing. Tow at Normal level. Use High Level I (selectable up to 80 km/h) or High Level II (up to 30 km/h) on fire trails, desert tracks and creek crossings. A permanent lift, lift links or a lift module would raise the centre of gravity and the towball, and none of them makes the combination more stable. Above 50 mm the national code requires certification for a car with stability control.

The limits that set the trailer:

- Braked towing capacity: 3,500 kg. So the trailer's ATM is 3,500 kg at most.
- Towball download: 280 kg. Porsche Australia quoted this maximum noseweight for its factory towbar, against 140 kg for European cars, and the owner has confirmed 280 kg for his car. The towbar fitted to it must carry the same rating, because the lower of the two applies.
- GVM 2,840 kg and payload about 755 kg (2,840 minus the 2,085 kg DIN kerb mass). The towball load comes out of that payload.
- Axle limits, read from the car by the owner: front 1,520 kg, rear 1,600 kg. Roof load 100 kg.
- GCM 6,340 kg, read from the car by the owner. It equals the GVM plus the braked rating, so it never binds on its own. The towball load counts in both the car's GVM and the trailer's ATM, so a car at its GVM towing a trailer at its ATM weighs 6,340 kg less the towball load. At the design point (car at its GVM, a 3.2 t trailer, 220 kg on the ball) the combination weighs 5,820 kg.

Towball target for the trailer design: about 220 kg loaded. Above that, the car's 1,600 kg rear axle limit starts to bind, and each extra kilogram on the ball then costs about two kilograms of gear in the car (see the load budget in section 1). Confirm the car's axle loads on a weighbridge.

Shared wheel and tyre: 255/55 R18, load index 109, on the Porsche 8J x 18 ET53 wheel (5x130). Porsche approves this wheel and tyre for the 2011 to 2014 GTS unless the car has ceramic brakes (PCCB). It is a factory size (737.7 mm diameter), so there is no question of legality or speedometer error. It is 2.4% smaller than the current 20-inch tyre, which lowers the gearing slightly. Its 1,030 kg rating covers the trailer's need of about 984 kg per tyre at 3,500 kg ATM with 220 kg on the ball.

The owner's car is registered MA (passenger car), so the tyre is the Yokohama Geolandar A/T G015 255/55R18 109H XL. A passenger car needs a speed rating of at least 180 km/h, and the H rating gives 210 km/h. That rules out the tougher BFGoodrich KO2 light-truck tyre, whose R rating of 170 km/h would be legal only on an MC car.

The owner's calipers are red, the standard steel brakes, so the car does not have PCCB and Porsche's 18-inch approval applies.

Towing tyre pressures on the car: Porsche's full-load figures are 2.6 bar (37 psi) front and 3.2 bar (46 psi) rear, cold.

Gearbox: no factory tow cooler was offered. Fit an ATF temperature readout before the first long tow, because the tuned engine makes more heat than Porsche's rating assumed. Add an auxiliary cooler only if the readings show it is needed.

What to change: confirm the towbar and its rating, fit 18-inch wheels with load-rated all-terrain tyres, add ATF temperature monitoring, check the air suspension's health, carry a spare compressor relay and fuse, and weigh the car.

What to leave alone: the ride height, the air springs, the height sensors and their links, and the factory levels.

### What the towball load does to the car's axles

The towball sits behind the rear axle, so it loads the rear axle by more than its own mass and lifts load off the front axle. With wheelbase L = 2,895 mm (Porsche Australia page above) and rear axle to ball centre a:

- Rear axle gain = P x (L + a) / L
- Front axle loss = P x a / L

The distance a was not published. It is estimated at 1,000 to 1,150 mm from the overall length (4,846 mm) and wheelbase, plus about 100 mm for the ball behind the bumper. That estimate gives:

| Towball load P | Rear axle gain | Front axle loss |
|---|---|---|
| 140 kg | 188 to 196 kg | 48 to 56 kg |
| 250 kg | 336 to 349 kg | 86 to 99 kg |
| 280 kg | 377 to 391 kg | 97 to 111 kg |

The air suspension restores the car's level but not its front axle load, so steering and braking balance still shift towards the rear. The owner should measure a and weigh both axles with the trailer coupled.

### The car's load budget

Two of the car's limits decide how much it can carry while towing: the 2,840 kg GVM and the 1,600 kg rear axle. The table gives the most gear the car can carry on top of two adults (160 kg) and the towbar (about 30 kg, estimate). It assumes the rear seats are out (about 50 kg, estimate) and the gear sits on the floor where they were.

The other assumptions will be replaced by weighbridge figures. The car's front axle is taken to carry 54% of its kerb mass, because no published figure was found. The ball is taken as 1.1 m behind the rear axle, the front seats as 1.15 m behind the front axle and the gear as 2.0 m behind it. Porsche's 2,085 kg DIN kerb mass is for a standard car, and options add to it. Car and Driver weighed its heavily optioned 2013 GTS test car at 5,108 lb, which is 2,317 kg ([Car and Driver](https://www.caranddriver.com/reviews/a15116462/2013-porsche-cayenne-gts-test-review/)). So the table also shows a car at 2,200 kg.

| Towball load | Gear, car at 2,085 kg | Gear, car at 2,200 kg | Limit that binds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 140 kg | 475 kg | 360 kg | GVM |
| 220 kg | 389 kg | 280 kg | Rear axle at 2,085 kg, GVM at 2,200 kg |
| 250 kg | 329 kg | 250 kg | Rear axle at 2,085 kg, GVM at 2,200 kg |
| 280 kg | 269 kg | 193 kg | Rear axle |

If the front axle carries 52% or 56% of the kerb mass instead of 54%, the figures for the 2,085 kg car move by up to about 60 kg either way. The front axle stays well under its 1,520 kg limit in every case.

Two things follow from the geometry:

- The ball sits about 1.1 m behind the rear axle, so each kilogram on the ball puts about 1.4 kg on the rear axle. A kilogram of gear on the floor where the rear seats were puts only about 0.7 kg there. Once the rear axle binds, each extra kilogram on the ball costs about two kilograms of gear.
- Gear in the boot sits over or behind the rear axle and fills it faster. At 220 kg on the ball, the 2,085 kg car carries about 260 kg of gear in the boot (load centred 0.1 m behind the rear axle), against 389 kg on the floor where the rear seats were.

So the trailer design aims for about 220 kg on the ball, which is 6.9% of a 3.2 t loaded trailer. Gear that does not fit the car's budget goes in the trailer, over its axles. There it changes the towball load very little, and a 3.2 t trailer still has about 300 kg in hand under its 3,500 kg ATM. The roof takes 100 kg at most. Anything on the roof counts in the same budget, and it raises the car's centre of gravity, so keep it light when towing.

## 3. Changes for towing and light off-road use (air-sprung car)

### 3.1 How it levels under towball load

The air suspension holds the selected level whatever the load. When the trailer is coupled, the rear air springs take more pressure and the car returns to its set height. So the towball height at Normal level stays nearly constant from an empty trailer to a full one. That makes the trailer easier to design, because the coupling height can be set for one ball height.

Self-levelling is not weight distribution. As section 1 shows, the front axle still loses load. The European-style detachable towbar has no receiver for a weight-distribution hitch, and no Porsche guidance on one was found.

Owners report two settings that help. A Porsche Club GB member tows with the firmer Sport damping "to avoid the porpoise effect of 140kg on the towball" ([Porsche Club GB forum](https://www.porscheclubgb.com/forum/threads/air-suspension-yes-or-no.159429/)). A Rennlist member tows a 6,000 lb camper at Normal level ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1199698-towing-3-questions-for-air-suspended-cars.html)).

### 3.2 Is there a towing mode?

Not in the sense of a changed chassis or gearbox setup. When a trailer is plugged in, the factory trailer control module recognises it. A retrofitted module needs dealer coding. Owners report that it then switches off the rear ParkAssist, Lane Change Assist and auto start-stop, shows the trailer's indicators, monitors trailer lamps, and sounds the alarm if the plug is pulled while the car is locked. They report that the transmission, the suspension levels and the cooling do not change ([Rennlist, tow module thread](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1149824-tow-module-working-2.html)). Whether the 958's stability control adds trailer-sway braking was not confirmed.

Two owner tips are useful. Hold the up button on the height switch to stop the system levelling before jacking the car. The Loading Level (stationary only) lowers the ball about 50 mm, which helps when coupling.

### 3.4 Lift links and lift modules

The control unit holds each level by reading the four height sensors. Adjustable sensor links change the sensor arm geometry, and electronic modules (emulators) change the sensor signal. Either way, the control unit thinks the car is lower than it is, so every level rises by the same amount. An example is AkiOverland's "Adjustable Level Sensor Links for Lifted Air Suspension Vehicles" ([AkiOverland 958 air lift range](https://www.akioverland.com/collections/cayenne-gen-2-958-air-suspension-lift-kits)). The Tecnoshock kit is also sold with an emulator ([Berg Peaks](https://bergpeaks.com/products/tecnoshock-46mm-2-way-air-suspension-lift-kit-for-porsche-cayenne-958-9ya)). Neither listing gives a weight, and no Australian stockist was found.

Effects on towing:

- Stability: no gain. The centre of gravity and the roll centre rise by the lift amount.
- Towball height: rises by the full lift amount at every level. The trailer would then ride nose-up unless its coupling is set for the new height.
- Suspension travel: the struts work closer to full extension, so droop travel shrinks. The upper levels may become unreachable or set faults. A US seller notes that its first-generation lift "will max out the functional range of the suspension level sensors" ([Eurowise](https://shop.eurowise.com/products/ewor9014)).
- Driveline: the same angle changes as a lift kit (see 3.5).
- Reliability: the self-levelling still works, but outside Porsche's calibrated range.

The car already offers +28 mm and +58 mm on demand, so links or modules are not recommended.

### 3.6 Australian rules on lifting

- National code: a car with ESC can be lifted up to 50 mm above the manufacturer's standard height as a basic modification without certification. A suspension lift above 50 mm, or any combination of suspension, tyre and body lifts above that, needs certification under Code LS7 or LS8 ([VSB 14 Section LS, clause 2.6 (archived)](http://web.archive.org/web/20190408024946/https://infrastructure.gov.au/vehicles/vehicle_regulation/bulletin/files/NCOP11_Section_LS_Tyres_Suspension_Steering_Nov_2015_v4.pdf)). The code warns that a lift can upset ESC programming, which is calibrated for the original suspension and tyres. Designs for ESC cars that the vehicle maker has not approved or that are not proven by testing fall outside Code LS3, and lifts over 50 mm need a lane-change test ([Section LS extract, LS3](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0834/9097/files/Section_LS_-_Suspension_Steering.pdf?v=1707707946)).
- NSW: certification is needed if a suspension change alters ride height by more than one third of the working suspension travel, or by more than 50 mm ([TfNSW VSI 06, July 2026, item 13](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-03/vsi-06-light-vehicle-modification-july-2026.pdf)). For an air car the "working travel" figure is not published, so the allowance is unclear.
- Other states follow the national code with their own variations. Check the owner's state.

### 3.7 Does a lift improve towing? No.

A lift helps ground clearance and approach and departure angles. For towing it makes things slightly worse. It raises the centre of gravity, which increases load transfer and body roll. It raises the towball, and on a car already near the top of VSB1's 350 to 460 mm window it could push the ball out of range. It can upset ESC calibration. Taller, softer all-terrain tyres, which usually come with a lift, also lower the car's rear cornering stiffness, and that lowers the speed at which a combination starts to snake.

What actually helps, from a University of Bath test programme on a car and an adjustable trailer: the dominant factors in high-speed stability were the trailer's yaw inertia, its nose mass (towball load) and its axle position. Tyre pressure mattered less. Trailer mass alone did not change high-speed stability much. Both a friction stabiliser and the car's electronic stability program improved stability and delayed the onset of snaking ([Darling, Tilley and Gao, 2009, Proc. IMechE Part D](https://doi.org/10.1243/09544070JAUTO981)). For this project that means:

- Use the towball allowance. A 250 kg download on a 3.2 t trailer is 7.8%. A 140 kg download is 4.4%.
- Keep heavy items near the axles. The bikes already ride over the axles.
- Fit the trailer's electronic sway control (Bosch TSC, report 06). The AL-KO AKS friction coupling is not sold for 3,500 kg trailers in Australia (report 08).
- Inflate the car's tyres to Porsche's full-load pressures.
- Tow at Normal level with firm (Sport) damping.

Steel-spring cars: the equivalent changes would be coil helper airbags or uprated rear springs. They were not researched because the owner's car has air suspension.

### 4.4 Australian rules on tyre size and speed rating

- Diameter: on a passenger car or a derivative, a tyre may be no more than 15 mm larger or 26 mm smaller than any tyre the maker designated for the model. The 50 mm allowance covers only 4WDs designed for off-road use, and the code excludes AWD "soft roaders" even when they are certified MC ([VSB 14 Section LS, clause 4.2.4](http://web.archive.org/web/20190408024946/https://infrastructure.gov.au/vehicles/vehicle_regulation/bulletin/files/NCOP11_Section_LS_Tyres_Suspension_Steering_Nov_2015_v4.pdf)). The 958 has on-demand AWD with no low range, so the safe reading is 15 mm. That makes 770.5 mm the ceiling. 255/55 R19 and 255/60 R18 pass. 265/60 R18 (+19.7 mm) does not.
- NSW sets its certification trigger at a 7% diameter change, or any change that affects speedometer compliance ([TfNSW VSI 06, item 15](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-03/vsi-06-light-vehicle-modification-july-2026.pdf)). 265/60 R18 is a 2.6% change, so NSW would not require certification for diameter alone. A bigger tyre makes the speedometer read low, so check it against GPS.
- Load rating: not less than the lowest load rating on the car's tyre placard (VSB 14 LS clause 4.2.12).
- Speed rating: at least 180 km/h (S) for a passenger vehicle whose placard requires more than S, and at least 140 km/h (N) for "vehicles with special features for off-road use". A lower rating needs the registration authority's agreement (VSB 14 LS clause 4.2.12). NSW's older guide says that if the placard rating is above 180 km/h, a lower rating may be fitted "but not lower than the vehicle's top speed". It also asks for a warning label on the windscreen ([TfNSW VSI 09](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-02/RMS-Guideline-for-alternative-wheels-and-tyres-Vehicle-Standards-Information-No-9-November-2003.pdf)). The GTS's top speed is 261 km/h (Porsche Australia specification page). Read literally, that sentence would rule out every all-terrain tyre in NSW, so confirm with TfNSW.
- ADR category: an MC off-road passenger vehicle has four-wheel drive and at least four of five features at unladen mass: approach angle of at least 28 degrees, breakover angle at least 14 degrees, departure angle at least 20 degrees, running clearance at least 200 mm, and axle or suspension clearance at least 175 mm (definition as quoted on [ClubTouareg](https://www.clubtouareg.com/threads/adr-definition-off-road-pax-vehicle-classification-mc.58040/)). Porsche gives 28.8, 23.4 and 27.2 degrees for the steel-spring GTS (US brochure), which meets three of them. Whether the clearances qualify is not known. The registration papers show the category.

These figures are for Porsche-approved tyres. For an all-terrain tyre, use them as the minimum and check them against the tyre maker's load and inflation table for the weighed axle loads. The trailer: set the pressure from the maker's load and inflation table for the weighed load on each tyre. A starting estimate for a 109 XL tyre carrying close to its rated load is about 290 kPa (42 psi), the usual reference pressure for XL tyres. The sidewall maximum is 50 psi (XL) or 65 psi (LT load range D). Air down for sand and corrugations as usual, and air back up before highway speeds.

## 5. Gearbox cooling

What the factory offered: the towing option was the towbar itself (codes 1D1, 1D2 and 1D3 for removable, detachable and electric versions) and its trailer module. The 958 option code list shows no transmission cooler or uprated cooling option ([option code list](https://forporsche.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Porsche-Equipment-Codes.pdf)). Porsche rated every Tiptronic 958 for 3,500 kg with the standard cooling.

What owners report:

- Trailer mode does not change the cooling. One owner found the grille shutters and thermostats behave the same with a trailer. Engaging Sport mode opened up the cooling system and gave "a significant change in coolant and oil temps" ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1149824-tow-module-working-2.html)).
- On the related VW Touareg, which uses the same 0C8 family of Aisin 8-speeds, owners say the car is rated to tow 3,500 kg with the standard cooler ([ClubTouareg](https://www.clubtouareg.com/threads/transmission-cooler.85236/)). Normal ATF temperatures are about 70 to 90 C, and heavy towing "most likely will push it way above 100 °C" ([ClubTouareg](https://www.clubtouareg.com/threads/transmission-oil-temp.288235/)).
- Owners disagree on the exact sub-model. One post says the S and GTS use a TR-81SD and the Turbo a stronger TR-82SD ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1318428-aisin-tr-80sd-upgrades.html)). The transmission's identification plate will settle it.

The rebuilt, stage 2 engine: the factory rating assumed 309 kW and 515 Nm (Porsche Australia specification page). The same gearbox family carries much more torque in the Cayenne Turbo, so torque capacity is probably not the limit. That is a judgement, because the tune's torque figure is not known. The extra heat is the concern, in both the gearbox and the PTM transfer case clutch. The transfer case is a known weak point on the 958. Porsche ran a workshop campaign on defective clutch baskets in some 2011 cars ([NHTSA copy](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2012/MC-10109297-9999.pdf)) and issued a bulletin to convert stocked transfer cases to a new type for 2011 to 2017 S, GTS and Turbo cars ([NHTSA copy](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2016/MC-10119330-9999.pdf)).

Recommendation:

1. Before the first long tow, fit a way to read ATF temperature from the transmission control unit (an OBD tool or gauge that reads it).
2. Do a test tow at about 3.2 t on a long climb in summer. Log ATF and coolant temperatures.
3. Tow in Sport, or hold a gear with the Tiptronic paddles on long climbs, so the gearbox does not hunt between gears. The owner report above says Sport also runs the cooling harder.
4. If ATF runs well above 100 C for long periods, add an auxiliary air-to-oil cooler after the factory heat exchanger. Have a transmission specialist plan it.
5. Shorten the ATF and transfer case fluid service intervals. Confirm the transfer case is the updated type.
