# 04 Bed above the bikes, and build materials for the bed and garage

Note added 20 Nov 2026, for the LandCruiser 79: the bed above the bikes is superseded. The bikes ride in the ute's bike box (UTE-GARAGE.md), and the trailer has a queen bed across its front on a honeycomb base (TRAILER.md revision H, BODY.md). The materials data in this report still feeds some mass lines.

Status: complete research draft. Weights marked "estimate" or "calculated" show their basis; everything else comes from the cited page. Links that blocked automated checking are listed under "Could not verify". The owner's bike figures from BRIEF.md are used throughout (Tuareg 660 top at 1,370 mm).

## 1. Geometry and orientation

The platform underside sits at about 1,420 mm (Tuareg 1,370 mm plus 50 mm). The collapsed-roof ceiling is at 1,800 mm, so 380 mm is available for structure, deck, underlay, mattress and bedding.

**Across the trailer (recommended).** The mattress's 2,030 mm length runs along the 2,330 mm interior width, leaving about 300 mm spare across the width (450 mm with a 1,880 mm caravan queen) for the ladder landing or a shelf. The mattress's 1,530 mm width runs along the trailer, so it fits the shortest garage (1,600 mm). The front and rear garage partitions run across the trailer, so they can carry the platform and the beams span only the garage length (1,600 to 2,000 mm). The side wall with the bike door carries no bed load. Toy-hauler queen lifts use the same arrangement, with the bed across the unit between rails on the side walls.

**Along the trailer.** The 2,030 mm length runs along the trailer, so the garage must be at least about 2,050 mm long (about 1,900 mm with a 1,880 mm caravan queen), which works against the 5,000 mm body target. One long edge falls on the door wall and the other in mid-trailer, so it needs a beam spanning the garage plus a support at mid-trailer. It leaves an 800 mm strip beside the bed. Use it only if the garage ends up 2,000 mm long anyway, or if the bed is allowed to overhang the living-area partition.

**The platform is also the garage ceiling.** For fuel smell and dirt separation (section 9), the deck should cover the whole garage plan (garage length x 2,330 mm) and be sealed to the partitions and walls. The living-area partition then only rises to the deck underside (about 1,420 mm), the bed space above the deck opens to the living area, and the ladder stands in the living area. All weight estimates below use a 1,600 x 2,330 mm platform (3.73 m2) unless stated.

### Condensation and mould

The bed sits over an unheated garage. Overnight the deck is close to garage air temperature, so moisture from two sleepers can condense on the deck under the mattress, which is where caravan mattresses go mouldy ([Aero-Mesh](https://www.aero-mesh.com.au/post/the-benefits-of-a-condensation-prevention-mat) describes the same failure). Three things reduce it: a ventilated layer under the mattress (section 3), a deck that is not colder than the room (section 4), and a mattress with a breathable, zip-off washable cover and no sealed vinyl base. Pure Sleep lists an anti-microbial finish on some covers ([Lunar](https://puresleep.com.au/product/lunar-caravan-mattress/)). No maker found publishes mould-test data for these mattresses.

**Recommendation:** a layered foam mattress with an HR base and a latex comfort layer, about 130 to 150 mm, full queen 1,530 x 2,030, made to order in Australia (Pure Sleep Lyra Firm, or the same build in 150 mm). Planning weight 19 kg (estimate, confirm with the maker). If the owner wants springs, the Orion Caravan Deluxe at 180 mm and 25.5 kg still fits under 1,800 mm (section 10).

## 6. Fixings, joining and isolation

The approach is bond plus mechanical fixing. An elastic polyurethane bond carries distributed load, seals, and absorbs vibration and the different thermal expansion of aluminium and composite. Rivets or bolts at every load point hold the joint if a bond is poorly made, and let the deck come out for service. Wherever stainless steel touches aluminium, or aluminium touches the galvanised steel chassis or body frame, the joint is isolated.

Reliability and service. HappiJac is the long-standing toy-hauler lift. Its manual lists field fixes (limit switches, control module and brake are replaceable, and there is a manual brake release and crank with a 1/2 in socket), and Lippert states that motor failures are extremely rare ([owner's manual](https://forestriverinc.com/files/component-manuals/Interior%20Equipment/Lippert%20-%20Bed%20Lift%20Model%20HappiJac%20Owner's%20Manual.pdf)). The bed must be fully raised and pinned for towing, and nothing but bedding may be on it when it moves. The Smart Bed has a hand crank for emergencies, and its belts and guides are sold as spares, but only through European dealers.

What a lift changes here:

- A moving bed cannot seal the garage ceiling, so the bed space shares air with the bikes. If fuel smell matters (section 9), a lift works against it.
- The lift only helps when the bikes are out: the bed can then drop to sofa height. With bikes in, the bed stays at the top.
- HappiJac rails sit at the four bed corners on the side walls, so the bike door must fit between two rails, and both side walls need backing at the rails.
- The Smart Bed's 60 kg moving limit is close to the recommended bed (frame, deck, underlay and mattress about 51 kg, plus bedding).

**Recommendation:** build the fixed platform now. If the owner wants a lift later, the Smart Bed is the lighter fit (8.6 kg plus a frame); HappiJac has toy-hauler history and some parts sold in Australia. Leave rivet-nut hardpoints in the side walls at the corner positions so either can be added.

## Open questions

1. Garage length (1,600 to 2,000 mm) and the bike door's width and position. These set the platform area, beam span, partition positions and whether lift rails could ever fit beside the door.
2. Sleeper weights and the design load case (static, kneeling point load, towing with the bed loaded) so the designer or engineer can confirm the RHS 50x25x2.5 beams and the 100 kg ladder rating.
3. Must the garage be sealed from the sleeping space for fuel smell? If yes, rule out a bed lift; if a lift is wanted, accept shared air and add garage extraction.
4. PanelMate: can they make a 15 to 20 mm glass-skinned or 0.5 mm aluminium-skinned honeycomb panel, and what does it weigh per m2? If not, import PCS Alustep 500 (CEL Components, Italy).
5. Pure Sleep: the actual weight of the Lyra in full queen, and whether they will build it at 150 mm.
6. Froli and HyperVent have no Australian stockist found. Is importing acceptable, or should Aero-Mesh be tested (ask Aero-Mesh for its weight per m2)?
7. How the bed guard attaches to the raised roof structure (depends on the deferred roof design).
8. Garage ventilation, floor drainage and fuel-vapour management are outside this report but decide how well the partitions work.
9. Will the engineer accept bonded-plus-riveted joints, and 6060-T5 for ledgers and cleats?
