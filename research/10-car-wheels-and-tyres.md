# 10: The car's own wheels and tyres for towing (2013 Cayenne GTS, 958)

Note added 20 Nov 2026. The Porsche Cayenne GTS is no longer the tow vehicle, so its own wheels and tyres no longer matter to the plan. The LandCruiser 79 and the trailer now share one wheel and tyre, the ROH Trak 2 16 x 7 ET 0 with LT265/75R16 123/120 (report 16 section 4), with one spare on each. Reports 14 to 16 and VEHICLE.md replace this report, which stays as the record of the Cayenne study.

This report builds on [report 07](07-tow-vehicle.md), section 4. The car no longer shares a wheel with the trailer, so this report picks the car's setup on its own merits. The trailer's wheels are in report 09.

## Recommendation

For a car registered in NSW, use one set of wheels for everything: the factory 20-inch wheels with a Y-rated SUV road tyre. In NSW no all-terrain tyre is both legal on this car and sold in Australia, so a second set of smaller wheels would still carry a road tyre. That set would gain 16.5 mm of sidewall and lose some highway stability, load rating and legal certainty. That trade is not worth a second set.

- Wheel: Porsche 20-inch RS Spyder, 9.5J x 20 ET47, part 958.362.151.00. This is the wheel already on the car. Porsche approves it for the 2011 to 2014 GTS with 275/45 R20 110Y XL ([Porsche Technical Information 06/18, item J and page 13](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10134873-9999.pdf)).
- Tyre: Michelin Pilot Sport 4 SUV 275/45 R20 110Y XL. The load index 110 means 1,060 kg per tyre. The Y rating means 300 km/h. Michelin Australia lists this size ([Michelin Australia, Pilot Sport 4 SUV](https://www.michelin.com.au/auto/tyres/michelin-pilot-sport-4-suv)). Its tread is 7.5 mm (9.5/32 in) deep and it weighs 15.3 kg (US data, [tiresize.com](https://tiresize.com/tires/Michelin/Pilot-Sport-4-SUV.htm)).
- Legal in NSW: yes. It is the factory size, its 300 km/h rating is above the car's 261 km/h top speed, and its load index equals the original tyre's ([TfNSW VSI 09 Rev 5](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-02/RMS-Guideline-for-alternative-wheels-and-tyres-Vehicle-Standards-Information-No-9-November-2003.pdf), pages 4 and 5).
- Towing pressures at full load, cold: 2.6 bar (37 psi) front and 3.2 bar (46 psi) rear ([Porsche TI 06/18, page 4](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10134873-9999.pdf)). At the 1,600 kg rear axle limit each rear tyre carries 800 kg, which is 75% of its 1,060 kg rating. On gravel and corrugations, drop to about 2.2 bar (32 psi) front and 2.6 bar (38 psi) rear, at reduced speed (estimate, not a Porsche figure). Do not go much lower on a 124 mm sidewall. Pump back up before highway speeds.
- Second set: none in NSW. If the car is registered in Victoria instead, add an 18-inch dirt set (described below). That option is clearly tougher off the bitumen.
- Spare: a sixth 20-inch RS Spyder wheel with the same tyre, about 30 kg (15 kg wheel, estimate, plus 15.3 kg tyre). The 958 has no well that takes a full-size wheel. The spare lies flat on the boot floor, where it loads the rear axle by about 31 kg. When towing, that costs about 45 kg of the car's gear allowance (details in "The spare" below). When the trailer is coupled, the car's spare could ride in the trailer over its axles instead.

The Victorian alternative, if the owner registers there:

- Road set as above for highway trips.
- Dirt set: Porsche 8J x 18 ET53 wheels (958.362.136.00.01, 958.362.136.20, 958.362.144.00/.01 or 958.362.144.20) with BFGoodrich All-Terrain T/A KO3 LT255/55R18 109/105S. BFGoodrich Australia lists this size ([BFGoodrich Australia, KO3](https://www.bfgoodrich.com.au/auto/tyres/all-terrain-t-a-ko3)). It is a light-truck (LT) tyre, load range D, with 11.9 mm (15/32 in) of tread and a 65 psi maximum pressure. It weighs 21.8 kg ([tiresize.com](https://tiresize.com/tires/BFGoodrich/All-Terrain-TA-KO3.htm)). Its S rating (180 km/h) meets the national minimum that Victoria applies, but not NSW's rule.
- Against the road set it has 58% more tread depth (11.9 against 7.5 mm), 16.5 mm more sidewall (140 against 124 mm) and light-truck construction. It costs 6.5 kg more per corner, a 2.4% smaller diameter and less highway stability.
- It needs its own 18-inch spare, about 36 kg (14.1 kg wheel, owner report, plus 21.8 kg tyre).

One thing blocks the 18-inch set in either state: the car's tyre placard. The 18-inch tyre has load index 109 (1,030 kg), below the original 110. NSW wants a replacement tyre's load rating to be at least the original tyre's, and the national code wants at least the lowest rating on the placard. So the 18-inch set is legal without certification only if the placard lists 255/55 R18 109. The owner should read the placard (glovebox, door pillar or fuel flap) before buying anything 18-inch.

## Highway towing stability

Report 07's finding holds. Among Porsche's approved sizes, the stock 275/45 R20 gives the rear axle the most lateral stiffness. It has the widest tread and the shortest sidewall, and it carries the highest load rating of the approved sizes.

| Approved size | Sidewall height | Width | Load index | Diameter | Against stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 275/45 R20 110 on 9.5J x 20 ET47 | 123.8 mm | 275 mm | 110 (1,060 kg) | 755.5 mm | Stock |
| 265/50 R19 110 on 8.5J x 19 ET59 | 132.5 mm | 265 mm | 110 (1,060 kg) | 747.6 mm | -7.9 mm (-1.0%) |
| 255/55 R18 109 on 8J x 18 ET53 | 140.3 mm | 255 mm | 109 (1,030 kg) | 737.7 mm | -17.8 mm (-2.4%) |

Sizes and approvals are from [Porsche TI 06/18](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10134873-9999.pdf). Sidewall heights and diameters are calculated from the size.

No tyre maker publishes cornering stiffness, so the ranking is an engineering judgement. A shorter, wider sidewall flexes less under side load, and the rear tyres' cornering stiffness is one of the things that holds a car and trailer straight. The University of Bath trailer study found the trailer's yaw inertia, towball load and axle position matter more than the car's tyre pressure ([Darling, Tilley and Gao, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1243/09544070JAUTO981)). So the tyre choice is a second-order effect. The trailer's design and the full-load pressures matter more.

Within the 20-inch size, the tyre choice is between road tyres, because NSW requires at least a W rating (see the rules section). A summer SUV tyre such as the Pilot Sport 4 SUV has a stiffer construction than an all-season crossover tyre, which is why it is the first choice for towing stability. That is also a judgement, not a measured figure.

What the 20-inch costs off the bitumen:

- A 124 mm sidewall leaves little room to air down for sand. The rim is closer to sharp rocks and pothole edges on corrugated roads.
- A summer compound with a treadwear grade of 220 chips faster on sharp gravel than a harder compound graded 520 or more.
- No sidewall protection beyond Michelin's rim protector rib (marked FRV on Michelin's size list).

The Yokohama Geolandar X-CV G057 275/45 R20 110W XL is the fallback if gravel chips the Michelin. It is NSW-legal (W is 270 km/h), Yokohama Australia lists it, and it has 8.7 mm (11/32 in) of tread and a treadwear grade of 520 ([Yokohama Australia, X-CV](https://www.yokohama.com.au/our-range/tyres/geolandar-x-cv-g057), [tiresize.com](https://tiresize.com/tires/Yokohama/Geolandar-X-CV.htm)). It is an all-season crossover tyre, so expect slightly softer handling than the Michelin.

Why not the P Zero N0 as first choice: it has the shallowest tread (6.3 mm) and is a sports-car summer tyre. If a Porsche Centre confirms its N0 approval for the 2011 to 2014 GTS, it is the Porsche-coded option for highway towing, with less margin on gravel.

## Height

The air suspension's height sensors measure the body against the suspension arms, not against the ground. So a smaller tyre lowers the whole car, and the towball, by the change in tyre radius.

| Set | Diameter | Change in radius, ride height and towball height |
|---|---|---|
| 275/45 R20 (stock) | 755.5 mm | 0 |
| 265/50 R19 | 747.6 mm | -4.0 mm |
| 255/55 R18 | 737.7 mm | -8.9 mm |
| LT255/55R18 KO3 | 738 mm (maker's figure) | -8.8 mm |

The 18-inch sets also make the speedometer read 2.4% higher than the stock tyre does. That is the legal direction, because a speedometer may read high but not low. The trailer's coupling height should be set for the 20-inch tyre. On the 18-inch set the trailer will sit about 9 mm lower at the front.

## The spare

- The 958 has no well for a full-size spare. Porsche offered space-saver spares in 18, 19 and 20 inches (option codes 1G1, 1G4, 1G6), a tyre repair kit (1G8), and a "Full size spare wheel placed in trunk" (3F4) ([958 option code list, third-party compilation](https://forporsche.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Porsche-Equipment-Codes.pdf)). The vehicle data label shows which one the owner's car came with.
- Owners report that the factory mount on the right rear of the boot is made for the collapsible spare, and a full-size wheel is too tall to stand there. They carry it face down on the boot floor and strap it to the tie-downs ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1313024-full-size-spare-for-off-road-fun.html), [Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1486230-first-cayenne-took-kit-question.html)).
- Mass: the 20-inch spare is about 30 kg. The wheel mass is an estimate of about 15 kg, from an owner's 15.3 kg weighing of the earlier 9.5J x 20 RS Spyder ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-955-957-2003-2010/973791-9-5-rs-spyder-wheels.html)). The tyre is 15.3 kg. An 18-inch spare with the KO3 is about 36 kg (14.1 kg wheel, owner report on [Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-958-2011-2018/1215524-wheel-weight.html), plus 21.8 kg tyre).
- Effect on the rear axle: lying flat on the boot floor, the spare's centre sits about 0.1 m behind the rear axle (estimate, same as report 07's boot load). With the 2,895 mm wheelbase, about 1.03 kg of each kilogram lands on the rear axle. So a 30 kg spare adds about 31 kg to the rear axle. When towing at 220 kg on the ball, the rear axle is the limit, and gear on the floor where the rear seats were puts about 0.69 kg per kilogram on it. So the spare costs about 45 kg of gear (31 / 0.69). If the kerb mass already includes a factory space-saver or repair kit, subtract that item's mass.
- Option: when the trailer is coupled, carry the car's spare in the trailer over its axles. There it changes the towball load very little and frees the car's rear axle. Report 09 sets where the trailer's own spare goes.
- Keep the spare the same size as the wheels fitted. The 958's transfer case shares drive between the axles, and owners of earlier Cayennes report transfer case warnings when one tyre's rolling diameter differs by about 3% ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/cayenne-955-957-2003-2010/1148428-collapsible-spare-question-for-my-06-turbo-s-please-help-me-figure-this-out.html)). Porsche also says new and worn tyres on one axle should not differ in tread depth by more than 30% ([TI 06/18, page 18](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10134873-9999.pdf)), so rotate the spare into the set at tyre rotations.

## NSW and Victoria rules

Speed ratings used below: S is 180 km/h, T 190, H 210, V 240, W 270, Y 300.

### NSW

TfNSW's Vehicle Standards Information No. 9 (VSI 09), Rev 5, dated 28 February 2019, is the current wheels and tyres guide. NSW's light vehicle modification guide of 1 July 2026 still points to it ([TfNSW VSI 06 Rev 4](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-03/vsi-06-light-vehicle-modification-july-2026.pdf), item 16). The wording, re-read from TfNSW's own copy ([VSI 09 Rev 5](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2021-02/RMS-Guideline-for-alternative-wheels-and-tyres-Vehicle-Standards-Information-No-9-November-2003.pdf), page 5):

- The speed rating should be equal to or higher than the original tyres'.
- If the specified rating is above 180 km/h, a lower rating may be fitted, "but not lower than the vehicle's top speed".
- If the replacement's rating is below the placard's, a warning label (at least 40 x 40 mm, orange with black letters) is recommended inside the windscreen.

The warning label does not open a path below the top speed. It applies only to tyres between the top speed and the placard rating. The GTS's top speed is 261 km/h (Porsche Australia, report 07), so the car needs W (270 km/h) or Y (300 km/h). An H-rated or V-rated tyre is not covered by VSI 09. This is stricter than Porsche itself: Porsche approves V-rated (240 km/h) all-season and winter tyres for the GTS in every size ([TI 06/18, items B, E and H](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2018/MC-10134873-9999.pdf)), and those would not meet VSI 09 either. VSI 09 says a vehicle outside its guidelines can be fined and given a defect notice, or fail its safety inspection (page 1).

The only route to a lower rating in NSW is the national code's: the registration authority may agree to it ([VSB 14 Section LS, clause 4.2.12](http://web.archive.org/web/20190408024946/https://infrastructure.gov.au/vehicles/vehicle_regulation/bulletin/files/NCOP11_Section_LS_Tyres_Suspension_Steering_Nov_2015_v4.pdf)). That means asking TfNSW in writing.

Other NSW rules that touch the candidates:

- Load rating: replacement tyres must have a load rating equal to or higher than the original tyres' (VSI 09, page 4). VSI 06 item 17 requires certification if the wheel and tyre do not meet the maker's minimum load on the placard. The original is 110, so 255/55 R18 109 needs the placard to list it.
- Rim width: wheels must not be narrower than the narrowest the maker fitted "for the particular model" (VSI 09, page 2). Porsche fitted 8J x 18 to the base 958 Cayenne, but the GTS came on 9.5J x 20. If TfNSW reads "model" as the GTS, the 8J and 8.5J wheels fall short. A placard that lists the 18-inch size settles this too.
- Diameter: certification is needed above a 7% change from the largest size on the placard ([VSI 06, item 15](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-03/vsi-06-light-vehicle-modification-july-2026.pdf)). All candidates are within 2.4%.
- Width: certification is needed above 25.4 mm wider than the largest rim or tyre on the placard (VSI 06, item 16). All candidates are the same or narrower.

### Victoria

Victoria's guide asks only for "a load and speed rating equal to or better than that required by the standards" ([VicRoads VSI 8, October 2011, third-party copy](https://vehiclecompliance.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/VSI8-Guide-to-modifications-for-motor-vehicles.pdf)). The national standard is at least 180 km/h (S) for a passenger car whose placard requires more than S, and a load rating not below the lowest on the placard (VSB 14 LS clause 4.2.12). So an H-rated G015 or an S-rated KO3 LT tyre is legal on this MA car in Victoria. The R-rated KO2 (170 km/h) is not. The 255/55 R18 109 still needs the placard to list a 109 tyre.

How much better the Victorian option is: the KO3 dirt set gives 58% more tread than the NSW road tyre (11.9 against 7.5 mm), a light-truck carcass, and 16.5 mm more sidewall to air down on sand and corrugations. That is a clear gain off the bitumen. On the highway it is worse than the 20-inch road set, which is why it would be a second set and not the only one. Fit a speed warning label for 180 km/h either way.

KO3 pressures for towing: an LT tyre carries less load at a given pressure than an XL passenger tyre. Expect to need about 3.4 to 3.8 bar (50 to 55 psi) rear to carry 800 kg (estimate). Read the figure from BFGoodrich's load and inflation table, and never go below Porsche's 3.2 bar (46 psi) rear.

## Open questions

1. What does the tyre placard on the owner's car list? If it lists 255/55 R18 109, the 18-inch set needs no certification in either state. If it lists only 275/45 R20 110, the 18-inch set needs certification or TfNSW approval.
2. Which spare option code does the data label show (1G1, 1G4, 1G6, 1G8 or 3F4)? That tells whether the kerb mass already includes a spare and how much.
3. Does Porsche Cars Australia currently list an N-coded 275/45 R20 110Y tyre for the 2011 to 2014 GTS? If so, it is the Porsche-approved alternative to the Pilot Sport 4 SUV.
4. Would TfNSW accept an H- or S-rated all-terrain tyre with a warning label on this car? Only a written answer settles that. If yes, the Victorian dirt set becomes available in NSW.
5. Is the 2013 GTS's tuned top speed relevant to TfNSW? This report uses Porsche's 261 km/h, which already needs W or better.
