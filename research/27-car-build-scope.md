# 27 The car build's scope, the injectors, the driveline for level B, and the trailer's weld evidence

Research for decisions H24 and H26, 22 Nov 2026. Prices in AUD including GST, read on 22 Nov 2026 unless the line says otherwise. `VEHICLE.md`, `data/vehicle/vehicle-parts.yaml`, `TRAILER.md` and `data/trailer-parts.yaml` carry the results.

## Findings

1. The DPF 1VD-FTV runs Denso piezo injectors. The only upgraded injector sold for it in Australia is GTurbo's Pro 100, with 100 % more flow. GTurbo publishes builds of 280 to 330 kW at the wheels on it, but this research found no independent record. Level B, about 170 to 185 kW at the wheels, sits under the 200 to 230 kW where GTurbo says the factory injectors start to restrict, so the upgrade buys margin rather than power. The best path is GTurbo's service of the truck's own injectors, AUD 4,890, fitted with the tune at S6. Their main risk is the LC4 certifier asking for a laboratory emissions test, and if it does, the build drops them and runs level B on the factory injectors.
2. The supply pump needs no upgrade for the Pro 100. GTurbo's 400 hp package includes no pump, and larger injectors reach the same fuel at lower rail pressure.
3. The truck's own H152F gearbox carries level B if it passes its S0 check. Torque is not its known weak point, worn synchros are, and the NPC 1300 clutch is the only driveline part GTurbo lists for its packages, which put more torque than level B through the factory box.
4. A level B rebuild needs nothing beyond factory specification. No head stud kit exists for the 1VD, and GTurbo's test truck ran 80,000 km at 70 % or more over factory power.
5. The 180 L tank and the gearbox temperature gauge fail Leonard's test. The factory 130 L tank covers the longest sealed-highway gap with about 200 km in hand, and no report of the manual gearbox overheating when towing was found. Both become optional extras.
6. Neither VSB1, the HVAIS inspection nor the concessional RAV entry asks for a qualified welder, a weld certificate or crack tests on a home-built trailer. The engineer's calculation that gives the RAV entry its drawbar evidence does assume welds made to AS/NZS 1554.1's SP category, so the plan prices Leonard's welder qualification test and his own dye-penetrant tests, AUD 1,144 before contingency.

## 1. Upgraded injectors for the DPF 1VD-FTV

Which injectors. Toyota fitted piezo injectors with the DPF from October 2016, and the earlier engines have solenoid injectors inside the rocker covers (report 14 section 1.1). So a 2017 to 2022 VDJ79 has the piezo type.

What exists. GTurbo offers two high-flow injectors for the 1VD: a +95 % type for the pre-DPF engine only and the Pro 100, +100 %, for the DPF engine only ([GTurbo booklet](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0823/4241/2531/files/1VD-FTV-VDJ70-Booklet-v1_4c.pdf) p. 24). No other Australian high-flow injector for the DPF engine turned up in this search. For the DPF engine GTurbo sells it four ways, read from its product pages on 22 Nov 2026:

| Option | AUD | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Pro 100 nozzle kit only, for a local fuel shop to fit | 2,790 (the page showed a sale price of 2,640) | [GTurbo](https://gturbo.com.au/products/toyota-vdj70-200-dpf-100-high-flow-injector-nozzles) |
| GTurbo services and high-flows the owner's eight injectors, SKU F I 1VDFTV S2-1, 2 to 5 business days, 12-month warranty on the nozzles | 4,890 | [GTurbo](https://gturbo.com.au/products/toyota-1vd-ftv-dpf-injector-service-and-upgrade-exchange-service) |
| New Toyota piezo injectors, high-flowed by GTurbo | 10,890 (the booklet listed 11,900) | [GTurbo](https://gturbo.com.au/products/toyota-1vd-ftv-dpf-injectors) |
| New Toyota piezo injectors, standard flow | 9,256 | Same page |

The exchange programme covers only the pre-DPF type (booklet p. 25).

Record. GTurbo says its 1VD high-flow injectors have carried builds of 280 to 330 kW and over 1,000 Nm at the wheels with matching parts, and that they suit targets as low as 200 hp and 550 Nm at the wheels, because the tuner sets the fuel (product page, booklet p. 24). Its long record on the 1VD is its turbo system: a test truck at 70 % or more over factory power for over 80,000 km, and three seasons of WA off-road racing from 2015 in a VDJ79 (booklet pp. 9 to 11). Those predate the DPF engine. This research found no owner's or independent report on the Pro 100.

Does level B need them? GTurbo finds the factory injectors become a real restriction at about 200 to 230 kW and 800 Nm at the wheels (booklet p. 41). Level B is about 230 to 250 kW at the crank, about 170 to 185 kW at the wheels (report 14 section 3.9), so the factory flow carries it with roughly 10 to 25 % in hand, and report 14 put it near their limit. The upgrade gives the tuner margin and lets the same fuel go in at lower rail pressure.

Best path. No upgraded injector for this engine has a record this research could check, so the choice rests on the maker. The Pro 100 is the only one sold in Australia, by the firm with the longest Australian record on the 1VD, and GTurbo services it in Balcatta, WA. The plan takes GTurbo's service of the truck's own eight injectors (FS01, AUD 4,890). The parts that wear in a piezo injector are the nozzle, which the service replaces, and the control valve, whose wear the S0 back-leak test measures (VEH-04). So a set that passes S0 comes back with new nozzles on proven bodies, for AUD 6,000 less than the new high-flowed set. A failed injector is replaced by a new genuine one before the run-in (FS06, AUD 1,157 each, est.), and it goes to GTurbo with the others. If Leonard prefers Toyota's record over the margin, the truck's own tested injectors carry level B at factory flow, and the build drops FS01 and FS07, AUD 5,080.

What the tune needs. GTurbo says the high-flow injectors are fully ECU-controlled and the tuner sets them to the target (booklet p. 24). The factory calibration is not set for nozzles with twice the flow. Assumption: it cannot run them, so they go in with the tune at S6, the 1,000 km run-in after the rebuild stays on the truck's own injectors, and the stock dyno run is made before the injectors go to GTurbo. If the LC4 certifier refuses the tune, the engine returns to the factory file on stock-flow nozzles. Assumption: GTurbo can return the old nozzles with the service. If not, a standard set is AUD 9,256.

What the supply pump needs. Nothing new. GTurbo's top single-turbo package, 400 hp and 1,100 Nm at the wheels, includes high-flow injectors but no pump, so it runs the factory pump type, and GTurbo explains that larger injectors deliver the fuel at lower rail pressure and so ease the pump (booklet pp. 24 and 39). The plan's new HP4 pump moves to the check list and is replaced only if S0 shows it worn (FS03). GTurbo's AUD 180 rail relief blank is for high-power builds (report 14 section 3.6), not level B.

What the law needs. TfNSW's [VSI 06 Rev 4](https://www.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/noindex/2026-03/vsi-06-light-vehicle-modification-july-2026.pdf) lists non-original or non-equivalent fuel injection equipment under item 5 and fuel system changes under item 49 as needing certification, beside item 6 for the reflash and item 2 for a power rise of more than 20 %. The Pro 100 injectors therefore go into the same LC4 certificate as the tune (TL05), with GTurbo's invoice and flow sheet in the evidence. Assumption: no extra fee.

The certificate's emissions evidence is the harder part. A modified vehicle must still meet ADR 79/04, and VSCCS Bulletin 2 accepts an IM240 test at TfNSW's emission testing facility or an equivalent NATA facility as the evidence ([Bulletin 2, October 2017](https://nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2023-01/VSCCS%20Bulletin%2002%20Modified%20_%20Individually%20Constructed%20Vehicles%20Oct2017.pdf)). Its 2024 revision adds the gazetted Interim NSW Vehicle Emissions Test Procedure as an alternative to ADR 79/04 (VSCCS Notice 19, seen as a search extract because the server refused the download). The scheme leaves the choice of tests to the certifier ([TfNSW's guide to becoming a certifier](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/system/files/media/documents/2022/vsccs-becoming-a-licensed-certifier.pdf)). Report 14 section 4.5 judged that high-flow injectors, as non-original fuel injection equipment and an explicit item 5 example, make laboratory emissions evidence likely. No published account of an LC4 certifier's evidence list for non-original injectors on a diesel was found.

Assumption used: the LC4 certifier accepts the TfNSW IM240 test of the finished truck with the clean DPF (TL04), with GTurbo's flow sheet for the matched set, and asks for no laboratory test. Fallback: if the certifier's written evidence list at S0 asks for a laboratory test with the Pro 100, the build drops FS01 and FS07 (AUD 5,080) and runs level B on the truck's own tested injectors, which carry it at factory flow with about 10 to 25 % in hand, and the certifier is asked for the list again for the tune alone. TL07, the AUD 10,000 (est.) laboratory test, stays on the check list for the case where even the tune alone needs one. So the laboratory test is the Pro 100's main risk, at 13 % of the build, and the fallback costs no more than the injectors' margin.

## 2. The gearbox and the clutch at level B

Level B is about 850 Nm at the crank (report 14). GTurbo finds the factory clutch slips beyond about 500 Nm (booklet p. 23), so the NPC Super Heavy Duty Organic 1300 kit stays in the build (CG01), with the bearings, bolts and input shaft seal fitted with it (CG06 to CG08).

Toyota publishes no torque rating for the H15x gearboxes (report 15). The evidence on torque is indirect but consistent. GTurbo's power packages for the VDJ70, which has only the manual box, give 850 Nm at the wheels for its Explorer package, 950 Nm for the Adventurer and 1,100 Nm for the Warrior, and the only driveline part it lists for them is the NPC clutch (booklet pp. 33 to 41). HD Automotive calls the H150, H151 and H152 tough and alike in strength (report 15). The faults owners report are worn synchros, mostly between second and third, which Unsealed 4X4 blames on high kilometres, heavy towing and the wrong oil, and it rates them as a medium-common, costly fault ([Unsealed 4X4](https://unsealed4x4.com.au/toyota-landcruiser-70-series-common-problems/)).

So the truck's own H152F carries level B if its synchros and bearings are sound, and the S0 check finds worn ones: crunch on a quick second to third, jumping out of gear on the overrun, and metal on the drain plug magnet (VEH-09, CG09). A new H152F, AUD 3,840, is bought only if it fails. The shock loads in low range, first and reverse stay the risk, which the tune's 450 to 500 Nm cap in those gears limits. The GL-4 75W-90 oil rule stays (RF11).

## 3. Does a level B rebuild need more than factory specification?

No, on the evidence found. Report 14 found no head stud kit for the 1VD, so every level uses new genuine torque-to-yield head bolts and correctly graded gaskets (report 14 section 2.1). GTurbo's development truck ran more than 80,000 km of high-load use at 70 % or more over factory power without a reliability problem, and its customers run over 365 kW at the wheels (booklet p. 9). Level B is 52 to 66 % over the factory 151 kW. The supporting parts GTurbo lists before a turbo upgrade are the clutch, a front-mount intercooler or fan kit, a 3 inch exhaust, the airbox, high-flow injectors and the power pipe, and no internal engine part (booklet p. 41). The rebuild is for 400,000 km of wear, so it stays at factory specification: the NVP kit's cast pistons, rings, bearings, graded gaskets and new head bolts (EN01), with the machine work report 14 planned.

## 4. The 180 L tank and the gearbox temperature gauge

Leonard's test is whether a part is an upgrade the plan needs or a replacement of something that may still work.

The tank. Report 25 puts towing fuel at about 21.3 L/100 km at 90 km/h behind the rounded-front trailer. With 10 % kept, the factory 130 L tank gives about 550 km between fills and the 180 L tank about 760 km, or about 470 and 650 km at the report's cautious 25 L/100 km. The longest gaps on Australia's sealed outback highways are about 274 km, Barkly Homestead to Camooweal ([Rita's Outback Guide](https://www.ritas-outback-guide.com/campervan-in-outback.html)), with the roadhouse about 220 km from Tennant Creek and 240 km from Camooweal ([Discover Central Australia](https://www.discovercentralaustralia.com/barkly-highway-petrol)). The Stuart Highway has fuel at about 200 km intervals ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuart_Highway)), and the Red Centre loop's longest gap is 234 km ([Discover Central Australia](https://www.discovercentralaustralia.com/visitor-information/fuel-in-central-australia)). So the 130 L tank keeps about 200 km in hand on the worst sealed gap even at 25 L/100 km. The 180 L tank replaces a working tank for range the plan's mostly highway towing does not use, so it becomes an optional extra (FT01). For a track with a longer gap, carry fuel or fit it then. The ute model keeps its extra 50 L of diesel as the heavier case, so the mass checks stay on the safe side.

The gauge. The overheating reports this search found concern automatic gearboxes, for example the [LandCruiser Forum thread on transmission overheating](https://www.landcruiserforum.com/threads/transmission-overheating-anyone-else.2108/) about an automatic Land Cruiser. None was found for the manual H15x, and report 15 planned no cooler until the senders showed one was needed. The towing limits that protect the engine and driveline, coolant, oil and EGT, are read on the gauges the tune needs (TL02, TL03), which stay in the build. So the gearbox and rear diff senders become an optional extra (CG11). VEH-16 and VEH-17 read them where fitted, or an infrared thermometer on the case after the climb (est.).

## 5. The other placements the brief asked about

- The sealed airbox (ID01) stays in the build. Unsealed 4X4 lists airbox dusting as a common 1VD fault: dust passes the factory seal, upsets the MAF sensor and wears the turbo, and an aftermarket airbox is the proven fix. A rebuilt engine and a new turbo are what it protects.
- The DPF clean (ID02) stays in the build, at S6. The emissions evidence for the tune's certificate needs a working DPF (TL04), and regeneration does not burn the ash that 400,000 km leaves.
- The radiator (EN14) stays in the build, because report 14 section 3.9 accepts level B for towing only with a new radiator. The reason is as-new heat rejection for sustained towing, which a flow test cannot show a 400,000 km core still gives. The factory-type core is enough, because level B's extra heat is held by the tune's EGT limit and its towing map, so a heavier core is not priced.
- The allowance follows the trailer's rule, 10 % of listed prices and 20 % of estimates. Report 22 section 2.15 found 10 to 15 % for DIY campers, 20 % for an owner-managed one-off and 15 to 25 % for early estimates. No better basis was found for a one-off truck build, so the rule stands, on the build only.

## 6. NSW registration and the trailer's chassis welds

The question is whether a home-built trailer of 3,500 kg ATM needs a qualified welder, a weld certificate or crack testing to be registered in NSW.

VSB1. [VSB1 Revision 6](https://img1.wsimg.com/blobby/go/630d18fb-e7b9-4863-b5b6-b89af2cdedee/vsb1-revision-6-.pdf) (September 2024, a hosted copy) sets no rule for who welds a trailer or how welds are tested. Welding appears only where it warns against welding T-rated safety chains, in coupling markings and in the plate's fixing. The drawbar must take a force of 1.5 times the ATM's weight along the bar and half of it across and vertically, each for at least 10 s, without cracks or lasting deformation (section 9.1), shown by a test at an approved facility (chapter 14) or by approved calculations that cover strength and fatigue.

The concessional RAV entry. An individual may enter up to four low-ATM trailers in 12 months this way ([Transport Tasmania](https://www.transport.tas.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0008/436058/Changes_Affecting_Low_ATM_Trailers.PDF)). For a low-ATM trailer the applicant need not upload every ADR's evidence but must show the basis of the ATM and hold the evidence, which the department may ask for. The evidence can be a component type approval, results from a registered test facility, calculations or other means the standards describe, or photographs and drawings supporting a declaration ([BTT Engineering Consulting](https://khaki-coyote-faew.squarespace.com/rover-rvsa-certification/concessional-rav-entry-approval)). Nothing asks for a weld certificate.

The HVAIS inspection. TfNSW's [AIS Notice 41](https://www.transport.nsw.gov.au/system/files/media/documents/2024/AIS-Notice-41-Inspecting-Concessional-Trailers.pdf) on inspecting concessional trailers could not be read (the server refused the download). The national manual's chassis check rejects a chassis whose welds are missing, loose, distorted or cracked, and repairs not made to recognised industry methods ([NHVIM V3.2 section 6.7](https://www.nhvr.gov.au/files/media/document/635/202607-0830-nhvim-section-6-structure-and-body-condition.pdf)). Assumption: the HVAIS examiner judges a light trailer's welds the same way, by eye.

What the plan still needs. None of the three asks for a qualified welder, a weld certificate or crack tests. The plan's own path to registration does lean on the welds, though. The RAV entry needs the drawbar's ADR 62/02 evidence in hand, and the plan's evidence is the engineer's calculation of CHASSIS.md (CMP-03), which H26 keeps. That calculation assumes the welds CHASSIS.md specifies: every structural weld in the SP category of AS/NZS 1554.1, inspected to report 02's WLD-03, visually throughout and by dye penetrant or magnetic particle on the drawbar-to-chassis welds, the coupling mount and the suspension mounts. AS/NZS 1554.1 has a qualified welder make SP welds. Its clause 4.12.2.3 qualifies a welder on a test piece of at least 300 mm welded to the job's procedure and examined visually and by macro, and a welder qualified to AS/NZS 2980 or AS/NZS ISO 9606-1 counts as qualified (clause 4.12.2.2), as summarised by [Technoweld](https://www.technoweld.com.au/services/welder-qualification/).

| Option | AUD | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Leonard qualifies himself to AS/NZS 1554.1 clause 4.12.2.3, the test witnessed by a NATA-accredited test house | 1,000 | Estimate: no Australian price is published and Technoweld quotes on request, so about 4 h of a weld inspector's time at about AUD 150 an hour, the macro section and the certificate |
| A qualified welder hired for the chassis and drawbar welds only | 3,100 to 3,400 | Estimate: the 28 h of welding in report 22's chassis hours at AUD 110 to 120 an hour, the rate [mobile welders](https://mobilewelders.com.au/) start from |
| Leonard's own dye-penetrant tests of the joints WLD-03 names, before galvanising, with two CRC Crick 110, 120 and 130 sets | 143.68 | Listing, converted from EUR 42.76 a set including VAT ([Soldufer](https://www.soldufer.com/producto/kit-soldadura-110120130-crick-ref117073160338-crc/)). No Australian listing was found |

The least that registers the trailer with the engineer's calculation standing is the first and the third: Leonard's qualification test and his own dye-penetrant tests, AUD 1,143.68 before contingency (CMP-14). The hired welder costs two to three times as much and still leaves the rest of the welds to Leonard. If the engineer accepts Leonard's welds without the test, CMP-14 falls to the dye-penetrant sets.

## Open points and the assumption used

1. Can the factory calibration run the Pro 100 injectors? Assumed not, so they go in with the tune.
2. Can GTurbo return the old nozzles with its service? Assumed yes. If not, a standard set is AUD 9,256 for a return to stock.
3. Does a passed S0 back-leak test show the piezo bodies sound for the Pro 100 service? Assumed yes, as the nozzle, the other wear part, is replaced.
4. Does the LC4 certificate cover the injectors at TL05's price, on the TfNSW test with no laboratory test? Assumed yes. If the S0 evidence list asks for a laboratory test with the Pro 100, the build drops FS01 and FS07 and runs level B on the factory injectors (section 1).
5. Does the HVAIS examiner judge a light trailer's welds as the NHVIM does? Assumed yes, by eye, as AIS Notice 41 could not be read.
6. What does a welder qualification test cost in NSW? Assumed AUD 1,000 (est.) until a test house quotes.
