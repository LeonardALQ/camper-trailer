The chassis is a ladder frame of two composite rails. Each composite rail is an RHS 150 x 50 x 3 main rail with an
RHS 100 x 50 x 3 raiser welded on top along its whole length, so the two bend as one 250 mm deep section and lift
the floor above the tyres. Crossmembers join the rails at nine stations, an outrigger runs out to the body edge at
every station, and an A-frame drawbar of RHS 150 x 50 x 5 runs from the coupling to the body front and on under the
rails for 1,000 mm. All the tube is C450L0 steel, every structural weld is SP to AS/NZS 1554.1, and the whole frame
is hot-dip galvanised after welding. The design starts from research report 11 and is set out in `CHASSIS.md`.

This is a planning-level proposal for the engineer to check. The drawings are drafts and are not for construction.

The frame analysis runs every case on all seven trailer cases, water included, with the masses scaled so the
heaviest case sits at the 3,500 kg ATM. LC5b runs at tare. The limit is the yield stress of 450 MPa divided by the

### Fatigue at each welded detail

Each welded joint has an IIW fatigue class (FAT). The frequent stress range must stay below the constant amplitude
fatigue limit (CAFL, 0.585 FAT). Joints marked for a hot-spot check are hollow-section T-joints, where the
stress at the weld toe is higher than the nominal stress, so the engineer checks them by the hot-spot method.
