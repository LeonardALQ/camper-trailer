A galvanised ladder frame of two composite rails, set out in `CHASSIS.md` revision AF from research report 11. Each
composite rail is an RHS 150 x 50 x 4 C450L0 main rail, 4 mm since the 4,000 kg plate, with an RHS 127 x 51 x 3.5 C350L0 raiser welded along its whole
length, so the two bend as one 277 mm deep section and lift the floor above the tyres. The rails run the full
{n(t.body_length)} mm of the body. Crossmembers join them at eight stations, an outrigger runs to the body edge at every
station, and an A-frame drawbar of RHS 150 x 50 x 6 runs from the coupling to the body front and 1,000 mm on under the
rails, its laps under them 2.5 mm open on stitch welds so both faces galvanise (FAT-21). The drawbar carries the box with the two generators and the fuel cans.

The rails, full-height crossmembers, drawbar and braces are C450L0, and the raisers, outriggers and floor
crossmembers C350L0. Every structural weld is SP to AS/NZS 1554.1, and the frame is hot-dip galvanised after welding.
Every case is checked at the {n(t.caps.plated)} kg plate (decision H2, amended 23 Nov 2026 from 3,500 kg), with VSB1's
drawbar and chain forces at that plate. The coupling is a Cruisemaster DO45 Plus on four M14 bolts, and the chains are
8 mm grade 80 to AS 2321, as VSB1 asks over 3.5 t. Each chain is held for good at its lug by a grade 100
connecting link, with no shackle at the trailer, as WA's CI-119C asks over 3.5 t, and is shackled only at the truck.
The drawbar's members went from 5 to 6 mm walls so its root keeps a quarter of its fatigue limit in hand (FAT-07).
At the body front a 16 mm plate closes each rail's end and takes the root gusset's heel. The gusset meets the plate
19.9 degrees off square, so the heel's fillets have legs of 7 mm inboard and 10 mm outboard to give both a 5.7 mm
throat. The A-frame's mitred butt splice under the heel, the heel's fillets and the plate each have a fatigue check of
their own (FAT-17 to FAT-19). The plate passes the heel's pull to the rail through full penetration welds made from
outside, the plate behind each root. Before galvanising the inspector views each root by videoscope from inside the
rail and records it, and the record must show complete root fusion along each root and no crack. The welds join an
RHS's 4 mm walls to a plate from one side, so they are read at IIW 424's FAT 45 for hollow sections (FAT-20), on each
weld's maximum principal stress range, at 0.88 of its limit.
The gusset runs from the member's centre at its toe to a heel 3 mm inboard of the plate's middle, where the two
webs' welds share its pull, and FAT-20 takes the pull along the gusset's line, its part along the plate as shear.

No NSW bath takes the frame's 2.4 m across the outriggers level, so it is galvanised in one dip at Valmont Coatings
Hexham, whose largest dip is 14.0 x 1.9 x 2.6 m, rolled about 46 degrees across, either side down, and hung drawbar
end down at up to 9 degrees (est.). Each rail's closed front end drains through two 20 mm holes in each web by the end
plate and a 10 mm slot under the plate, and vents along the rail to its open rear end (CT-103 detail K). Each drawbar
member drains at its apex end and at its mitre, vents at its apex end's top corners and is open at its snipe, and every
other closed end drains by its floor and vents by its top (CT-103 note 2). CHS-11 reads every closed section in that dip,
either side down and at every pitch from level to 9 degrees, and fails the build if a low point stands more than 50 mm
under its nearest drain or a high point more than 50 mm over its nearest vent. Each shock support plate and each
rail-to-raiser seam is a seal welded overlap the guide asks to be vented, so each has two 12 mm vents, which CHS-11 reads too.

The recovery case asks no yield at 58.9 kN and no fracture at the 78.5 kN ultimate. Report 11 gives the pull but not
what holds the trailer, so the ground holds it: on its four tyres at their rates (est.), each tyre's side force no
more than its grip, 0.35 to 0.8 times its load (est.), or with one wheel held fast against an obstacle. To turn about
the held wheel the trailer must scrub the other axle's tyres sideways, so it turns only as far as their grip lets it,
and the held wheel takes the pull and their side forces together. Every pull is read at both ends of that range of
grip and the worse governs, and with every wheel free a side force the tyres cannot hold slides the trailer sideways,
so the frame takes the pull it slides at. The trailer's weight acts with the pull, at tare and fully loaded, and no
support can pull. A tyre that would leaves the ground, the nose stands on its jockey wheel or is held down by the
coupled truck, and where no set of supports holds the trailer at the whole pull it tips first, so the frame takes the
pull it tips at. An airbag cannot pull either, so an arm
the pull forces down drops onto its shock absorber at full extension, which then pulls on its crossmember (CHS-15).
CHS-08A and CHS-08U read every case, every member on its effective section against its yield stress and every hole on
its net section, less every hole within a diameter of it along its member, against 0.85 of its tensile strength at the
ultimate. Beside the XT centre crossmember each rail has four holes in one cross-section, the side markers' cable into
the outrigger and the crossmember's drain, vent and check hole, and neither set can move a diameter along the rail
inside the outrigger's or the crossmember's end, so they are read together. A held wheel puts the whole pull through its XT
hinge, which a crossmember's welded end alone does not carry, so each hinge has a 10 mm cheek either side of its crossmember on
the rail's inner face, welded to the rail's web and down the hinge bar's rear face, or ahead of the crossmember down a filler's
front face. Those short welds pass the force but not the moment, so the frame model pins the cheeks there (CHS-12). The bar
then hands its moment on as a couple, the pin pushing the cheeks and the bar's welds pulling the crossmember, so the pin can
pass more than the hinge's own force. Each cheek is read at its own share of it: the front cheek meets its filler 77 mm
ahead of the pivot and the rear cheek meets the bar 23 mm behind it, so with a pin that passes no moment the rear cheek takes
up to 77 % of the force across and up (CHS-14). The front hinges' front cheeks stand clear of the brake
supply K-10, which crosses under the road rail ahead of them and passes them inboard, and of the plan braces, whose rear
ends meet the rails 200 mm ahead of the front hinge crossmember. Each cheek's end is coped over the web, clear of its
crossmember's end fillet, and each cheek beside a hinge gusset carries a step the gusset's end lands on, which spreads
its push and pull over the rail's 4 mm web as far as the step's own bending lets it (CHS-16). The front hinge's steps are
150 mm long and 93 mm tall, so they end clear of the front airbags' top brackets, and no gusset stands ahead of the XT
centre crossmember, where the front shocks' brackets stand on its front face (FIT-08).
Each hinge crossmember is two RHS 150 x 50 x 6 seam welded along their length, 100 mm wide in all with 8 mm fillets
round each tube's ends, the rear tube where Cruisemaster's 150 x 50 x 6 stood, since its positions run from it, and the
front tube where the wider crossmember grew forward. Each 50 mm hinge bar stays flush with that rear face, welded to its
crossmember by 6 mm fillets in three 120 mm runs a side, which CHS-18 reads, where Cruisemaster's sheet 3 draws three 80 mm runs,
since page 3's runs fail in fatigue at their roots on the twin crossmembers (FAT-26). On the elastic bars the
frame model holds each run at its welds' own stiffness, so a run passes a moment only as its two fillets' forces apart,
and reads each run on its own. Each run's rear fillet stands over the rear tube's rear web and its front fillet over the
seam, where the two tubes' webs stand face to face, as both stand over webs on Cruisemaster's own RHS 150 x 50, so no
wall bends under them. On the single RHS 150 x 100 x 6 the front fillet stood on the bottom wall's flat and braking took
the runs past their strength (`CHASSIS.md` revision AD). The seams, a flare-V groove weld filled flush by GMAW in each
pair of R15 corners where the tubes meet, top and bottom, make the pair one closed section, and CHS-20 reads them in
every case through the joint. Each tube drains and vents through the rails' inner webs at both ends, and the two webs
face to face are a sealed overlap vented through both. Each front cheek ends at its crossmember's front face on a filler, a steel block 50 x 110 x 20 mm under the
crossmember's front half from that face to the bar's front face, welded to the bar, to the crossmember and along the rail's
outer bottom corner, since the cheek's own 10 x 26 mm part under the rail could not carry its share 50 mm back to the bar.
The cheeks' and the fillers' flare-bevel welds in the members' rounded corners are made by GMAW to a procedure proven by a
macro section, which gives them 6.25 mm throats in the rails' corners and 9.4 mm in the crossmembers' (est.).
CHS-19 reports the bars and their hinge plates, which are Cruisemaster's, for Cruisemaster to rate. The wider crossmember
carries the couple the arm's two bushes put on the bar across the trailer's line, which the 50 mm one did not, and
the rear tank's guard is an 8 mm galvanised steel sheet, cut 10 mm clear of Cruisemaster's rear airbags and shocks,
its front corners stepped 50 mm clear of the rear arms' pivot tubes and inner legs over their travel (FIT-05),
bolted to short angles on the rails ahead of the airbags' brackets and to angles under the XT rear shock crossmember,
so the rear tank's bay carries the pull's sideways part through it (CHS-13). The tank sits on the sheet, so nothing
holds its free edges, and CHS-13 reads its buckling on the points that hold it over the sheet as cut, its bolts and
its tongues' and wings' roots. The trailer is jacked only under Cruisemaster's jacking point under each arm by its
hub, the air released from the springs first, and the jack's push reaches the frame through the arm's bump stop and
its airbag's top bracket (LOC-04). Each recovery point is rated 58.9 kN, and the frame
limits the pull to 40 kN with every wheel free, 15 kN with one wheel held fast and 15 kN with both wheels of one axle held,
each by the rule the largest pull at which every check passes on the twin hinge crossmembers with the bars' 120 mm runs
(`CHASSIS.md` revision AE). A pull that tips the trailer never reaches the frame at its full size, so the user's limits
below keep it on its wheels as well. Every check is read
on the hinge bars rigid and elastic at their own section on the arms' free and stiff bushes. The hinge bars' weld runs set all three limits, each hinge's runs taking
the bar's twist from the arm's pull 77 mm under the bar (`CHASSIS.md` revisions AC and AE).
Every check passes, and
Cruisemaster accepts in writing, before they are built, the hinge joints, the holes in its crossmembers, the
hinge bar's moment and the pins' push on it, what its shock absorbers and airbags carry in a recovery, the places the
design scales from its sheets, the hinge crossmembers of two RHS 150 x 50 x 6 seam welded, the bars' 120 mm weld runs, and the front shocks at the 900 mm axle
spacing: under the working default a front shock about 14 mm longer at ride than the rear's (est.) from the XT centre
crossmember's front face, 50 mm further forward than it stood, since no crossmember of their own read so far carries them.

## Recovery limits

Pull only at a front lug or a rear tongue within 30 degrees of the trailer's line sideways, or at the coupling along the
trailer's line only, all within 15 degrees up or down, and no harder than the limit for what can be seen at the point. A
wheel that cannot be seen down to the ground, or one against a rock, log, stump or bank, counts as held.

| The line on | Every wheel seen free, the line level or falling | Every wheel seen free, the line rising | A wheel not seen free |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A front lug | 40 kN | 25 kN | 15 kN |
| A rear tongue | 30 kN | 20 kN | 15 kN |
| The coupling | 40 kN | 15 kN | 15 kN |

Each limit is the lesser of the frame's for the wheel states it allows and 75 % of the least pull that tips the trailer
in it, empty or fully loaded, coupled or not, rounded down to 5 kN, after the mobile crane's practice of rating its load
at no more than 75 % of the tipping load (SAE J765 and ISO 4305, est. as an analogy). The empty trailer tips from 21.7 kN
at the coupling pulled along its line and 15 degrees up, 28.7 kN at a rear tongue pulled 30 degrees across the trailer
and 15 degrees up, 38.1 kN at a front lug pulled 23 degrees across the trailer and 15 degrees up, and 42.8 kN at a rear
tongue pulled along its line and 15 degrees down. Pulled level it does not tip up to the points' 78.5 kN ultimate, on
level ground (est.).

The limit is the force at the point, read by a load cell there or set by a winch whose rated line pull on the layer in
use is no more than the limit, and a snatch block at the point doubles the force there. No kinetic rope or snatch strap
is used on the trailer, since its peak force is not known. The ute's own traction reaches about 35 kN at its 4,500 kg GVM
on ground gripping at 0.8 (est.), so the ute pulls the trailer, coupled or on a static strap, only where the limit is
40 kN. On wet grass a sideways pull slides the empty trailer toward it from 14.7 kN. While the line is loaded everyone
stands at least 1.5 times its length away, the distance
[Queensland's Office of Fair Trading](https://productsafety.gov.au/system/files/Recovery%20straps%20safety%20flyer%20%28Queensland%20OFT%29.pdf)
asks for a recovery strap and [winch makers](https://sdda.semadata.org/Standard/Rough%20Country/92RS4500.pdf) ask for a
winch cable, out of the line's path and off the side the trailer can slide or tip toward, and nobody steps over the line.
CT-003 gives the same table and rules.

### Fatigue at each welded detail

Each welded joint has an IIW fatigue class (FAT). The frequent stress range must stay below the constant amplitude
fatigue limit (CAFL, 0.585 FAT). Joints marked for a hot-spot check are hollow-section T-joints, where the
stress at the weld toe is higher than the nominal stress, so the engineer checks them by the hot-spot method.
The hinge joint's weld roots (FAT-26, FAT-27) also read a braking block and a cornering block, each 2 million cycles
from ADR 62/02's drawbar test, and their Miner damage is added to the bump's at each weld (est.).
