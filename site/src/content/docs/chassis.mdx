A galvanised ladder frame of two composite rails, set out in `CHASSIS.md` revision AA from research report 11. Each
composite rail is an RHS 150 x 50 x 4 C450L0 main rail, 4 mm since the 4,000 kg plate, with an RHS 127 x 51 x 3.5 C350L0 raiser welded along its whole
length, so the two bend as one 277 mm deep section and lift the floor above the tyres. The rails run the full
{n(t.body_length)} mm of the body. Crossmembers join them at eight stations, an outrigger runs to the body edge at every
station, and an A-frame drawbar of RHS 150 x 50 x 6 runs from the coupling to the body front and 1,000 mm on under the
rails, its laps under them 2.5 mm open on stitch welds so both faces galvanise (FAT-21). The drawbar carries the box with the two generators and the fuel cans.

The rails, full-height crossmembers, drawbar and braces are C450L0, and the raisers, outriggers and floor
crossmembers C350L0. Every structural weld is SP to AS/NZS 1554.1, and the frame is hot-dip galvanised after welding.
Every case is checked at the {n(t.caps.plated)} kg plate (decision H2, amended 23 Nov 2026 from 3,500 kg), with VSB1's
drawbar and chain forces at that plate. The coupling is a Cruisemaster DO45 Plus on four M14 bolts, and the chains are
8 mm grade 80 to AS 2321, as VSB1 asks over 3.5 t. Each chain is held for good at its lug by a grade 100
connecting link, with no shackle at the trailer, as WA's CI-119C asks over 3.5 t, and is shackled only at the truck.
The drawbar's members went from 5 to 6 mm walls so its root keeps a quarter of its fatigue limit in hand (FAT-07).
At the body front a 16 mm plate closes each rail's end and takes the root gusset's heel. The gusset meets the plate
19.9 degrees off square, so the heel's fillets have legs of 7 mm inboard and 10 mm outboard to give both a 5.7 mm
throat. The A-frame's mitred butt splice under the heel, the heel's fillets and the plate each have a fatigue check of
their own (FAT-17 to FAT-19). The plate passes the heel's pull to the rail through full penetration welds made from
outside, the plate behind each root. Before galvanising the inspector views each root by videoscope from inside the
rail and records it, and the record must show complete root fusion along each root and no crack. The welds join an
RHS's 4 mm walls to a plate from one side, so they are read at IIW 424's FAT 45 for hollow sections (FAT-20), on each
weld's maximum principal stress range, at 0.88 of its limit.
The gusset runs from the member's centre at its toe to a heel 3 mm inboard of the plate's middle, where the two
webs' welds share its pull, and FAT-20 takes the pull along the gusset's line, its part along the plate as shear.

No NSW bath takes the frame's 2.4 m across the outriggers level, so it is galvanised in one dip at Valmont Coatings
Hexham, whose largest dip is 14.0 x 1.9 x 2.6 m, rolled about 46 degrees across, either side down, and hung drawbar
end down at up to 9 degrees (est.). Each rail's closed front end drains through two 20 mm holes in each web by the end
plate and a 10 mm slot under the plate, and vents along the rail to its open rear end (CT-103 detail K). Each drawbar
member drains at its apex end and at its mitre, vents at its apex end's top corners and is open at its snipe, and every
other closed end drains by its floor and vents by its top (CT-103 note 2). CHS-11 reads every closed section in that dip,
either side down and at every pitch from level to 9 degrees, and fails the build if a low point stands more than 50 mm
under its nearest drain or a high point more than 50 mm over its nearest vent. Each shock support plate and each
rail-to-raiser seam is a seal welded overlap the guide asks to be vented, so each has two 12 mm vents, which CHS-11 reads too.

The recovery case asks no yield at 58.9 kN and no fracture at the 78.5 kN ultimate. Report 11 gives the pull but not
what holds the trailer, so the ground holds it: on its four tyres at their rates (est.), or with one wheel held fast
against an obstacle, the trailer turned until the pull passes through that wheel, which then takes all of it. The
trailer's weight acts with the pull, at tare and fully loaded, and no support can pull. A tyre that would leaves the
ground, the nose stands on its jockey wheel or is held down by the coupled truck, and where no set of supports holds the
trailer at the whole pull it tips first, so the frame takes the pull it tips at. An airbag cannot pull either, so an arm
the pull forces down drops onto its shock absorber at full extension, which then pulls on its crossmember (CHS-15).
CHS-08A and CHS-08U read every case, every member on its effective section against its yield stress and every hole on
its net section, less every hole within a diameter of it along its member, against 0.85 of its tensile strength at the
ultimate. Beside the XT centre crossmember each rail has four holes in one cross-section, the side markers' cable into
the outrigger and the crossmember's drain, vent and check hole, and neither set can move a diameter along the rail
inside the outrigger's or the crossmember's end, so they are read together. A held wheel puts the whole pull through its XT
hinge, which a crossmember's welded end alone does not carry, so each hinge has a 10 mm cheek either side of its crossmember on
the rail's inner face, welded to the rail's web and down the hinge bar. Those short welds to the bar pass the force but
not the moment, so the frame model pins each cheek there (CHS-12). The bar then hands its moment on as a couple, the pin
pushing the cheek and the bar's welds pulling the crossmember, so the pin can pass more than the hinge's own force. Every
hinge shares it between its two cheeks and holds it (CHS-14). The front hinges' front cheeks stand clear of the brake
supply K-10, which crosses under the road rail ahead of them and passes them inboard, and of the plan braces, whose rear
ends meet the rails 200 mm ahead of the front hinge crossmember. Each cheek's end is coped over the web, clear of its
crossmember's end fillet, and each cheek beside a hinge gusset carries a step the gusset's end lands on, which spreads
its push and pull over the rail's 4 mm web as far as the step's own bending lets it (CHS-16). The front hinge's steps are
150 mm long and 93 mm tall, so they end clear of the front airbags' top brackets, and no gusset stands ahead of the XT
centre crossmember, where the front shocks' brackets stand on its front face (FIT-08).
Both hinge crossmembers are RHS 150 x 100 x 6 with 8 mm fillets at their ends, each with its rear face where the
150 x 50 x 6 stood, since Cruisemaster's positions run from it, and 50 mm wider forward. Each 50 mm hinge bar stays flush
with that rear face, tied to its crossmember at the three weld runs a side where Cruisemaster's sheet 3 draws them, and
each front cheek's part under the rail runs on under its crossmember to the bar's front face. The wider crossmember
carries the couple the arm's two bushes put on the bar across the trailer's line, which the 50 mm one did not, and
the rear tank's guard is an 8 mm galvanised steel sheet, cut 10 mm clear of Cruisemaster's rear airbags and shocks,
its front corners stepped 50 mm clear of the rear arms' pivot tubes and inner legs over their travel (FIT-05),
bolted to short angles on the rails ahead of the airbags' brackets and to angles under the XT rear shock crossmember,
so the rear tank's bay carries the pull's sideways part through it (CHS-13). The tank sits on the sheet, so nothing
holds its free edges, and CHS-13 reads its buckling on the points that hold it over the sheet as cut, its bolts and
its tongues' and wings' roots. The trailer is jacked only under Cruisemaster's jacking point under each arm by its
hub, the air released from the springs first, and the jack's push reaches the frame through the arm's bump stop and
its airbag's top bracket (LOC-04). With one wheel held fast
a recovery limit of 55 kN applies, and with both wheels of one axle held one of 50 kN, each the largest pull at which
every check passes, and CT-003 gives the user the rule: pull within 30 degrees sideways and level, since a pull 15
degrees up or down can tip the empty trailer first. Every check is read on the hinge bars rigid and elastic at their
own section on the arms' free and stiff bushes, and the hinge cheeks' welds on the rigid bars set the held-wheel limit and
the hole H-01 in the kerb-side raiser the held-axle one, while the rated pull holds with every wheel free (`CHASSIS.md`
revision AA). Every check passes, and
Cruisemaster accepts in writing, before they are built, the hinge joints, the holes in its crossmembers, the
hinge bar's moment and the pins' push on it, what its shock absorbers and airbags carry in a recovery, the places the
design scales from its sheets, the hinge crossmembers at RHS 150 x 100 x 6, and the front shocks at the 900 mm axle
spacing: under the working default a front shock about 14 mm longer at ride than the rear's (est.) from the XT centre
crossmember's front face, 50 mm further forward than it stood, since no crossmember of their own read so far carries them.

A planning-level proposal for the engineer to check. The drawings are drafts, not for construction.

### Fatigue at each welded detail

Each welded joint has an IIW fatigue class (FAT). The frequent stress range must stay below the constant amplitude
fatigue limit (CAFL, 0.585 FAT). Joints marked for a hot-spot check are hollow-section T-joints, where the
stress at the weld toe is higher than the nominal stress, so the engineer checks them by the hot-spot method.
