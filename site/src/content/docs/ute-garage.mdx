The bike box carries the Aprilia Tuareg 660 Rally and the Triumph Street Triple 765 side by side, noses forward, with
about 60 kg of tools. It is a welded aluminium body on its own subframe, bolted to Ford's body mounting pads on the
<a href={withBase('/tow-vehicle/')}>Ranger Super Duty</a>, locked, sealed against dust and vented for fuelled bikes. The
build document is `UTE-GARAGE.md` revision G and the parts list `data/ute/box-parts.yaml`. Leonard builds it himself to
its own checks and does not have it certified (decision H25).

## 3D model

The ute as it travels, with the box closed. The Ranger Super Duty and both bikes are modelled as the real vehicles at their
makers' dimensions. Every part stands on, hangs from or is fixed to another, down to the tyres, and the trailer's
support check (H27) tests the ute's two models the same way. Research report 29
gives each dimension and its source. The repo's own code builds them from those published dimensions, with no maker's
CAD file, so close up they are simplified on purpose. Hide the body group in the tree, or click a wall and choose Hide this part, to see
the bikes, straps and tool chests inside.

### Loading

Parked for loading, the barn doors fold back against the side walls on their hinges and the ramp hooks on the sill. The
Tuareg is half way up the ramp on the winch's rope, which runs from the front wall to a soft loop round its lower triple
clamp, and the Street Triple stands strapped in its chock. With the bikes out, the folded ramp travels on its edge along
the Tuareg's bay behind its chock, held by the Tuareg's straps, rather than across the back. That keeps the added mass of
the empty box inside Ford's centre of gravity window (UTE-07, UGC-07 and drawing CT-704).

## How it is built

- Body: walls, front and door skins in 5052-H32 2.5 mm, the roof in 1.6 mm, the floor in 4.0 mm, the wheel tubs in
  3.0 mm and a folded 2.5 mm flare over each rear tub, TIG-welded with ER5356 to a 6061-T6 frame of SHS 40 x 40 x 3.
  Buy the 6061-T6 with mill certificates, because the common stock tube is 6060-T5, which is weaker when welded.
- Subframe: 6061-T6 runners over Ford's body mounting pads and clear of the rail tops, with a crossmember at every pad
  station and outriggers outside them. Under each track row a hot-dip galvanised C350L0 RHS 65 x 35 x 4.0 runs the full
  width. The rear one stands on the mounts over Ford's rearmost pads and the front one on steel stubs that reach Ford's
  first pads, so a strap's load reaches the frame through steel. Nothing is drilled or welded on the frame (Ford's body
  equipment mounting manual, BEMM p. 155).
- Mounting: Ford asks an exceptionally rigid body, as a welded box is, to sit on flexible mounts at its two forward pads
  and rigid mounts at all the others (BEMM p. 124). So the first pair has two rubber cushions of about 1 kN/mm with the
  travel stopped at 12 mm and a captive bolt, and the other three pairs have galvanised steel spacers. Each of the eight
  pads takes one M14 10.9 bolt, head up from inside the subframe with a locknut below, so the box comes off only from
  inside it (CT-702 detail A).
- Doors and security: barn doors on continuous stainless hinges with dog bolts, a cam bar on the road leaf and an
  ABLOY PL362 padlock in a welded shroud. Inside, an alarmed ABUS disc lock on each front disc closes a 12 mm chain to a
  floor anchor at X 450 mm. A Teltonika tracker with door switches and a siren, and a Monimoto 9 in each bike.
- Restraint: a Condor SC-2000 chock for each front wheel, two rows of plain Kinedyne FE750 track, US Cargo Control
  1519 double-stud fittings and 35 mm SpanSet straps rated LC 500 kg (LC is the rated lashing capacity). An anchor must
  withstand its strap's breaking load, about 9.8 kN, and the steel crossmember carries that at its worst anchor (UGR-03).
- Services: a filtered, ignition-protected fan keeps the box above outside pressure, two low vents let air and petrol
  vapour out, and a fume detector starts the fan and signals the tracker.

## The ute

Case UC3 is the heaviest: towing with both bikes in the box and the trailer's heaviest towball with growth on the ball
(the trailer's MAS-03B checks it). The static stability factor (SSF) is half the track divided by the centre of gravity
height, and a lower SSF means a higher risk of rolling over.

## Checks

Clearance and contact checks measured on the 3D model. The clearance limits come from the retired trailer garage's
design and the tyre clearances from Ford's wheelhouse envelope (BEMM p. 122). A fail check that misses its limit fails the build, and a reported check
never does.

<Ute kind="garage-checks" />

### Loads on Ford's pads

Ford's road-load inputs of up to 2 g in each direction at each pad (BEMM p. 124): braking and cornering at 2 g with
gravity, a 2 g bump on top of gravity and a net 1 g upward, on the box and its contents with the growth allowance. Ford
gives the inputs but no capacity for its pads, so Leonard proof-loads each one to 1.25 times the largest loads below
before the box goes on (`UTE-GARAGE.md` step 9), and the rough-road drive and dye-penetrant test of step 11 check them
in use.
