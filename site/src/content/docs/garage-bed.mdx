This module is the garage for two motorcycles with a queen bed above them. It sets the trailer's width, its height
with the roof down and much of its weight.

This page shows revision D of the design. The owner did not want the bikes in the middle of the trailer, because
there the garage split the living space into two rooms. So revision D puts the garage at the back with a rear
door, and the <a href={withBase('/trailer/')}>whole-trailer layout</a> moves every heavy item towards the back to
keep the towball inside the car's limits.

## The design and why

### The bikes stand along the trailer

The two bikes are an Aprilia Tuareg 660 Rally (206 kg fuelled, 2,220 mm long) and a Triumph Street Triple 765
set up for the track (175 kg, 2,055 mm long). They stand side by side along the trailer, noses forward, in a
garage 2,330 mm long and 2,330 mm wide. Each loads nose first through the rear door and unloads backwards.

The front tyres stop 50 mm short of partition A, the garage's front wall. That gap is for the nose of the wheel
chock, and it must be measured on a real chock.

The Tuareg stands towards the road-side wall, 565 mm from it, and the Street Triple 760 mm to its left. That
leaves the spare width on the kerb side. The Tuareg's panniers sit at its back and the Street Triple's tail is
narrow, so the gas locker goes in the kerb-side rear corner, beside the Street Triple's tail.

### Why 760 mm between the bikes works

The bikes' handlebars overlap when seen from above, but they are at different heights. The Tuareg's bars and
hand guards are at about 1,190 to 1,215 mm, which is above the Street Triple's tank and bars at about 1,010 mm.

The tight spot is the Street Triple's right bar end against the Tuareg's side panels at the same height. The
owner measured the Tuareg 585 mm wide there, so the gap is about 71 mm against the 30 mm the design asks for. The
garage width is fixed, so a larger spacing pushes the Street Triple towards the kerb-side stowage. Spacings up
to 780 mm fit. Above that, check CLR-10 fails and the layout has to change (see the
<a href={withBase('/measurements/')}>Measurements</a> page).

### The rear door

The door is in the body's rear wall and is hinged at the top. Its opening is 1,910 mm wide and 1,440 mm high,
from 40 mm inside the road-side wall to the gas locker's inner face, with its header at the bed beams. Check
CLR-09 rolls each bike straight back: it measures each bike against the door frame extended forward over the whole
garage. The Tuareg's pannier clears the road-side jamb by 35 mm. The 3,048 mm ramp hooks on the sill at 16.6
degrees, or about 15.4 degrees with the air bags vented.

### Gas locker, generator, fuel and ramp

The two 9 kg gas cylinders and their regulator sit in a sealed aluminium locker, 760 by 380 by 690 mm, in the
kerb-side rear corner. It is reached only through a hatch in the kerb-side wall and has a 25 mm drain whose outlet
is 500 mm below the floor, which keeps it away from the door and vents. That follows the Australian rules for LPG
compartments in research report 02.

The generator and the jerry cans are petrol, like the bikes, so they ride in the garage, strapped against the
kerb-side wall in front of the gas locker. The ramp travels folded on its edge in a slot beside the locker, so it
slides out of the rear door first. The generator and the jerry cans go in first and come out last, through the
Street Triple's empty bay.

### How the bikes are held

Each bike's front wheel sits in a Condor SC-2000 chock, which holds the bike upright on its own. Two rows of
flush tie-down track run across the trailer, one in front of the bikes and one behind them, on the positions
research report 03 gives from each bike's axles. Each bike has two straps at the front, two at the rear and one
through the rear wheel, all 35 mm ratchet straps rated to 1,000 kg on soft loops.

Braking now pushes each bike forward into its chock, and cornering pushes it sideways. Check RES-01 works out
every strap's load with report 03's off-road load cases. The worst is the Tuareg's rear pair at 277 kgf, against
395 kgf when the bikes stood across the trailer. Every anchor and its floor fixing is still designed for 20 kN.

Every strap is reached from a place where a person can kneel. The Tuareg goes in first and is strapped from the
empty Street Triple bay, its road-side straps hooked on their anchors before it goes in and clipped to the bike by
reaching across it. The Street Triple is strapped from the front kerb corner and from inside the rear door beside
the gas locker. DESIGN.md has the reach distances.

### The bed is the garage ceiling

The bed platform covers the whole garage and is sealed all round, so it is also the garage ceiling. Five
aluminium beams run across the trailer between ledgers on the two side walls, a span of 2,330 mm. The longer span
needed a stiffer beam, so they are now 50 by 50 by 3 mm square tube, which sags about 5.7 mm under two sleepers
and keeps the 50 mm depth. A 20 mm honeycomb deck, 2,375 by 2,330 mm, sits on them and laps over the top of
partition A.

The beam underside is at 1,460 mm, which clears the Tuareg's screen with its mirrors folded by 70 mm. On the deck
sits a Froli sprung base, then a 150 mm queen mattress, which lies across the trailer at the back of the deck.
That leaves an 845 mm landing along partition A for climbing in. Allowing 50 mm for bedding leaves 27 mm under
the 1,800 mm ceiling.

The ladder stands in the living area on the kerb side and hooks onto the deck edge at partition A. It is
owner-built from aluminium square tube, about 1,580 mm long with rubber feet, so that at a 15 degree lean it
reaches the living-area floor.

### One partition

Partition A is a 20 mm honeycomb panel up to the deck underside, sealed to the floor, the walls and the deck. It
has no doorway, so the garage is reached only through the rear door.

### Garage safety

Petrol vapour is heavier than air. The garage has two low vents and one high vent, two fume sensors low down
with the alarm display beside the bed, and a 2.5 kg extinguisher on the gas locker by the rear door. No
switches, relays or chargers go in the lowest 500 mm, so the inverter and chargers are on the living side of
partition A. The positions in the model are placeholders until the body design fixes them.

### Loading

With a fixed bed at 1,460 mm, you walk a bike in stooped. A 12 V winch on a quick-release plate on partition A,
in front of each chock, pulls the bike up the ramp and into its chock while you steady it.

### Bike envelopes

The bikes are envelope models built from the measured points in research report 05. Their overall length,
width and height match the owner's figures within 10 mm. Values that were never measured, such as the peg and
fairing widths, are labelled as estimates in the data. In the garage each bike is pitched nose-up a little,
because the chock lifts its front wheel by about 25 mm.

## Mass and centre of gravity

Masses come from the bill of materials. Extrusions, track and panels are weighed from the model's own lengths
and areas, so the totals change when a dimension changes. Bought items keep their published or estimated mass.
The centre of gravity is in the module frame above.

### Mass schedule

Every line has its source and its maturity. A spec mass comes from a maker or retailer, a calculated mass from
the model and a supplier's kg per metre or square metre, and an estimate from the research reports.

The growth column is the allowance proposed in the design practice report (MASS-06) until each part is weighed:
20 % on an estimate and 7 % on a calculated mass. A spec mass is taken at 0 % here. The engineer agrees the final
allowances. The "With growth" column in the load cases above adds them up.

## Checks

These are the checks the design must pass, measured on the 3D model. A failed check shows in red with the
measured gap. CLR-01b, CLR-06b, CLR-08 and BED-01 are reported and never fail the build. Revision D added CLR-09
(each bike rolls out through the rear door), CLR-10 (the Street Triple's straps clear the kerb-side stowage) and
RES-01 (strap loads along the trailer).

## Open decisions and findings

These need the owner's decision or a measurement. The model does not make design decisions.

- Getting the generator out with the bikes aboard needs a hatch into the kerb strip. Without one it comes out after the Street Triple.
- The way to the Street Triple's front kerb-side straps is a floor-level corridor about 425 mm wide under the bed. Try it in the mock-up.
- Sleeping with fuelled bikes inside: the answer changes the garage ventilation and the fuel-smell sealing.
- Bike measurements: the Tuareg's screen height with mirrors folded, its length with the plate hanger (2,220 or about 2,263 mm), both bikes' peg widths, and the chock's nose depth and cradle rise.
- With the photo-measured 2,263 mm length the Tuareg's plate is 10.8 mm from the closed rear door, against 15 mm wanted. Moving both bikes 10 mm towards partition A gives 20.8 mm but leaves 40 mm for the chock nose.
- The chock takes the braking push, about 330 kgf for the Tuareg. The engineer confirms the chock and its bolts for it.
- The panniers' position along the Tuareg and their height are estimates. Measure them on the bike.
- The positions of the vents, the sensors and the extinguisher are placeholders.
