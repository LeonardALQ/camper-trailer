This is the block layout of the whole trailer, revision C. The <a href={withBase('/garage-bed/')}>garage and bed
module</a> sits at the back of the body with a rear door, and one living area runs from the front wall to the
garage. Every other system is a block with a mass, not a design. The point is to find where the axles go, what the
car carries on its towball, and what the chassis has to be. The design notes are in `TRAILER.md` in the
repository, and drawing CT-001 is the general arrangement.

## The layout and why

The owner did not want the bikes in the middle of the trailer, because there the garage split the living space
into two rooms. With the garage at the back, the bikes sit {n(t.solved.bikes_behind_axle_mm)} mm behind the
axles, so taking them out raises the towball. To keep that inside the car's limits, every heavy item moves to the
back.

From front to back there are the drawbar with the coupling, stone guard and jockey wheel (no drawbar box now),
then the living area, {n(t.zones.living)} mm long, then the {n(t.zones.garage)} mm garage. In the living area the
heavy items stand against partition A: the wet bathroom, the washer-dryer, the diesel heater with the air supply
on it, and the inverter and chargers on the partition. The fridge and the chest freezer stand along the road side
in front of the bathroom. The light items are at the front: the desk with the air-conditioner under it, the seat,
and the wardrobe over the kitchen slide-out, which stays on the kerb side under the awning. The entry door is in
the kerb-side wall behind the kitchen. Floor space is kept clear at the entry door, the ladder foot, the bathroom
door, the seat and a walkway between them.

The gas cylinders, the generator and the jerry cans moved from the drawbar box into the garage: the cylinders into
a sealed locker in the kerb-side rear corner, and the generator and cans along the kerb-side wall in front of it.
The 12 kWh battery moved to the back, under the garage floor between the rails, 400 mm in front of the rear wall.
The water tanks stay over the axles, so the towball barely changes as the water is used.

The floor height comes from the running gear in report 06. The tyre is 737.7 mm across, the hubs sit at
{n(ch.hub_z)} mm, and the XT suspension rises 60 mm at full bump. With 50 mm of clearance above the tyre, the floor
underside is at {n(ch.floor_underside_z)} mm and the garage floor surface at {n(ch.floor_z)} mm.

The axle group centre is {n(P.axle_x)} mm behind the ball. The build solved it for {n(t.target_kg)} kg on the ball
fully loaded, the target from report 07, and moved the tanks and the suspension with it. The garage stays at the
back.

## Ballast to tow with no bikes

With the bikes out, the towball rises above the car's 280 kg rating in the cases with water and gear aboard. The
build therefore works out the least ballast that brings those cases to 280 kg on base weights, placed at the back
of the garage, and carries it in the three cases without bikes (TC1, TC4 and TC5). The registration tare does not
include it. With the growth allowance far more is needed, which check BAL-01 reports.

## Checks

The trailer is to be registered in NSW. The NSW examiner caps GTM at the lowest of the tyre, coupling, chain and
brake ratings ({n(t.caps.gtm)} kg here) and ATM at the lower of the coupling and chain ratings ({n(t.caps.atm)} kg),
and MAS-07 and MAS-08 check against those caps. The towball limits are design targets from report 07, not legal
limits. 280 kg is the car's rating, used as a ceiling, and 6 % of the trailer's mass is the floor set for
stability. A failed check shows in red. The checks that fail do so only with the growth allowance, which is the
design's real state, and the limits stay as they are. The levers are set out in `TRAILER.md`.
