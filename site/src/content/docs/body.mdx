A box of bonded sandwich panels on the chassis, {n(t.body_length)} mm long and {n(t.params.body_width)} mm wide. Each
panel is two thin glass-fibre skins bonded to a light foam core: the floor is 30 mm with woven-roving skins on PVC
foam, the walls 35 mm with gelcoat skins on XPS foam, and the fixed roof 40 mm of the same kind, bonded to the walls
and carrying the four solar panels. Aluminium extrusions join every panel edge. The body bolts to the chassis at every
station with 32 M12 bolts into welded nut plates on the raisers, and is not bonded to the steel. The design is
`BODY.md` revision C, from research report 12.

A planning-level proposal for the engineer to check.

## 3D model

The body as it travels, with the four solar panels bonded to the roof and the awning's case on its brackets. The tree
on the left lists every panel, opening and fitting. The windows and doors are the Dometic S4 and Aussie Traveller DC
products in their cut-outs, with their frames, panes, blinds and handles.

### Inside

The same body fitted out, in the day and travel position with the bed raised. Every appliance is its maker's product
at its published size, and every part stands on, hangs from or is fixed to something that is modelled, the way its
maker's guide or a cabinet maker would fix it (H27, research report 28). The bed's platform lies on the HappiJac kit's
support rails, the desk stands on its wall cleat and two legs, the washer and dryer stand on the machine base over the
battery box, the toilet and the grey tote stand on the wet room's tray, and the fridge, oven and RESPA stand on the
tower's shelves. Hide the Body group in the tree to see the fit-out, and point at any part to see its line.

### Twist sharing

The frame model takes the body as a torsion box tied to the chassis at every station. With the roof bonded to the
walls the box is closed and takes almost all the twist. The chassis checks stay on the bare frame, so they hold if the
body mounts loosen.
