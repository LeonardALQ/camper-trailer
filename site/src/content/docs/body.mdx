The body is a box of bonded sandwich panels on the chassis, 4,420 mm long and 2,400 mm wide. A sandwich panel is two
thin glass-fibre skins bonded to a light foam core. The floor is a 30 mm panel with woven-roving skins on a PVC foam
core. The walls are 35 mm panels with gelcoat skins on XPS foam. The fixed roof is a 40 mm panel of the same kind,
bonded to the walls, and it carries the four solar panels. Aluminium extrusions join every panel edge. The body bolts
to the chassis at every station with 32 M12 bolts into welded nut plates on the raisers, and it is not bonded to the
steel. The design starts from research report 12 and is set out in `BODY.md`, revision B.

This is a planning-level proposal for the engineer to check.

### Twist sharing

The frame model takes the body as a torsion box tied to the chassis at every station. With the roof bonded to the
walls the box is closed, so it takes almost all of the twist. The chassis checks stay on the bare frame, so they hold
if the body mounts loosen.
