The trailer runs at 48 V. Research report 20 chose the parts and models every load minute by minute through four
design days (`research/models/20/energy.py`), and the tables on this page are that model's output. The parts and
their prices are in the <a href={withBase('/trailer/#parts-list')}>trailer's parts list</a>.

- Battery: five Victron Lithium NG 51.2 V 100 Ah modules, 25.6 kWh, upright in a sealed, lined box that forms the
  plinth under the washer and the dryer. The box vents outside and has a heat detector, as AS/NZS 3001.2 asks for a
  battery inside the body.
- Board: a Lynx Smart BMS NG and a Lynx Distributor, a MultiPlus-II 48/6k5 inverter-charger for the 230 V circuits,
  two Orion-Tr 48/12-30 for the 12 V bus, two Orion-Tr 48/24-16 for the 24 V bus, and a Cerbo GX MK2 that runs the
  load rules and shows them on its touch screen beside the entry door.
- Charging: four Sunman eArc 430 W panels on two SmartSolar MPPT 150/35, two Orion-Tr Smart 12/48-8 chargers in the
  ute (car build line TC01), two Honda EU22i in parallel, and shore power. Shore power and the generators each have a
  15 A inlet, and a SHORE - OFF - GENERATOR changeover switch on the board feeds the MultiPlus-II's one AC input. On GENERATOR it also bonds neutral to earth on the generator side, for the licensed electrician to confirm under AS/NZS 3001.2.
- Generators: run them on the ground at least 6 m from the trailer and downwind of it, wherever the wind comes from, with the
  exhausts pointing away, never in the drawbar box, and stop every fan and intake on the side they stand on. Ahead of the
  drawbar that is fans C and D, low on the front wall: on GENERATOR one auxiliary contact of the changeover switch opens
  their 12 V feed, so fans A and B run on as a pair with the RESPA-CF2, and a second closes the Cerbo GX's digital input
  3, which shows the mode. Behind the trailer the wet room's RA 15-60 extract stops too, and off the kerb side fans A and
  B, so the RESPA-CF2 runs alone. Never stand them off the road side, where the RESPA-CF2 and the top-up fan draw their
  air: with the wind toward the road side, stand them ahead or behind. The RESPA-CF2 keeps the cabin above outside. A CO alarm sits in the bed zone.

## Services checks

SVC-10 rates every cable where it lies: each stretch of a run takes its installation method and the design hot day's
air from where it is (inside a chassis member, through a wall's foam, under the floor, on the drawbar, in the board,
the battery box, a conduit, the ceiling's covers or the cabin), and the loaded cables beside it derate it. Its design
current and its fuse must stay within the rating of its worst stretch. SVC-11 keeps the cables clear of the Combi's hot
outlet, its exhaust and the oven, and SVC-12 holds every cable at least every 600 mm (VSB1 7.1). SVC-10 is reported,
not failing, while A-01 waits for its 10 mm² round cable, which needs an RM 30 module that the road frame G15 has no
room for. SVC-01 keeps every run clear of the chassis fittings too: the drawbar gussets, the recovery lugs, the D-rings
and each corner steady over its swing from folded to standing. The marker lamps' looms cross the raisers square into
the outriggers through round holes on their centre lines, and no run leaves a rail or a raiser through its 50 mm face
but the three cables under the kerb frame G20 (CHS-10).

## Energy budget

Each design day's energy by load group. A 230 V load counts at the inverter's output and a DC load at its own bus,
and the conversion losses are a group of their own, so each column adds up to the energy taken from the battery. The
hot day runs everything: the air-conditioning, two 20 min showers, the hob and a 45 min bake, a wash and dry, desk
work and Starlink. The cold day heats on diesel, or on the heat pump. The last row is the model's own total, and the
rows are rounded.

## Autonomy

Decision H3 asks the battery alone, with no sun, generator or shore power, to run a design hot day for 24 h when new
and for 12 h at end of life, with 90 % of the nominal capacity usable. The installed bank runs the all-electric hot
day for {n(A.hours_new, 1)} h when new and {n(A.hours_end_of_life, 1)} h at end of life, from full and at the worst

## Lamps, reflectors and the number plate

The trailer is over 6 m long and 2.1 m wide, so <a href={s.lamps.src}>ADR 13/00</a> asks for two reversing lamps,
end-outline lamps front and rear, and side marker lamps as well as the usual set. It carries {s.lamps.n_lamps} lamps
and {s.lamps.n_reflectors} reflecting devices. The ute drives them at 12 V through the 12-pin plug.

- The stop, tail and indicator lamps, the reversing lamps and the red triangles sit on the rear outriggers' faces,
  below the floor, where every height rule holds.
- The rear end-outline lamps sit under the outdoor unit's cradle. That is the highest place both sides can share.
- The front position lamps and the white reflectors sit on the front outriggers, within 150 mm of the widest point.
  That point is the overall width's: the entry door's handle on the kerb side.
- Three amber side markers with built-in reflectors go on each side, on the outrigger end caps.
- The number plate sits on the rear wall's kerb half, with its top at {n(s.lamps.plate.z1)} mm against the
  <a href={s.lamps.plate_src}>1,300 mm limit</a>. That keeps it clear of the rinse box, the rear door and the
  recovery tongues.

The marks L1 to L9 match drawing CT-011. All prices are in the trailer's parts list, line LGT-01.
