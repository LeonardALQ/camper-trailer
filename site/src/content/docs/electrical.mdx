The trailer runs at 48 V. Research report 20 chose the parts and models every load minute by minute through four
design days (`research/models/20/energy.py`). The tables on this page come from that model's output, so they follow
it when it is rerun. The parts and their prices are in the <a href={withBase('/trailer/#parts-list')}>trailer's parts list</a>.

- Battery: five Victron Lithium NG 51.2 V 100 Ah modules, 25.6 kWh, upright in a sealed, lined box that forms the
  plinth under the washer and the dryer. It vents outside and has a heat detector, as AS/NZS 3001.2 asks for a
  battery inside the body.
- Board: a Lynx Smart BMS NG and a Lynx Distributor, a MultiPlus-II 48/6k5 inverter-charger for the 230 V circuits,
  two Orion-Tr 48/12-30 for the 12 V bus, two Orion-Tr 48/24-16 for the 24 V bus, and a Cerbo GX MK2 that runs the
  load rules and shows them on its touch screen beside the entry door.
- Charging: four Sunman eArc 430 W panels on two SmartSolar MPPT 150/35, two Orion-Tr Smart 12/48-8 chargers in the
  ute (car build line TC01), two Honda EU22i in parallel, and a 15 A shore inlet.

## Energy budget

Each design day's energy by load group. A 230 V load counts at the inverter's output and a DC load at its own bus,
and the conversion losses are a group of their own, so each column adds up to the energy taken from the battery.
The hot day runs everything at once: the air-conditioning, two 20 min showers, the hob and a 45 min bake, a wash and
dry, desk work and Starlink. The cold day heats on diesel as designed, or on the heat pump. The last row is the
model's own total, and the rows are rounded.

## Autonomy

Decision H3 asks the battery alone, with no sun, generator or shore power, to run a design hot day for 24 h when new
and for 12 h at end of life, with 90 % of the nominal capacity usable. The installed bank runs the all-electric hot
day for {n(A.hours_new, 1)} h when new and {n(A.hours_end_of_life, 1)} h at end of life, from full and at the worst
starting hour.

## Circuits and load shedding

Every 230 V circuit has its own 30 mA RCBO, and the board is labelled as inverter-supplied. The shower heater sheds
first, so cooking keeps priority, and the split never sheds.
