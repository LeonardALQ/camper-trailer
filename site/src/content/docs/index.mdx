This site holds the design of a one-off off-road camper trailer and the vehicle that tows it. The owner is designing
both and will build them himself in Australia. The project has three parts:

- **Car build**: a used Toyota LandCruiser 79 single cab V8, rebuilt in stages into a reliable tow vehicle.
- **Bike box**: an enclosed, lockable box on the ute's tray for the two motorcycles and the tools.
- **Trailer**: the home trailer, a 4,420 mm body with a lift bed over the desk across the front, a wet room at the
  rear, a fixed roof, and the generators and fuel in a box on the drawbar. It is built to VSB1, the Australian guide for light trailers, then inspected for registration and
  reviewed by an engineer. Most of its life is highway towing, plus grass, desert tracks, fire trails, light mud and
  shallow river crossings.

Each part has its plan, its parts list, its 3D model and drawings, and the measurements to take, in the menu under
the part's name. Everything comes from one set of data files: the models, the checks, the mass schedules, the
drawings, the parts lists and the costs are generated from them, so they always agree. When a number changes in the
data, the whole site is rebuilt.

## The constraints

- The trailer body is 2,400 mm wide at most. That is a hard limit. With 35 mm walls it leaves 2,330 mm inside.
- The trailer is plated at 3,500 kg ATM (decision H2), so the combination stays inside the 7,400 kg GCM of Lovells' revision with the ute at its 3,850 kg limit. Toyota's 6,900 kg applies if the revision is refused.
- The towball carries at least 8.0 % of the trailer's mass in every case and at most 320 kg with growth (decision H17), inside the towbar's 350 kg. The ute's Lovells Elite kit keeps its rear axle within 2,850 kg at that ball.
- The ute's ball sits 440 mm above the ground laden, plus or minus 20 mm, so the coupling stays inside VSB1's 460 mm.
- The bike box is 1,850 mm wide outside at most and weighs less than 440 kg empty.

## How products are chosen

Each product is chosen in this order. First comes reliability and a record of hard use, for example in race
trailers, toy haulers, boats or overland caravans. Next comes parts and service, in Australia if possible. Then
comes serviceability, so a part can be repaired or swapped, ideally in the field. Only then does weight decide,
and the lightest product that passes the first three wins. Price is ignored for the trailer. The car build has a
budget.
