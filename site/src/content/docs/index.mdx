This site holds the design of a one-off off-road camper trailer. The owner is designing it and will build it
himself in Australia. It will be built to VSB1, the Australian guide for light trailers, then inspected for
registration and reviewed by an engineer.

The trailer carries two motorcycles in a garage, with a queen bed above them. It is towed by a Porsche Cayenne
GTS. Most of its life is highway towing, plus grass, desert tracks, fire trails, light mud and shallow river
crossings.

Everything on this site comes from one set of data files. The 3D model, the clearance checks, the mass schedule,
the drawings and the parts lists are generated from them, so they always agree. When a number changes in the
data, the whole site is rebuilt.

## The constraints

- The body is 2,400 mm wide at most. That is a hard limit. With 35 mm walls it leaves 2,330 mm inside.
- The body is 6,000 mm long at most. The target is about 5,000 mm to save weight, so every part of the layout should be as short as it can be.
- With the roof collapsed, the inside is 1,800 mm high measured from the garage floor. That is the target, and 2,000 mm is the hard limit. A raising roof adds headroom at camp.
- The Cayenne can tow 3,500 kg with trailer brakes, and its towball rating is 280 kg. The design aims for about 220 kg fully loaded, where the car's rear axle limit starts to bind (research report 07).
- The garage floor is flat, with no wheel arches inside the garage.

## How products are chosen

Each product is chosen in this order. First comes reliability and a record of hard use, for example in race
trailers, toy haulers, boats or overland caravans. Next comes parts and service, in Australia if possible. Then
comes serviceability, so a part can be repaired or swapped, ideally in the field. Only then does weight decide,
and the lightest product that passes the first three wins. Price is ignored.

## What is done

- Five research reports: the tech stack for this site, design and drawing practice, bike restraint, the bed and its materials, and the bike geometry. They are under Research in the menu.
- The design baseline for the garage and bed module, with its bill of materials.
- This first version of the site: a parametric 3D model of the module, the design checks measured on that model, a mass schedule, three draft drawings and eleven draft build steps.
- Revision B of the design, from the first model run: the deck laps over partition B, an owner-built ladder reaches the living-area floor, and the note on converting to the trailer datum is corrected. Drawings CT-401 and CT-501 are at revision B.
- Three more research reports: the trailer's running gear, the tow vehicle, and the coupling and hitch.
- The <a href={withBase('/trailer/')}>whole-trailer block layout</a>: every zone and system as a block with a mass, the axle position solved for the towball target, seven load cases, the whole-trailer checks, a comparison of 5,000, 5,500 and 6,000 mm bodies, the chassis inputs and the general arrangement CT-001. It fixes the garage floor at 858 mm, which is revision C of the garage and bed module, together with the Tuareg's panniers.

## What is next

- Build a full-size mock-up of the garage and measure both bikes in it. Several bike dimensions on this site are estimates, and the chock's nose and cradle rise are not published. Build step 1 lists what to measure.
- Decide how the bikes are loaded: a winch with a fixed bed, or a powered bed lift used with the roof raised.
- Decide whether anyone will sleep in the trailer with fuelled bikes inside, because that changes the garage ventilation.
- Bring the trailer's mass under its 3,500 kg ATM with the growth allowance. Fully loaded it is at the ATM before any allowance (the Whole trailer page has the levers).
- Design the chassis from the inputs on the Whole trailer page, with the engineer.
- Design the body, the garage door and the raising roof.
- Ask the engineer to review the drawings, the restraint design and the bed structure.
