# Home trailer

Status: concept, revision J of 21 Nov 2026, updated 22 Nov 2026 for Leonard building the trailer himself (H26), the parts that stood on nothing (H27), the floor plans and the 3D services plan (H28), and the weld evidence the engineer's calculation needs (CMP-14), and 23 Nov 2026 for the 4,000 kg plate behind the Ranger Super Duty (H2 as amended): the DO45 coupling, grade T chains, the load-sharing XT Air, 4 mm rails and the fresh tanks' whole 400 L net in travel, and 25 Nov 2026 for both hinge cheeks at every XT hinge and the fresh tanks' corners notched round the chassis' hinge gussets and jacking pads, which takes the water to 395 L (H21). DRAFT, not for construction until the engineer reviews it. Data: `data/assemblies/trailer.yaml`, `data/load_cases.yaml`, `data/checks.yaml`, `data/services.yaml`, `data/measurements.yaml` and the parts list `data/trailer-parts.yaml`. Code: `cad/trailer.py`. CHASSIS.md and BODY.md design the frame and the shell, `docs/decisions.md` records the decisions, and research reports 19 to 22 and 25 hold the research. Positions are in mm in the project datum DIM-01: X rearward from the ball, Y positive to the road side, Z up from the ground.

The brief is a home for long stays off-grid: a lifted bed over a 1.2 m desk, a wet bathroom entered from a rear door, a recirculating shower for two 20 min showers a day, an electric kitchen with an oven, a washer and a dryer, filtered fresh air with the windows shut, blackout blinds, and power for a hot day of air-conditioning for at least 12 h, 24 h if possible.

- Plate: ATM 4,000 kg (H2, amended 23 Nov 2026 from 3,500 kg), inside the Super Duty's 4,500 kg braked towing and the 4,500 kg of the DO45 and the brakes. The heaviest case with growth is 3,638.4 kg, 261.6 kg inside H2's 3,900 kg target (MAS-10), so H21 takes nothing off the water. The examiner caps GTM at 4,500 kg, from the coupling and the brakes, and ATM at 4,500 kg, from the coupling (MAS-07, MAS-08). Over 3.5 t the trailer is ADR category TC, and VSB1 Rev 6 still covers it, as it does every trailer to 4.5 t ATM. NSW registers it as a light vehicle, a trailer of 4.5 t ATM or less, with a yearly safety check once it is five years old ([TfNSW](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/vehicle-registration/renewing-vehicle-registration/safety-checks-and-vehicle-inspections)), and the concessional RAV entry still takes it as a low-ATM trailer.
- Water: all 395 L of fresh and grey water may ride while towing (H13 as amended), so fresh and grey together never pass what the fresh tanks hold, and H21 still takes any excess over the target off the water in 5 L steps. The tanks are filled at camp. The grey tank travels empty, and TC7 and TC8 check the trailer with it full.
- Towball: at least 7.5 % in every case (MAS-04, H2) and at most the DO45's 450 kg and the Super Duty's 450 kg (est., no Ford document gives it). While the masses are estimates the axle sits in the middle of a 92.9 mm window between 9.0 % in the lightest case and 420 kg with growth in the heaviest (MAS-12, MAS-13, H17 as amended). Ford's towing guide aims at about 10 % and Smart Hitch recommends 10 to 15 %: TC2 has 10.9 % and the lowest case, TC7 with a full grey tank, 9.7 %. The 9.0 % floor stays now that the tanks hold 396.1 L net, not 420 L, because a 10 % floor would move the axle back, and the rear fresh tank, which ends at the wet room's front, would shorten with it and take the tanks under the 395 L the design carries, for 0.3 of a point on TC7 alone.
- Ute: the Ford Ranger Super Duty single cab chassis on Ford's ratings (`data/ute/ute.yaml`, UTE-GARAGE.md). The heaviest towball, 394.7 kg with growth, puts its rear axle at 2,546 kg with growth against Ford's 2,800 kg (MAS-03, H14), and stays 55.3 kg under the Super Duty's 450 kg (est., Ford's figure is in the owner guide) and the DO45's 450 kg. MAS-14, the LandCruiser's return to the Platinum kit, is retired with the kit.
- GCM: the ute with growth, 3,748 kg, plus the 4,000 kg plate is 7,748 kg against Ford's 8,000 kg (GCM-01, UTE-06, H20). Ford's GCM needs no revision, so there is no fallback plate or water limit. GCM-01 reads the plate from `data/load_cases.yaml`.
- Balance: the centre of gravity sits at most 37.2 mm to the kerb side against a 50 mm rule (MAS-11), at Z 1,203 to 1,297 mm with growth. The static stability factor is at least 0.62 on the 1,655 mm track.
- Roll: with the body rolled onto its bump stops and tyres, the static rollover threshold is about 0.42 g (est., Gillespie's method), over the 0.35 g of PBS level 1 and the 0.40 g target (STB-01, STB-02). Decision H22 widened the track from 1,555 to 1,655 mm for it, through the rails. The ute with the bikes aboard has a static stability factor of 0.95 (UTE-11).
- Sway: the yaw inertia is about 7,728 kg m² fully loaded with growth, and the towball never falls under 9.7 % of the trailer (STB-03). No snaking speed is estimated, because the published models need tyre and hitch data that are not known. The Bosch TSC brakes the trailer when it senses sway.
- Axles: the XT Air is ordered in Cruisemaster's load-sharing tandem configuration, each side's two bags on one circuit (CIS-006), so VSB1's 120 % rule for close-coupled axles that do not share load does not apply. Without it the rule would need 4,440 kg at the plate against the 4,500 kg build's 4,050 kg. The axle group carries 3,283 kg with growth and 3,700 kg at the plate with the 7.5 % ball (MAS-02). Each wheel and tyre takes 1,005 kg on the heavier side with the 120 % rule kept as margin, against 1,400 kg and 1,550 kg (MAS-09, MAS-06).
- Parked in wind: unhitched on its corner steadies, the trailer slides on wet grass in a side gust of about 27 m/s at tare and 30 m/s fully loaded, forecast gusts of about 108 and 119 km/h, before it tips at about 31 and 34 m/s, both with the same force coefficient (STB-04, the friction est.). When a forecast gives gusts of 85 km/h or more, close the awning, and hitch the trailer to the ute, strap it down or move it. 85 km/h is the lower speed, sliding at tare, over the square root of 1.5, rounded down to 5 km/h, so the wind's force keeps a factor of 1.62 against sliding and 2.07 against tipping. A Bureau of Meteorology warning of damaging winds, 90 km/h and more, always meets it ([BoM](https://www.bom.gov.au/resources/learn-and-explore/severe-weather-knowledge-centre/severe-weather-and-coastal-hazard-warning-services)). Four rated lashing points under the outrigger ends take ratchet straps down to four Tie Down Engineering 59099 ground anchors (model M607, 3/4 in rod, 60 in, one 7 in helix), each with a Tie Down 59286 stabilizer plate on the trailer side of its rod and installed as RADCO's listing #1349 tested them, in the steps below. The listing rates the anchor at 3,922 lb (17.4 kN) working and 5,883 lb (26.2 kN) ultimate at a 45 degree pull in HUD's class 4B, "Loose sands", "firm clays" and "alluvial fills", and in the firmer classes ([Tie Down's manual with the RADCO listing](https://irp.cdn-website.com/871418b2/files/uploaded/Tie_Down_Anchor_Installation_Manual.pdf), [24 CFR 3285.202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/3285.202)). Strapped down it holds in region B's 500-year gust, 51.9 m/s at 3 m, with 14.3 kN in each windward strap at 55 degrees, 0.82 of the anchor's working load (STB-05). Where the soil reads less than 225 in-lb on the probe (step 1 below), as in class 5, "Uncompacted fill", "peat" and "organic clays", or an anchor will not wind in to its full depth, hitch the trailer or move it. The kit, 45.0 kg, rides in the ute while towing and stays with the trailer whenever the ute is unhitched for more than a day, so it is there with the ute away. No stays in the cyclonic regions C and D in the cyclone season, 1 November to 30 April ([BoM](https://www.bom.gov.au/resources/learn-and-explore/tropical-cyclone-knowledge-centre)). The walls are designed for region B's 25-year wind, which takes in the Queensland coast (H23).
- Setting each anchor, from Tie Down's manual, pp. 4, 6 and 9:
  1. Mark the anchor's place 502 to 646 mm out from the point under its lashing point on level ground, so that the strap runs at 48 to 55 degrees to the ground. 48 degrees stays 3 degrees clear of the listing's 45 degree minimum, so ground slope and the trailer's ride height cannot take the pull under it. Probe the soil there: Tie Down's 59200 soil test probe, driven to the helix depth, about 1.50 m, must read at least 225 in-lb, the Missouri Public Service Commission's figure for the M607 ([Missouri PSC](https://psc.mo.gov/CMSInternetData/ManufacturedHousing/Anchoring%20Companies%20and%20Systems/Tie%20Down%20Engineering.pdf)).
  2. Wind the anchor into undisturbed soil at a 10 degree back angle, its helix further from the trailer than its head, to about 2/3 of its depth, with 355 to 405 mm (14 to 16 in) of it left above the ground.
  3. Drive the plate vertically on the trailer side of the rod, 75 to 100 mm (3 to 4 in) from it.
  4. Wind the anchor to its full depth, the head at the ground.
  5. Tension the strap until the rod bears on the plate, and check the strap's angle along it with an angle finder: 48 to 55 degrees.

  Never dig a hole and backfill it. Tie Down's manual installation digs a hole to 2/3 of the anchor's length and backfills it, and Tie Down warns that loose soil may then need a test of the anchor's holding power (p. 6), which the kit cannot do.
- Awning: close the awning in wind, legs pegged. Peg the legs whenever it is out, and close it before gusts reach 40 km/h, Beaufort force 5, and whenever the trailer is left. Fiamma gives no speed. Its roof bolts are checked with the canopy open in a 60 km/h gust (BDY-11), a check of the fixing only: the 40 km/h rule is what protects the canopy, its arms and its legs.

## Structure

The chassis is a galvanised ladder frame (CHASSIS.md revision S, CT-101 to CT-106). Two RHS 150 x 50 x 4 C450L0 rails, 4 mm since the 4,000 kg plate, each carry a continuous RHS 127 x 51 x 3.5 C350L0 raiser, so the floor clears the 805 mm tyres. Eight stations carry crossmembers and outriggers, the XT front hinge, rear shock and rear crossmembers RHS 150 x 50 x 6 and the XT centre 150 x 50 x 5 for the recovery pull's 78.5 kN ultimate, each XT hinge held to its rail by two 10 mm cheeks, and the drawbar is RHS 150 x 50 x 6. The rails are 1,275 mm over their outsides for the 1,655 mm track (H22). Steel and zinc weigh 591 kg. Checked at the 4,000 kg plate, the recovery pull governs at 0.81 of yield, held by the ground at the tyres or by one wheel held fast, VSB1's drawbar tests reach 0.78 and the travel cases 0.54. At the 78.5 kN ultimate every member and hole holds, the worst at 0.87 (CHS-08U). The rear tank's steel guard carries the rear bay's racking as a shear panel (CHS-13), and Cruisemaster accepts the hinge joints in writing (CHASSIS.md items 14 and 15). The frame twists 0.33 degree against 0.5, and every fatigue detail stays below its endurance limit. Heavy items bolt through the floor into the raisers and outriggers (CT-104).

## Body and roof

The shell is bonded sandwich panels in aluminium joiners (BODY.md revision C, CT-201 to CT-203): a 30 mm GRP and PVC foam floor, 35 mm GRP and XPS walls 2,000 mm tall, and a fixed 40 mm roof that carries the solar panels. It bolts to the frame at 32 points and takes 97 % of the twist. Seven aluminium members on the inner skin, RHS 110 x 19 x 3.5 made to order with 13 mm of XPS behind them, hold each side wall to 19.7 mm in region B's 25-year wind (AS/NZS 1170.2), against 20 mm (BDY-10, H23), and stand at least 25 mm clear of every opening, gland, insert strip and each other, BD-20 and the bed's rear kerb-side hardpoint sharing one routed channel with an H200 fill (FIT-07). W1's and W2's sides take bonded inserts that fill the core to the bed's hardpoints, so the windows clamp a solid edge. As plates with every opening cut out, the walls deflect at most 18.9 mm, at the entry door's front edge, where a second jamb member (BD-20) stands behind the bed's rear hardpoint (BDY-24), and carry the ultimate wind at 0.72 (BDY-25). No metal crosses the core. Each member stands on a rigid PVC bearing block clear of the floor joiner's inner leg and stops under the wall's top insert strip, and foam covers the joiners, so on the design cold night, at ISO 13788's surface resistance of 0.25 m²K/W, the inside surface over the members and the joiners stays at 10.2 °C or more, against report 19's rule of the dew point plus 4 K (BDY-22).

The roof is fixed, with rounded front edges, 150 mm at the roof edge and 250 mm on the front verticals (H12). Towing at 90 km/h it uses 21.3 L/100 km, against 25.0 with a square front and 20.6 with a hard raising roof, which would add 117 kg and about AUD 32,900 and rule out the S4 windows, the stock doors, the lift bed and a full-height wet room (research report 25).

Four Dometic S4 windows (W1 and W2 at the bed, W4 in the kitchen, W6 frosted in the wet room) and the glass in both doors have integrated blackout blinds (H8, H15, CT-011, CT-013). W1's 804 x 454 mm opening is the second way out. All 18 openings, G2's box of inlets among them, keep 300 mm of wall between them (BDY-18).

The awning is a Fiamma F80s 400 on the roof's kerb edge, its case 5 mm inside the wall's skin. Its five brackets bolt through the roof's insert strip with Panelok APLB-1024 bolts, whose grip takes the 40 mm roof (BDY-11). Its legs stand on the ground: set for a 10 degree fall they are about 2,531 mm long and keep about 155 mm of reach for ground that falls away (BDY-23, est.). ADR 43/04 and VSB1 5.2 allow 2,500 mm and leave out only the lamps and reflectors, so every frame, hood, cowl, handle and box on a side wall counts. The S4 frames stand 21 mm proud, the hoods, cowls and flush-mounted inlet boxes at most 30 mm and the entry door's handle 40 mm (est.), so the trailer is 2,470 mm wide against a 2,490 mm design limit (DIM-01, DIM-05, TR-11).

A Lippert HappiJac four-rail lift carries a 1,550 x 2,290 mm honeycomb platform and a 180 mm queen mattress across the trailer (H1). By day and in travel the platform's underside is 1,600 mm up, so a seated adult clears it. At night it stops at 805 mm, with the mattress top at 1,044 mm, 956 mm clear above it and 75 mm over the desk (BED-01). Index plungers lock it, a safety edge stops it on an obstruction, and a socket at the rail tops lowers it by hand. The lift is rated 272 kg standing and 204 kg moving (Lippert), so at night the bed takes two adults of up to about 208 kg together (BED-02). The platform lies on the kit's two support rails, one along each wall from the front carriage to the rear one. The desk is 1,200 x 600 mm at 730 mm, its back edge on an aluminium cleat on the front wall and its front corners on two 40 mm square legs, and nothing else stands under the bed.

## HVAC and fresh air

Research report 19's load calculation gives a design hot day of 37.1 kWh of heat to remove, peaking at 3.29 kW, and a design cold night of 17.6 kWh over 14 h (CT-621).

- Cooling and heating: a Mitsubishi Electric 2.5 kW inverter split (H7). The indoor unit sits high on the partition, the outdoor unit high on the rear wall on wire-rope isolators, its cradle on two stays to the chassis (BDY-21). It uses 9.0 kWh of battery on the hot day, 5.1 kWh less than the best rooftop unit.
- Backup: the Truma Combi D 6 E heats air and water on diesel or on its elements.
- Fresh air: two LUNOS e2 60 pairs exchange 60 m³/h by day and 45 m³/h at night through 83 % heat recovery, holding CO₂ near 900 ppm for two. A filtered top-up fan adds 15 m³/h, so the cabin sits about 3 Pa above outside, and a relief valve lets the excess out through the wet room. A wet room extract and the ducted hood remove moisture at the source.
- Dust: while towing, a Sy-Klone RESPA-CF2 with a powered precleaner and MERV 16 and carbon filters holds about 20 Pa, and the other fans stop. No window needs to open.

## Electrical

48 V throughout (research report 20, CT-601, CT-602).

- Battery: five Victron Lithium NG 51.2 V 100 Ah modules, 25.6 kWh and 185 kg, in a sealed, vented and lined aluminium box that forms the plinth, with a heat detector (AS/NZS 3001.2).
- Conversion: a MultiPlus-II 48/6k5 inverter-charger feeds ten 230 V circuits on 30 mA RCBOs. Orion-Tr converters feed the 12 V and 24 V buses, and a Cerbo GX runs the load rules.
- Charging: four Sunman 430 W panels give about 9.3 kWh on the design hot day, the ute's chargers 3.6 kWh on a 5 h drive, and the two EU22i put a hot day back in 8.4 h while the household runs. Shore power and the generators have a 15 A inlet each, side by side, and a SHORE - OFF - GENERATOR changeover switch on the board feeds the MultiPlus-II's one AC input from one of them.
- Generators: they run on the ground at least 6 m from the trailer and downwind of it, wherever the wind comes from, exhausts pointing away, never in the drawbar box, and every fan and intake on their side stops. Ahead of the drawbar that is fans C and D of the e2 pairs, low on the front wall, so on GENERATOR one auxiliary contact of the changeover switch opens their 12 V feed and a second tells the Cerbo GX, on its digital input 3, which shows the mode. Fans A and B on the side walls run on as a pair, and the RESPA-CF2 holds the cabin above outside through its MERV 16 and carbon filters, and the cold night's dew point stays at -1.7 °C. Behind the trailer the wet room's RA 15-60 extract, high in the rear wall, stops as well. Off the kerb side fans A and B stop and the RESPA-CF2 runs alone, and the dew point is -1.2 °C (BDY-22). They never stand off the road side, where the RESPA-CF2 and the top-up fan draw their air: with the wind toward the road side they stand ahead of the drawbar or behind the trailer, where it carries their exhaust past the trailer's end. The GENERATOR position also bonds neutral to earth on the generator side, because an inverter generator's neutral commonly floats (Honda to confirm), so the RCBOs keep an earth return. The licensed electrician confirms it under AS/NZS 3001.2 (ELI-01). The switch and the second inlet cost about AUD 250 and weigh 0.5 kg (est.).
- Autonomy (H3): the design hot day, all electric with the air-conditioning, two showers, cooking, a wash and dry, desk work and Starlink, takes 19.6 kWh. With 90 % usable, the battery alone runs it for 25.1 h when new and 17.2 h at end of life. The cold night takes 4.7 kWh on diesel heat or 11.2 kWh on the heat pump.
- Shedding: the shower heater drops out first when the hob or the oven runs, and the shower stays warm on boiler water. The Combi element, washer and dryer follow. The split never sheds. The worst managed load is 89 % of the inverter's 40 °C rating.
- Fire: a 2 kg ABE extinguisher stands by each outside door, D1 and D2, with a fire blanket at D1, a smoke alarm and a CO alarm (VSB1 13.2.2). They cover the wet room whether or not it counts as a second compartment. A dry powder unit will not stop a lithium module in thermal runaway, so if the battery box smokes or burns, open the 300 A isolator, get everyone out and call 000. The extinguishers are for a fire that has not reached the box (LA-16).
- Cables: each is rated where it lies, for its installation method, the design hot day's air there (45 °C under the floor and in the chassis members, 50 °C in the walls' foam and on the drawbar, est.) and the loaded cables beside it, and its design current and its fuse stay within that rating (SVC-10, AS/NZS 3000 clause 2.5.3, AS/NZS 3008.1.1). The twin cables take Tycab's rating for each size by its code, and the 48 V double-insulated pairs AS/NZS 3008.1.1's by Prysmian's table, since Tycab rates no double-insulated cable. A cable with a design current and no fuse fails unless nothing can overload it. The bank's cable to the BMS, D-06, and the inverter's pair D-07 are 95 mm² under a 200 A MEGA each, D-06's in the battery fuse holder's sixth way, because the five 60 A module fuses add to 300 A (I2-31). The 12 V converters' pair D-13 is 25 mm² and three 230 V circuits 4 mm². The 230 V runs are round 2C+E TPS, so every gland and frame module seals on them. A-01, the inverter's output to the board, runs alone: clipped down the partition beside the dryer and through a module of its own in each frame, so it is grouped with no other circuit. Its 25 A breaker, the board's main switch in series with AC-out-1 (Victron's installation manual), is over the board's managed peak, 23.8 A at 5,465 W on the all-electric cold day (report 20), and its 4 mm² is rated 28.1 A under the floor at 45 °C. In the bunched conduit it would have needed 10 mm², 16.8 mm across, past an RM 20's 14.5 mm, with no room in G15 for an RM 30. Every cable is held at least every 600 mm, inside a chassis member, on clips or on one of nine added cleats, trays and angles, inside a cabinet only by a panel or a cleat it is clipped to, and under the floor the 230 V only inside its corrugated conduit, saddled every 500 mm (SVC-12, VSB1 7.1, I2-36), and keeps 25 mm, 50 mm for 230 V, from the Combi's hot outlet before the mixing valve and 50 mm (est.) from its exhaust and the oven's case (SVC-11, Truma's installation instructions). The marker lamps' looms run inside the raisers and turn square across them into the outriggers OR-05 through a round hole on each outrigger's centre line, and the lamp loom and the brake circuit leave the road rail through its outer web, and the breakaway charger's, the compressor's and the ute charging cables come down from G20 beside the kerb raiser and into it through its inner web, so no run makes a hole in a rail's or a raiser's 50 mm face (CHS-10, CHASSIS.md).
